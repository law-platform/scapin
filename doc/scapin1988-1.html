GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 091.31 (1 Apr 49) ESS/PF

(SCAPIN - 1988/1)

APO 500

8 May 1950

MEMORANDUM FOR :Japanese Government
SUBJECT :Dried Skim Milk for School Lunch Program

1. Reference SCAPIN 1988, dated 1 April 1949, subject: Yen Counterpart of GARIOA and EROA Imports.

2. In addition to purposes provided in the SCAPIN referenced above and Japanese Government laws, ordinances and regulations enacted in relation thereto, in order to effect offset against the use of foreign exchange, held in the accounts of the FECM of the Japanese Government, for the purchase of dried skim milk for the school lunch program, the Japanese Government will cause sufficient Counterpart Funds to be made available to buy the necessary foreign exchange to cover both purchase and shipment so that the milk will be landed in Japan free to the school lunch program.

3. Procedure for accomplishing action required above necessitates (1) purchase of Foreign Exchange from the Foreign Exchange Special Account by the Foreign Trade Special Account of the Ministry of International Trade and Industry at the time a letter of credit is issued covering a specific contract for the shipment of dried skimmed milk to be used for the school lunch program; (2) billing of the Counterpart Fund by the Foreign Trade S/A upon release of import at time of arrival to agency designated by the Ministry of Education for all yen expenditures made in purchasing foreign exchange for the dried skim milk imports; (3) payment of yen amount billed from the Counterpart Fund to the Foreign Trade Special Account.

4. Total funds to be made available from the Counterpart Fund will not exceed one billion three hundred fifty million yen (¥1,350,000,000) and will be applicable to the Japanese Fiscal Year 1950-51 only.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

/s/ K. B. Bush
/t/ K. B. BUSH
Brigadier General, USA,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1988/1)

EMR/ATM/eb

5 May 1950

1. References:

a. Radio W 80626, dated 4 December 1948 from DA to SCAP.

b. Radio W 81008, dated 11 December 1948 from DA to SCAP.

c. SCAPIN 1988, dated 1 April 1949, subject: Yen Counterpart of GARIOA and EROA Imports.

d. Memorandum to the Ministry of Finance for the Chief, ESS, 123 (7 Jan 50) ESS/PF, dated 7 January 1950, subject: Dried Skimmed Milk Imports for School Lunch and Nursery Feeding Program.

e. Radio W 09962, dated 26 January 1950 from DA to SCAP.

f. Radio C 55015, dated 19 February 1950 from SCAP to DA, subject: Dried Skim Milk Procurement.

g. Radio W 81370, dated 1 April 1950 from DA to SCAP.

2. The attached SCAPIN makes possible the use of Counterpart Funds to pay for foreign exchange, held in the accounts of the FECB of the JG, used in purchase and transporting to Japan dried skim milk to be used in support of' school lunch program for Japanese school children. Detailed procedure in accomplishing the above is outlined to the JG in order to insure that proper action is taken and that there is no misunderstanding of the proposed SCAPIN which deals with Counterpart Fund policy. The SCAPIN limits the amount to be used to ¥1,350, 000, 000 and makes it applicable to the Japanese Fiscal Year 1950-51 only. During JFY 1949-50, as explained in para 3 below, milk for the school lunch program was purchased with GARIOA Funds and necessary adjustments accomplished to make it free to the Japanese school children.

3. Reference 1a stated that the donation of food for the Japanese Child Feeding Program without charge against the Japanese economy had been approved, but reference 1b indicated that the donations should be accomplished slowly and without publicity. At the time SCAPIN referenced 1c was issued PH&W had not completed negotiations with the JG regarding a sound program for receiving and delivering the milk to the ultimate school children consumers, and consequently no provision was made for deviating from the policy and excepting transfer of the yen equivalent of the dollar cost of milk donated to the Counterpart Fund. Reference 1d removed dried skim milk purchased with GARIOA funds and distributed for use in the school lunch program from the category of "American Aid" and designated such milk as "gift" or "donation" Such action caused the milk to be considered outside SCAPIN 1988 and gave the JG authority to adjust records.

4. Radio reference 1e notified SCAP that due to legal regulations the U.S. Department of Agriculture could sell dried milk to the Japanese Government for 4 cents or less per pound whereas it would cost a U.S. agency, purchasing with GARIOA funds, up to 12 cents per pound. DA suggested explaining the above facts to the JG and proposing to them that GARIOA Funds earmarked for dried skim milk purchase be diverted for the purchase of other foods for Japan and that foreign exchange held in the account of the JG be used to purchase the milk. DA further stated that in order to make the milk free to the school children and to preclude Japan being charged with the GARIOA dollar equivalent of the milk, SCAP should consider making Counterpart Funds available to pay for commercial exchange necessary.

5. Radio referenced If notified DA that there was no objection to using foreign exchange held in the accounts of the Foreign Exchange Control Board of the Japanese Government for purchase of milk for the school lunch program and that Counterpart Funds would be made available to buy the necessary foreign exchange to cover both purchase and shipping costs. DA was also informed that an annual supply of milk would total 30,000 tons and would cost ¥1,350,000,000.

6. Radio referenced 1g gave DA approval for including ¥1,350,000,000 in the Counterpart Fund expenditure program for the JFY 1950-51.

7. Concurrences: PH&W Gen. Sams (by telephone) 4 May 50

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 062.2 (29 Nov 46) CPC/FP

(SCAPIN-1367)

APO 500

29 November 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Illegally-Possessed Foreign Films in Japan.

1. Reference is made to memorandum for the Imperial Japanese Government, file AG 062.2 (8 Jul 46) CPC/FP, SCAPIN-1056, 8 July 1946, subject “Census of Foreign Films in Japan,” from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

2. An analysis has been made of films listed as having been impounded by the Home Ministry as a result of the referenced memorandum. It indicates that among them are some which probably belong rightfully to Japanese nationals who for various reasons are unable to show legal documents proving ownership or to obtain such documents under present circumstances.

3. The Home Ministry shall determine which films fall into this category and re-examine them. Those which have classroom value and can be withdrawn from use only to the detriment of education in scientific and technical fields shall be returned to those who claim to own them or the understanding that such return does not constitute recognition of legal ownership and that surrender of the films and compensation for their use may be demanded if proof of ownership cannot be obtained when the reopening of normal communication channels makes possible the obtaining of such proof. To determine whether a film has value in scientific or technical education, the Home Ministry shall consult the Education Ministry. Scenic films, newsreels and animated cartoons, unless purely scientific or technical, shall not be regarded as educational in the above sense, and in no case shall a 35-millimeter film produced by a foreign concern engaged primarily in the production of commercial entertainment films be returned.

4. A list of illegally-possessed films by country of origin will be compiled giving title of picture in language of origin, name of producer, prefecture where located, and name of possessor or person who made report on it. Sample form to be used is inclosed as Annex A. Four copies will be submitted to General Headquarters. Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers within one month of the date of this memorandum.

5. The analysis of films listed in compliance with memorandum referred to in paragraph 1 above also reveals that:

a. In Kanagawa Prefecture a Japanese national holds “Lady Windermere’s Fan” and “The Second Degree,” two Warner Brothers 35-millimeter prints;

b. In Yamaguchi-ken a Japanese national holds “Great Success of Coogan,” a 35-millimeter Universal print.

The films are deemed as being illegally possessed and should be taken into custody.

6. All impounded film not released by the above-ordered review shall be shipped to Central Motion Picture Exchange, Civil Information & Education Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, Kanesaka Building, Tokyo.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JHON B. COOLEY
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

(To addressee only)

MEMORANDUM FOR :Japanese Government
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo
SUBJECT :Carrying of Japanese Currency by Japanese Merchant Ships

1. Reference is Central Liaison Memorandum No. 3023 (LO 1068-FI/FF), dated 23 April 1947, subject: Export and Import of Japanese Currency Carried by Ships and Their Crews. This memorandum forwards a proposed plan whereby Japanese currency belonging to Japanese merchant ships and crew members could be carried abroad, provided it was sealed by customs officials at port of departure from Japan. Upon return from foreign ports the seal would be removed by customs officials and the currency would thus be immediately available for payment of crew's salaries, purchase of new supplies of fresh food and other expenses that need to be met without delay.

2. Proposed plan forwarded by reference memorandum is approved.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

/s/ R. M. Levy
/t/ R. M. LEVY,
Colonel, A. G. D.,
Adjutant General

MEMO FOR RECORD : (SCAPIN - 1708)

22 May 1947

1. C. L. O. M. No. 3023 (LO 1068-FI/FF), dated 23 April 1947, subject: Export and Import of Japanese Currency Carried by Ships and their Crews, forwards a letter from The Ministry of Finance proposing a plan whereby Japanese currency belonging to Japanese merchant ships and crew members could be carried abroad on board ship provided it was first sealed by customs officials at port of departure from Japan and" written permit issued by local customs house. Upon return to Japan from foreign ports the permit would be presented to customs officials at port of entry and the seal removed from the currency. Thus cash would be immediately available to pay off crew members and meet other necessary expenses that must be settled without delay. In cases of emergency requiring removal of the seal in a port in Japan in which there is neither a customs office nor a customs officer, the nearest police office would be contacted and approval obtained through it from the nearest customs house.

2. Memorandum for the IJG from GHQ, SCAP, AG 091. 3 (22 Sep 45) ESS (SCAPIN-44), dated 22 September 1945, subject: Controls over Exports and Imports of Gold, Silver, Securities, and Financial Instruments, prohibits the exportation or withdrawal from Japan to any foreign country, or the importation into Japan from any foreign country, of gold or silver coin, currency, securities, checks,, drafts, bills of exchange, promissory notes, payment instructions, transfer orders, or other financial instruments, or authorization or instructions to effect financial or property transactions within or outside Japan.

3. Memorandum for the Imperial Japanese Government from GHQ, SCAP, AG 091. 31 (19 Feb 46) ESS/FI, (SCAPIN-1533), dated 19 February 1947, subject: Export and Import of Japanese Currency by Crews of Japanese Ships, prohibits the carrying by Japanese merchant ships of special funds for use in foreign ports to meet docking charges, because such charges are to be settled on an inter-governmental level.

4. An investigation into this matter brought out the following facts: It is highly desirable that Japanese merchant ships plying between Japan and foreign countries carry a reasonable amount of cash aboard to meet immediate needs upon return to the first Japanese port, such as paying the crews all money due them, purchasing new supplies of fresh food and meeting any emergency expenses that may arise at that time. Although the Civilian Merchant Marine Committee has regular branch offices in most major Japanese ports, agents do not always have on hand cash to accommodate a vessel at a moment's notice and obtaining cash from the bank takes several days because of the procedures required by the Emergency Financial Measures. The banks are also closed on Sundays and holidays. The proposed plan whereby all ships' and crews' funds would be sealed during voyage and opened only by customs officials upon return to Japan would make currency immediately available for necessary use upon return of the ship to Japan and at the same time would help insure that their Japanese currency was not misused in any foreign ports at which the ships might stop. It is recommended that this application be approved. This matter has been coordinated with SCAJAP/Merchant Shipping, and ESS/FT.

5. This is an administrative memorandum and does not require the surveillance of the Eighth Army.

6. Concurrences: SCAJAP.

W. K. L.

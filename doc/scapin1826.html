GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 004 (24 Nov 47) ESS/CCC

(SCAPIN-1826)

APO 500

24 November 1947

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :The Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
SUBJECT :Industrial Recovery Corporation (Sangyo Fukko Kodan.)

1. References are:

a. Memorandum for the Japanese Government, from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, file AG 004 (1 Nov 46) ESS/IN, (SCAPIN 1308), dated 1 November 1946, subject: Industrial Recovery Corporation.

b. Law No. 46 of 1946 of the Japanese Government, dated 21 October 1946, subject: Sangyo Fukko Eidan Bill, as amended by Law No. 57 of 1947 of the Japanese Government, dated 15 April 1947, subject: Sangyo Fukko Kodan Bill.

2. Reference 1 a above is revoked.

3. In administering reference 1b above, the Japanese Government will comply with the following provisions:

a. The Industrial Recovery Corporation shall dispose of, with the approval of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, any interest it holds or may hold in any plant or facility. This paragraph shall not prohibit the holding of any interest acquired under paragraph 3 c below.

b. The Industrial Recovery Corporation shall have no direct or indirect control over management or operation of any private enterprise, either by agreement with the company or otherwise. The Japanese Government shall declare invalid and inoperative any agreement or understanding for approval on the part of the Industrial Recovery Corporation or of any officer or employee thereof, of the appointment of any company official or of any act of the company.

c. When existing commercial and financial channels are not adequate to effect a program to rehabilitate or expand a critical industry, the Japanese Government will request the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers to approve such a program as a designated rehabilitation program. If a program is so approved, the Industrial Recovery Corporation may procure and lease or resell to private industry the equipment and facilities necessary to effect such a designated program. The Industrial Recovery Corporation will in no event be the sole agency for the procuring of such equipment and facilities. The Industrial Recovery Corporation shall not in any event procure facilities and equipment except as provided above.

d. The Economic Stabilization Board may authorize the Industrial Recovery Corporation to acquire and sell the following goods in accordance with the directions and programs of the Economic Stabilization Board:

(1) Any goods acquired by the Japanese Government in accordance with Economic Stabilization Board procedures for confiscation or for the redistribution of excessive inventories which do not come under jurisdiction of any other agency, or goods for which the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers holds the Japanese Government responsible for redistribution within the civilian economy.

(2) Goods to be distributed under a program of the Economic Stabilization Board for the rehabilitation of Japanese industry, where the goods cannot be distributed pursuant to such program without purchase by the Japanese Government and providing appropriations are made by the Diet for such governmental purchases.

e. The Industrial Recovery Corporation will dispose of no goods under control without complying with the appropriate ordinance relative to allocations, rationing, and/or price control. Consistent with these controls, the Industrial Recovery Corporation shall establish a procedure for fair competitive bidding, auctioning of materials, or sale by lottery, and shall, except where otherwise directed by the Economic Stabilization Board, provide a reasonable time and notice of any sale of goods.

f. Except for the powers specifically enumerated above, the Industrial Recovery Corporation will engage in no activities without the assent of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

g. The salaries of the officers and directors will be made public.

h. Adequate representation of middle-sized and small business will be included on the Board of Directores.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1826)

TKW/JMC/emm

24 October 1947

1. On 1 November 1946 by SCAPIN 1308 the Japanese proposal for the establishment of a public corporation (Industrial Recovery Corporation) for the rehabilitation of Japanese industry was approved with certain specific modifications. The purpose of the directive was to retain the necessary functions of the Sangyo Setsubi Eidan (Industrial Equipment Corporation), and at the same time permit liquidation of its wartime activities. During the last year it has become apparent that certain of the abuses of the Sangyo Setsubi Eidan have been perpetuated in the Sangyo Fukko Kodan (Industrial Recovery Corporation). These abuses exist without actual violation of the directive.

2. Under the directive as issued on 1 November 1946, the Sangyo Fukko Kodan was limited to dealing in industrial war materials, facilities, and equipment for the rehabilitation of peace-time industry. It has become apparent over the last year that there was an equally pressing need for a governmental corporation to distribute certain end products in cases where no other means for distribution were available.

3. The attached amendment to the directive of 1 November 1946 will accomplish the following results:

a. The Sangyo Fukko Kodan is directed to dispose of the plants and facilities which it has acquired or built. Under the original directive the Sangyo Fukko Kodan has, by purchasing or building new plants and leasing them to companies, in effect established a financial institution.

b. Although the Sangyo Fukko Kodan was specifically denied the power to nominate members of the Board of Directors of plants for which it provided facilities or equipment, it has achieved the same result by making informal agreements with the officials of companies which have applied for assistance requiring the approval of Sangyo Fukko Kodan on certain actions of the company, or the appointment of certain key officers. This results in actual control over management of a considerable portion of Japanese industry by the Sangyo Fukko Kodan which is a step away from the desired end of free enterprise.

c. Recently the Japanese have made informal proposals to establish public corporations to handle certain phases of production in specific industries. These informal proposals were unacceptable because they are in conflict with basic occupation policy. The Sangyo Fukko Kodan has the legal power to provide critical facilities and equipment required for the rehabilitation of peace-time Japanese industry. The Sangyo Fukko Kodan can therefore, be used as a device by the Japanese to circumvent SCAP policy. The proposed amendment requires the Japanese Government to get specific permission from SCAP before providing equipment and facilities for any industrial recovery program. It is contemplated that the coal production industry will be the test case for the new policy.

d. The directive provides that the Sangyo Fukko Kodan may, with the approval of ESB, acquire both industrial raw materials and consumer goods under certain specific circumstances. This occurs where goods are acquired by confiscation in black market raids or under the redistribution procedure. The amendment provides that the ESB may authorize the Sangyo Fukko Kodan to act as distributing agent for link goods or for other goods which are distributed pursuant to an ESB program for rehabilitation of industry. This function can only be authorized where a central purchasing agency is necessary and there is a specific Diet appropriation for the program.

e. There is a provision requiring that the Sangyo Fukko Kodan establish a procedure for competitive bidding or public auction.

4. Until the directive of 1 November 1946 is amended, the Sangyo Fukko Kodan can legally operate contrary to the original intention of this Headquarters. Furthermore, if SCAP-sponsored programs involving the redistribution of consumer goods are to be carried out, it will be necessary to condone violations insofar as it limits the activities of the Sangyo Fukko Kodan to exclude transactions in consumer goods.

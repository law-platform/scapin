MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1850)

ESS/IN JZR/RCAP/BVH/mn

12 January 1948

1. Ltr, CLO 9913 (EG), AG 464.6, subj: Request for Permission to Use Rotary Kiln at Alumina Plant of Nitto Kagaku Kogyo K.K. for Acid Production, dtd 4 Dec 47, requests use of rotary kilns and accessory equipment which are part of the alumina plant, for the purpose of supplementing pyrites roaster capacity of the sulfuric acid plant.

2. The Hachinohe plant belonging to Nitto Kagaku Kogyo K.K. is an approved fertilizer plant (SCAPIN 962, AG 464.6 (17 May 46) ESS/IN, subj: Production, Distribution and Use of Fertilizers). The rated capacity for fertilizer is 50,000 metric tons of ammonium sulfate per year. Acid requirement for this quantity of fertilizer is 60,000 metric tons per year expressed as 50° Be’ acid, or 38,100 metric tons per year expressed as 98% sulfuric acid. The present acid plant has a design capacity of 36,500 metric tons per year as 98% acid, and present capability is limited to 27,400 metric tons per year. The plant is ten years old and requires extensive repairing, particularly to existing roasting units. Combined use of old roasters and rotary kilns will make possible a production of sulfuric acid necessary to balance ammonia production. Capacity of the acid plant after proposed work has been completed will be 43,000 metric tons per year as 98% acid. However, production at this rate can be realized only under favorable conditions, and the apparent excess in capacity will allow the equipment to meet the acid demand for fertilizer if it becomes necessary to process lower grades of pyrite ore than presently planned. Also, excess acid is desirable at this plant since the 50,000 metric tons per year of ammonium sulfate is a conservative estimate based on ammonia capacity.

3. Memo to Japanese Government approves equipment listed in reference CLO letter for use in conjunction with manufacture of sulfuric acid.

4. No concurrence necessary.

5. Special surveillance by the Eighth Army is required, and letter is forwarded to CG, 8th Army.

6. This problem as it relates to Korea has been considered.

7. Refer questions to Mr. B. V. Hettich, 26-8953.

J. Z. R.

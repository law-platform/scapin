GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 368.3 (16 Oct 45) GD

(SCAPIN-151)

16 October 1945

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
SUBJECT :Materials, Supplies, and Equipment to be turned Over to the Imperial Japanese Government by Allied Forces.

1. Receipt is acknowledged of your memorandum, file number 219, same subject, dated 10 October 1945.

2. All policies relating to the subject material, supplies, and equipment are to be formulated on the basis of, (1) alleviating suffering and starvation, (2) restoration of the production and distribution of foodstuffs, (3) production of consumer goods and (4) the restoration of other civilian economy, mentioned in the order of priority.

3. The use of existing civilian channels in the distribution of these materials, supplies and equipment is satisfactory and is approved.

4. It is to be clearly understood that proceeds from the sale of any of the subject material, supplies, and equipment are to go to public (governmental) funds and are to be used solely for the relief of the civilian population. Detail records are to be kept which will clearly indicate not only the disposition of all items turned over to the Imperial Japanese Government but also disposition of funds which accrue from the sale of any of these items.

5. The program as submitted does not reflect adequate provision for holding stocks of staple food, clothing and medicine for distribution as direct relief in the future. It is recognized that need for all three materials currently exists throughout Japan. It is felt, however, that the need will be much more urgent and widespread in the late winter months of February, March and April, As the only known reserve for this critical period, these stocks of food, clothing, and medicines must be carefully husbanded. The exact time and place of greatest need, requiring use of these stocks, is to be determined by the Japanese Government giving full consideration to the objective that the stocks provide relief to the maximum number of Japanese civilians.

6. It is noted that the term “military repatriates” is used. With the complete demobilization of the Japanese Armed Forces, this headquarters recognizes no such group of “ex-military” personnel. The stocks in question are to be distributed to Japanese civilians. Included in the term “civilians” are, of course, demobilized military personnel, however disiribution for relief is be based on current need of the individual and in no case is any consideration to be given to the prior status of such individuals.

7. Under the items of particular emphasis listed in paragraph (3), page a of your memorandum, should be the constrnction of emergency housing. Maximum utilization must be made of building materials of all kinds for emergency housing in devastated areas.

8. Nothing in this letter is to be construed in such a manner as to prohibit the immediate disposal by the most expeditious means of any foodstuffs which are in danger of spoiling or becoming otherwise unfit for human consumption. Aggressive action to prevent such waste is directed. Retentisn of stocks for future distribution for civilian relief is to be limited to those which can be stored for the required time without serious deterioration.

9. The Imperial Japanese Government will take the necessary steps to provide adequate protection and maintenance for all stocks of supplies, materials and equipment received under the provisions of this program until final disposition of such stocks is made.

10. Subject to the provisions outlined in the foregoing paragraphs, authority is granted to initiate immediately distribution of the subject supplies, equipment and materials. As a matter of record, a revised program reflecting the indicated changes will be submitted to this headquarters without delay.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

H.W.ALLEN,
Colonel, A.G.D.,
Asst Adjutant General.

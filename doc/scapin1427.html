GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 004.01 (30 Dec 46) ESS/AC

(SCAPIN-1427)

APO 500

30 December 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Financial Plan for Rehabilitation of the Cotton Industry in Japan.

1. Reference is Central Liaison Office Memorandum No. 5770 (LO 1966/1149), dated 30 October 1946, subject: Financial Plan for Rehabilitation of the Cotton Industry in Japan, forwarding summary and details of rehabilitation fund plan and requesting permission to borrow ¥1,318,954,280. for the restoration of the cotton spinnig industry in Japan.

2. No objection is offered to the above application, subject to the following conditions:

a. The sum to be made available at this time shall not exceed ¥600,000,000.

b. There shall be submitted to the Antitrust and Cartels Division, Economic and Scientific Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, applications by each of the companies concerned showing details of proposed expenditures, prior to beginning the rehabilitation work and expenditure of funds.

c. No new or used spindles or spinning frames shall be purchased with the allocated funds, but new or used complementary equipment, except looms, sufficient to operate presently owned spindles may be purchased, if necessary, after approval of applications.

d. No part of the allocated rehabilitation funds is to be expended for the repair of buildings or machinery, or for the purchase of new complementary machinery, or construction of new buildings beyond the necessary requirements to operate the total spindles presently owned, whether or not installed.

e. First expenditure shall be made for the rehabilitation of buildings and machinery necessary to put the presently installed operable spindles into production.

f. A report shall be submitted on the last day of each month to the Textile Division, Economic and Scientific Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, in quintuplicate, typed in English on 8” x 11” paper, for each plant, containing the following information:

(1) Number of installed spindles.

(2) Number of operable spindles.

(3) Number of spindles owned but not installed.

(4) Number of cards installed.

(5) Number of cards operable.

(6) Number of cards owned but not installed.

g. This Memorandum shall not be construed as approval for priority of raw materials, equipment or facilities to carry out the plans for which this-loan is made. This established regulations, policies, procedures and priorities instituted by the Imperial Japanese Government shall prevail.

h. Direct communication is authorized between the interested Staff Sections of General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, and agencies of the Imperial Japanese Government concerned to implement all provisions of this Memorandum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD : (SCAPIN - 1427)

WFM/WHK/EMcK/lc

18 December 1946

1. References are:

a. CLO Memorandum No. 5770 (LO 1960/1149), 30 October 1946, forwards financial plan for rehabilitation of the cotton spinning industry of Japan and summary and details of rehabilitation fund plan for permission to borrow ¥1,318,954,280 for restoration of the cotton industry in Japan.

b. AG 004 (8 Dec 45) ESS/AC (SCAPIN-403), subject: "Establishment of a Schedule of Restricted Concerns."

c. AG 300.8 (8 Dec 45) ESS/AC (SCAPIN-408), subject: "Regulations Affecting Restricted Concerns."

d. AG 004 (8 Jun 46) ESS/AC (SCAPIN-1004), subject: "Addition of Certain Textile Companies to the Schedule of Restricted Concerns."

e. AG 004 (28 Sep 46) ESS/AC (SCAPIN-1238), subject: "Supplementary Regulations Affecting Restricted Concerns."

f. AG 004.05 (4 Oct 46) ESS/AC (SCAPIN-1250), subject: "Amendments to Memoranda Affecting Restricted Concerns."

2. a. The spinning industry in Japan is the basis of a large portion of Japan's exports. During the war much of the equipment and buildings of the ten (10) spinning companies of Japan, hereinafter named, were damaged or converted into war industries.

b. In order to restore Japan's economy, quantities of war cotton were imported to Japan by the United States Government to be processed and used for both export and domestic consumption.

c. The Textile Association and the Ministry of Finance have recommended rehabilitation for the following ten (10) companies in the amount of ¥1,318,954,280.:

(1) Dai Nippon Cotton Spinning Company, Ltd.

(2) Oriental Cotton Spinning Company, Ltd.

(3) Daiken Industrial Company, Ltd.

(4) Daiwa Industry Company, Ltd.

(5) Shikishima Cotton Spinning Company Ltd.

(8) Fuji Cotton Spinning Company, Ltd.

(7) Kurashiki Industry Company, Ltd.

(8) Nisshin Cotton Spinning Company, Ltd.

(9) Nitto Cotton Spinning Company, Ltd.

(10) Kanegafuchi Cotton Spinning Company, Ltd.

3. a. The Textile Division, ESS, states that the proposed rehabilitation loan is essential for the purpose of bringing production of cotton spinning to its necessary level. Since this proposed rehabilitation must be accomplished as quickly as possible, it is desirable that approximately one-half of the proposed credit be made available at this time and that the balance be held in abeyance pending observation of production results. Without increased cotton yarn production the payment for raw cotton obtained through the Commodity Credit Corporation would be delayed.

b. No new or used spindles or frames are to be purchased with the allowed credit and each company must file an application with Antitrust and Cartels Division, ESS, showing the details of the proposed expenditures for each operating mill prior to allowance of the respective expenditures. Monthly reports of installations and repairs are to be submitted to the Textile Division, ESS.

c. The respective companies may finance their loans through regular banking facilities or the Reconstruction Finance Bank of Japan.

d. No objection is offered to the above application provided that the present loan does not exceed ¥600,000,000.

4. This is an administrative matter and no surveillance by the Eighth Army is required.

5. The interests of USAFIK are not concerned.

6. Concurrences: ESS/TD (Major H. S. Tate, Mr. W. R. Eaton); ESS/FI (Lt. Col. D. H. Jennings);

ESS/RS (Mr. S. H. Deibert).

13 August 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Reparations Selections Within Privately-Owned Munitions Plants.

1. The Imperial Japanese Government is notified that all munitions plants on the attached list are hereby taken into custody and control of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

2. This list designates plants subject to removal under the interim reparations program. Pending further instructions they may continue operations if they are producing items necessary to the civilian economy, in accordance with instructions from the Commanding General, Eighth United States Army. In all cases, whether or not operations continue in listed plants, you will take such measures as are necessary to insure proper protection, maintenance and safeguarding of equipment.

3. You will make available to the Commanding General, Eighth United States Army, all personnel, equipment and supplies deemed necessary by him for the establishment of proper custody, control and protective maintenance.

4. Within seventy-two (72) hours of the receipt of this memorandum the Imperial Japanese Government will have qualified representatives report to the Commanding General, Eighth United States Army, to receive detailed instructions to accomplish the purpose of this memorandum.

5. Provisions of this memorandum shall not be, construed to preclude immediate shut down and removal of specific plants that may be designated by subsequent memoranda.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

Information of General Application pertaining to Directive Number (SCAPIN-1134) file AG 387.6 (13 Aug 46) ESS/IN dated 13 August 1946 Subject: "Reparations Selections of privately owned munitions Plants. "

1. With reference to Memorandum Number (SCAPIN-1134), from the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers to the Imperial Japanese Government, the following is published for the information of all concerned.

2. The purpose in issuing the attached letter to the Imperial Japanese Government and accompanying letter to the Commanding General, Eighth United States Army, is to insure availability for interim reparations removals of listed facilities in the best possible condition.

3. The same standards will be adhered to in preserving these facilities as in other plants heretofore listed for reparations.

4. All machinery, equipment, and accessories, which by virtue of initial design, construction, or major structural change, are, as individual items, special purpose in nature and functionally limited to use in connection with the manufacture of equipment or supplies for war or warlike purposes, will be held at locations you specify, pending further instructions concerning their disposition.

5. The closing down of listed factories is not required by this directive. Insofar as possible all production in these factories should be arranged so that shut-down, when necessary for reparations removal, will have the minimum disruptive effect on the overall civilian economy.

6. It is contemplated that the Japanese will furnish personnel and material required by you; that they will use, for custodial personnel, individuals previously employed in each factory who are familiar with its layout and requirement for maintenance.

7. It is expected that the Japanese will perform proper maintenance of equipment to preserve it in good condition. Such maintenance is to include sufficient oiling and greasing with necessary materials to be furnished by the Japanese Government when not available at any plants.

8. It is expected that a system will be established for spotchecking each installation periodically. When corrective action can be accomplished locally by explanation it should be done. In cases of wilful violation or neglect immediate report should be made through military channels for corrective action.

9. A letter of command instructions pertaining to the execution of the subject memorandum follows through command channels.

/s/ A. J. Rehe

/t/ R. G. HERSEY,

Lt. Col., AGD,

Asst. Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD : (SCAPIN - 1134)

JZR/CDR./frg

13 August 1946

1. Washington radio 251337 Z from Joint Chief of Staff dated 26 June directs SCAP to designate privately owned munitions plants for availability under the interim reparations program. It defines such plants as facilities primarily engaged in the manufacture of weapons, missiles, ammunition and military explosives.

2. a. During the war virtually 50% of the Japanese industry was engaged in munitions manufacture to some extent. This list of plants in eludes only those facilities originally designed for making arms, armament and military explosives, and those whose conversion to such production was of a radical nature making reconversion to peacetime operation unsuitable.

b. This selection of plants may be summarized as follows:

(1) Metal Working Plants: No. of Plants Total

Over 1,000 Machine tools 18 51,455

Over 500 Machine tools 21 14,826

Over 100 Machine tools 187 38,951

Over 50 Machine tools 18 1,536

No record of machine tools 8

Total 252 106,768

(2) Military explosives plants:

21 Privately owned factories, primarily designed and operated to produce explosives are included in this list. No plant concerned in the current fertilizer program or the manufacture of other vitally needed industrial chemicals or explosives is named for custody and control.

(3) Thus, 106,768 machine tools and 21 explosives plants are made available for reparations or other disposition under the interim

program.

3. This directive to the Imperial Japanese Government, Command letter to Eighth Army, and letter of general application will accomplish custody and control of listed plants which will be held for reparations.

4. Concurrences: G-4 - Col. Breden

J. Z. R.

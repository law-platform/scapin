SUBJECT :Construction of Breakwater in the Izu Islands

1. Reference is C. L. O. letter No. 3022 (CM), dated 23 April 1947, subject as above, in which details of the proposed breakwater construction were outlined as directed by Memorandum for the Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, AG 560 (7 Jan 47) ESS/IN, (SCAPIN-1441), dated 7 January 1947, subject: Construction of Breakwater in the Izu Islands.

2. The proposed construction of the breakwater in the Kaminata Harbor on the Island of Hachijo Jima in the Izu Islands is approved.

3. You will contact the Commander Naval Forces Far East for details as to availability and methods of disposition of the craft to be utilized in implementing this plan.

MEMO FOR RECORD : (SCAPIN - 1724)

2 June 1947

1. References are the following:

a. CLO Letter No. 4669 (CM), dated 13 September 1946, Subject: Conversion of Ex-Naval Vessels into Breakwater on the Izu Islands.

b. SCAPIN 1441, dated 7 January 1947, subject: Construction of Breakwater in the Izu Islands.

c. C. L. O. letter No. 3022 (CM), dated 23 April 1947, subject: Construction of Breakwater in the Izu Islands.

2. Reference a above requested approval of a plan to utilize three ex-Japanese naval vessels subject for scrapping, for the construction of a breakwater in the Izu Islands. The breakwater is proposed to be constructed in the Kaminato Harbor on the Island of Hachijo Jima. Reference b above indicated that the breakwater plan was favorably considered, but that prior to the construction of the breakwater, detailed specifications, plans, method of financing and source of quantity of materials required be submitted for approval to SCAP. Reference c above submits the information as directed by Reference b.

3. ESS/lndustry personnel inspected the site of the breakwater to ascertain the needs of the breakwater and the benefits which the community would derive from a fishing and cargo port. Hachijo Jima is a volcanic island, located 150 miles south of Tokyo and is included in the Tokyo Prefecture. The island produces for export to the Tokyo Area marine products, butter, salt, powered milk, charcoal and silk worms. These exports amount to about twenty-five (25%) percent more in value than the island imports. The construction of a harbor for a fishing base and cargo port will increase the marine products for export from Hachijo Jima over ten-fold as the sheltered harbor would enable the fishing vessels to enter and leave the harbor at all times, except during severe storms. The size of the fishing vessels would not be restricted to small five ton vessels which are pulled up on marine ramps at the conclusion of each fishing operation to avoid destruction by choppy seas. Also, the harbor would allow the construction of a small quay wall where cargo ship-to-shore operations would increase the efficiency of cargo handling. Fifty thousand bales of charcoal were undelivered from the last fiscal year production due to rough weather not permitting the loading in the open roadstead.

4. From information outlined in the above paragraph, it is believed that the breakwater construction plan is justifiable and will give numerous benefits to the island, as well as to the Japan mainland, from the increased production of needed marine food products and added facilities for shipping export items to the mainland.

5. At a conference between ComNavFe (Capt. Dunning) and ESS/IN (Mr. Valentine), the proposed breakwater was discussed and it was indicated that the vessels for the construction were available. The Kumano Maru is, at present, being used for repatriation purposes but will be released in September 1947. The partial dismantling of the vessel will require some time but should be ready for sinking in May 1948, which is the most ideal month for the operation. The two smaller vessels can be made immediately available.

6. The Industrial Division is aware that such a method of construction of a breakwater has certain drawbacks due to the possibility of the vessels shifting in a heavy storm or typhoon which might endanger local water traffic. The location of the proposed breakwater is remote from Allied shipping and any potential hazards are the responsibility of the Japanese Government. The proposed method of constructing the breakwater is very economical and can be accomplished in a considerably shorter time than the conventional method of constructing coffer dams, driving piles and concrete pourings.

7. Colonel Melberg, Commander of the Tokyo-Kanagawa District Military Government, is aware of the island's needs and harbor development plans. At a conference with ESS (Mr. Valentine), Colonel Melberg stated that it was his belief that the breakwater would be of great benefit to both the island and the Tokyo Prefecture.

8. Action taken: The attached proposed SCAPIN approves the construction of the breakwater in the Izu Islands.

9. Routine surveillance is required on this activity and will be reported in normal monthly Occupation Activities Reports.

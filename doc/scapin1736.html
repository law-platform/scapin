SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

APO 500

21 June 1947

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Angaur Phosphate Project.

1. The Japanese Government will take immediate action and assume responsibility as of 1 July 1947 for the complete operation of the Angaur Phosphate Project under the supervision of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

2. To implement this project, the Japanese Government will:

a. Recruit, transport, and maintain sufficient Japanese administrative and labor personnel on Angaur Island. Palau Group, so that the mining and loading operations presently under contract to J. H. Pomeroy & Co., Inc. will not be retarded.

b. Mine and process only phosphate stockpile and blanket type deposits estimated at 1,258,741 long tons.

c. Observe native customs and private rights, and segregate all Japanese personnel in areas specified in accordance with orders of the representative of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

d. Return to Japan all Japanese personnel upon completion of this project.

e. Be responsible for the custody and maintenance of the operating facilities and Keep complete records therof.

f. Submit requests to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers for such facilities as may be required but which are not indigenous to Japan.

g. Upon completion of this project, return to Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers all facilities, in good order, less normal wear and tear, along with all supporting records.

h. Rehabilitate the six (6) damaged kiln dryers and all other accessory equipment required in the drying process located, at Angaur Island, as soon as possible.

i. Process on arrival in Japan, all of the phosphate rock, within the shortest possible time into superphosphate in the factories nearest the ports of discharge. The superphosphate will be sold through official channels at ceiling prices in the areas of greatest need. The Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers will be furnished with a complete accounting of the distribution made, by prefectural areas.

j. Be responsible to insure that all ministries and agencies of the Japanese Government concerned will assist in the executioncution of this project.

3. Subsequent information and operational instructions relative to this project will be isued as required.

4. Direct communication is hereby authorized between the Economic and Scientific Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, and the interested agencies of the Japanese Government to implement the provisions of this memorandum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R. M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

2. Washington radio ref a. advises that Navy and J. H. Pomeroy and Co., Inc., contractor, both desire to, terminate their Angaur Phosphate Project responsibilities 1 July'1947 and Navy suggests SCAP take over, using Japanese personnel. WAR states that Japanese opr considered more desirable than US contractor on CPFF basis since if Japanese operate Angaur, less appropriated funds will be required, which would release such funds for greater procurement of potash and ammonium nitrate.

3. SCAP radio ref b., offers use of SCAP personnel for supervision and liaison at Angaur and Japanese personnel for actual operation of their Project starting 1 July 1947.

4. Washington radio, ref. c., grants permission to SCAP to assume operating responsibility, 1 July 1947 under present operating conditions.

5. To implement the transfer of supervision from the Navy to SCAP and operation from J. H. Pomeroy & Co. , Inc. to the Japanese Government, Maj Emzy H. Lynch, F.A., 0-200759, and S/Sgt. Elwood A. Williams, RA, 39399051, were ordered to proceed to Angaur, and departed from Yokohama, Japan, aboard the SCAJAP Steamer "Arimasan Maru", 10 May 1947. The above officer and EM to act as liaison team to Navy Military Government, also SCAP representatives to supervise the Japanese operation commencing 1 July 1947 and will maintain their headquarters at Angaur.

6. Accompanying the team mentioned in para 5 above were Mr. Ernest Blessing, Scientific Consultant, Natural Resources Section, and Major Haydn A. Schettler, C.E., 0-236212, Unit Chief, Metals, Minerals & Fertilizer Unit, Foreign Trade Div., ESS. Mr. Blessing's responsibility was to review the phosphate reserves, observe the mining and loading operations and proposed drying procedures and make recommendations for future operations under SCAP supervision. Maj Schettler's responsibility was to review all transfer details, supply problems, shipping schedules, obtain pertinent papers and drawings covering the operation, and set up proposed-Japanese operating organization.

7. Accompanying the Army personnel were the following Japanese officials:

a. Mr. Tomoyasu Yoda, Mining Eng and official of the Board of Trade;

b. Mr. Isao Uchimura, Agricultural Technician and official of the Board of Trade;

c. Mr. Takayuki Takahashi, Agricultural and Mining Eng.;

d. Mr. Morichika Shibata, Hydraulic Eng.;

e. Mr. Masayoshi Hiroki, Agricultural Technician;

f. Mr. Shinkichi Minami, Managing Director, Phosphate Import Association.

8. The Arimasan Maru arrived at Angaur Friday, 16 May 47 and the SCAP group and Japanese representatives were met by Commander C. E Spellman, USN, representing Captain E. L. Hansen, USN, officer in charge of construction and contract NOY 13853 (Angaur Pomeroy Contract) Both Captain Hansen and Cmdr. Spellman make their Hq. at Guam. Commander Spellman arrived at Angaur by air from Guam the night before to be on hand to assist the SCAP group in the transfer details. Other officials that boarded the Arimasan Maru were: Mr. E. L. Causey, Project Mgr J. H. Pomeroy & Co., Inc. of contracts NOY 13311 (Guam) and NOY 13853 (Angaur). His Hq were also at Guam; Mr. C. Y. Hammett, Asst Project Hq. Angaur; Mr. J. B. Carmichael, Navy Project Mgr. Angaur-Peieliu Group, Hq. Angaur.

9. Commander Spellman and Mr. Causey did not have any documents at Angaur and very little concrete info was available re the Navy-Pomeroy contract, NOY 13853. It appears that the Navy issued a letter of intent in 1946 (exact date unknown) (Cost plus Fixed Fee) to the contractor covering two projects:

Project I - Mine and load phosphate rock aboard lighters until 1 July 1947;

Project II - Rehabilitate loading bridge and conveyor, to be completed by 1 Apr 47 (actually put into operation 13 May 47).

Mr. Causey stated that a contract drawn on a standard NOY form was signed by the J. H. Pomeroy Co. in San Francisco, California about 5 May 1947 and returned to Washington. No copies to date had been received either at Guam or Angaur. He also stated that there was no option clause in contract for an extension beyond 1 July 1947. There was a change order clause in the standard Y&D form; however, Mr. Causey stated that his company was willing to complete their contract by 1 July 1947 and had no objection to SCAP taking over on that date.

10. It appears that all facilities (except some of the Belt conveyor loaders) in the Angaur operation originated from the U. S. Armed Forces (Army, Navy, Marine Corps). The 91st Infantry Div. built up a large quonset hut and tent area at Angaur, now being used to house the contractor personnel, natives, and some Japanese. Roads, water, elec., and other facilities are available and in good condition to house approx. 1,500 people.

11. The bulk of the mining equipment was shipped from Guam (Navy) and consists of approximately 262 pieces, including everything from jeeps to power shovels and tugs. An inventory will be assembled and made the subject of a separate report.

12. From Peleliu, located about 6 miles northeast of Angaur, a host of fill-in items originated (Marines). Capt. L. O. Fox, USN, Cmdr of the Western Carolines sub-area, Iscom, Palaus, and Senior Military Govt officer, was interviewed on 17 May at Peleliu. He advised that Peleliu station was closing down about 1 July 47 and that he and his group of several officers and about 40 EM were leaving for Guam. At its height of activity, Peleliu had a large force of troops based there, possibly 10,000 or more. There are very large stockpiles of all types of military impediments, buildings (quonset type est. at from 700 to 1000), vehicles, and warehouses full of new spare parts for practically every type of engine or vehicle now at Angaur.

13. Capt Fox advised that a large part of this material was earmarked for China and the balance for MG unit, Palaus. It is strongly recommended that clearance be obtained from the necessary auth for any material at Peleliu that can be used on Angaur during the life of the phosphate project, presently est at 4 to 5 years. This clearance should be gained immediately. SCAP, Angaur representatives be so advised, and the physical transfer commenced. Capt Fox advised that he had turned the MG aspect of his command over to Commander Wm. C. Ball, USNR, located at Koror Island, about 40 miles N. E. of Angaur.

14. On 21 May 47, Lt. H. E. Wahl (Navy), MG officer stationed at Angaur, Maj Lynch and Maj Schettler proceeded to Koror Island aboard the LCI 977, arriving at 1530. Commander Ball, Dept Chief MG Unit, Palaus, Lt. Com. Donald Heron, Exec Officer, and Lt F. W. Avila, Industry Officer, were interviewed.

15. From this interview, it was determined that MG desires SCAP to allow any native on Angaur the opportunity to work on the Phosphate Project. As of 19 May 47, there were 84 natives out of a total Angaur native population of 383 on the J. H. Pomeroy Co. payroll. Their wages were set by MG and ranged from 80 to 50 cents per work man per day. It was agreed that Maj Schettler, on his return to Japan, would request funds, at the rate of 75 cents, U.S. per day per one year, to work 75 natives 25 days per month. MG has no funds for such work.

16. MG felt that it would be very unwise not to allow the Angaur natives to continue working and could be a source of trouble. It would also give money to the natives to buy trade goods. Majors Lynch and Schettler were of the same opinion. It was further decided that in the event funds were available to work the natives, that a separate native organization be set up whereby native workers would have a native foreman, and a native superintendent, who would report directly to Lt Wahl, MG rep at Angaur; that Lt Wahl and Maj Lynch, SCAP liaison officers, would work together on policy matters.

17. Commander Spellman and Mr. Carmichael agreed to allow every item now at Angaur to remain there to continue the operation under SCAP. Maj Lynch and Lt Wahl will work out details re division of facilities among SCAP, Navy, the natives, and Japanese. All items are listed on stock control cards and spot checks will be made to insure items are physically at Angaur. No equipment was brought in or is owned by the contractor.

18. As of 1 July 47, the following phosphate reserves are est. will be available at Angaur:

Stockpiled: 34,000 long tons reduced to dry phosphate

Unmined: 759,726 long tons reduced to dry phosphate

Total 793,726

A separate report will be submitted detailing above figures. It is est. 1,215,561 long tons of phosphate ore containing 40% moisture and 20% impurities are to be mined.

19. Mining, Segregation and Drying Plan - Mine approx. 1000 long tons phosphate ore daily for 25 days per month. This ore is est. to contain approx. 40% moisture plus about 20% impurities such as overburden, coral, vegetation, etc. This raw ore will than be sundried from approx. 40% moisture to approx. 23% moisture or from 1000 LT to approx 830 LT. During the sundrying process, the coral, overburden, and vegetation will be hand segregated to further reduce the 830 LT down to 730 LT (10% of 1000 LT).

20. Mechanical Drying Plan - Operate 4 to 5 kiln dryers; each dryer capable of producing 120 to 150 tons of phosphate ore, containing not over 5% moisture, per 24 hours, or 730 long tons of phosphate ore will enter the dryers and produce an est. 600 LT of phosphate ore, containing not more than 5% moisture and relatively free from coral, overburden, and vegetable matter. Kilns to operate 25 days per month x 600 LT equaling 15,000 LT monthly.

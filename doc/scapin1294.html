GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 463.7 (25 Oct 46) ESS/AC

(SCAPIN-1294)

APO 500

25 October 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Distribution of Petroleum Products.

1. References are the following Memoranda for the Imperial Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers:

a. AG 334 (3 Apr 46) ESS/IE, 3 April 1946, (SCAPIN-854), subject: “Board of Trade (Boeki Cho).”

b. AG 463 (21 May 46) GD, 21 May 1946, (SCAPIN-791), subject: “Receipt and Distribution of Petroleum Products.”

2. The Imperial Japanese Government will revoke all statutes, ordinances and orders so far as they relate to the distribution of petroleum and petroleum products. Statutes, ordinances and orders relating to the use of petroleum and petroleum products will remain in effect.

3. The Imperial Japanese Government will dissolve the Petroleum Distribution Control Company, except for the purpose of holding assets for liquidation.

4. The Imperial Japanese Government will submit to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, in quintuplicate, typed in English on 8"x 11" paper, within fifteen (15) days of the receipt of this directive, a proposal for establishment of a corporation responsible to the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, including in this proposal the following considerations:

a. A provision that the corporation be capitalized in whole with government funds.

b. A provision that the corporation be the sole distribution agency for petroleum products, except that those persons engaged in the business of selling petroleum products to consumers map engage in such distribution.

c. A provision that the corporation have the following powers:

(1) To acquire by lease any or all facilities and equipment of the Petroleum Distribution Control Company.

(2) To acquire and distribute all petroleum products, whether imported or domestic.

(3) To allocate, with the assent of the Ministry of Transportation and acting under the direction of the Economic Stabilization Board, all transportation facilities suitable for the distribution of petroleum and petroleum products.

5. The Imperial Japanese Government will establish a program for rationing of petroleum products to individual purchasers to be administered by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry and acting under the direction of the Economic Stabilization Board.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1294)

WFM/VHK/TKW/gg

17 October 1946

1. Boeki Cho, a government agency, was established by SCAP on 3 April 1946, as the sole importing concern for petroleum products. On 21 May 1946, SCAP established the PDCC, a privately owned profit corporation, as the sole agency for the distribution of petroleum products, because of the critical shortage of supply and the consequent need for the minutest of controls over the distribution of petroleum. In the fiscal year 1 April 1946 - 31 March 1947 approximately 700,000 KL of refined petroleum products will be available for all Japanese requirements. In 1934, 2,537,000 KL were consumed by the civilian economy. Thus it is clear that petroleum products essential to the operation of transportation, fishing, and key industries of Japan will be in extremely short supply and can be made available only for the most essential consumption. Neither the PDCC, nor any other private organization is in a position to do an effective job of rationing such a commodity. It is a governmental function to be performed by a governmental agency if possible.

2. Under JCS directive 1380/15 SCAP is charged with removal of private associations from the exercise of governmental functions, and to create public agencies where it is necessary to continue those functions. The control over the distribution of oil in short supply is a governmental function. However, PDCC is charged with distribution and rationing functions, subject to the directions of the Ministry of Commerce and Industry. The directions of the Ministry relate only to allocations by prefecture. The distribution is made, for the most part, either directly to large consumers or through trade associations of consumer who themselves ration the product to the users. The effective rationing of petroleum is handled, therefore, largely by private associations without any substantial governmental control. Not only does this method lead to discrimination, but deprives the government of reliable statistics on which to determine the requirements for petroleum in essential uses. G-4 makes an estimate of essential needs for petroleum based on requirement submitted through PDCC. PDCC submits requirements on the basis of any information received from the associations and large consumers with whom it deals. The fact that, during the war, the Ministry of Commerce and Industry was able to administer a coupon rationing system for petroleum products is sufficient proof that the Ministry is able to take the responsibility for estimating essential demands for petroleum and to supervise an equitable distribution system.

3. Of the 700,000 KL total supply in fiscal year 1947, 200,000 KL are being refined from indigenous crudes and 500,000 KL are being imported by the Japanese Government. In the absence of an official rate of exchange the cost of imported refined products cannot be determined, but both imported and domestic products are pooled and sold at the same retail price so as to avoid price competition between the two. The operation of this pool is a function which can be performed best by a governmental agency. When an exchange rate is established, there will be profits or losses from the pooled sales and it is appreciated that in either case the public treasury should assume the risk.

4. PDCC has been wasteful and corrupt. That a considerable amount of gasoline has found its way onto the black market cannot be doubted. BY substituting a clear line of responsibility in the government for the mechanics of distribution of petroleum, it is hoped that the situation can be improved. PDCC is now staffed for its wartime job, at which time it handled several times the petroleum now in distribution. An excessive number of personnel has been kept on the job.

5. PDCC has been inefficient. There has been no effective governmental control over PDCC, and there have been many occasions of delay in meeting program dates for rationing because of failures by PDCC to make prompt deliveries. There have also been many instances in which petroleum products were not delivered at all to places difficult of access. For example, supplies for Hokkaido fishing vessels have been spotty. The PDCC has complained that lack of transportation is responsible for its inefficiencies. To a certain extent, the complaint is sound and, accordingly, it is proposed that the government agency be given power to allocate transportation facilities, with the assent of the Ministry of Transportation. While PDCC has a certain amount of transportation facilities, it has no control over those belonging to Crude Oil Transportation Company which can well be used in the petroleum distribution program. Similarly, facilities under the control of chemical companies, used in the distribution of alcohol, could to a certain extent be directed to the transportation of petroleum in cases of emergency.

6. Very recently an association called the Petroleum Distributors Association has been created to handle the operating functions of PDCC leaving PDCC solely as a liquidating agency. The PDA differs in composition only in that the interests of the refineries have been eliminated. It is a private association operating in every way as the PDCC has been operating. The same considerations exist for the removal of control functions from this association, although it may have a reason to continue to exist as a trade association.

7. While the creation of a public corporation will not be a complete solution to the problem of petroleum distribution, it will be an improvement. In any event, no sufficient reasons exist for departing in this case from the JCS policy that governmental functions be handled by government agencies.

8. This is an administrative matter, and no surveillance by the Eighth Army is required.

9. The interests of USAFIK are not concerned.

10. Concurrences: Government Section (Brig. Gen. C. Whitney); ESS/IN (J. Z. Reday); ESS/PCR (Mr. H. F. Alber); ESS/LE (Major E. R. Minnich); TS (Mr. E. F. Powell); G-4 Petroleum (Col. R. W. Moore).

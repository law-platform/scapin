SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 401.1 (21 Feb 48) ESS

(SCAPIN-1863)

APO 500

21 February 1948

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
SUBJECT :Inventory of Critical Materials.

1. Reference:

a. Memorandum for the Japanese Government, AG 091 (15 Sep 45) MG, SCAPIN 29, subject: Production in Non-War Plants.

b. Economic Stabilization Board Instruction No. 6, dated 10 Feb 48, subject: Regulations Concerning the Procedure of Utilization of Idle Materials.

2. Reference 1a above requires the Japanese Government to “initiate, maintain and enforce production and other economic programs that serve the following purposes:....b. The assurance of a just and impartial distribution of available supplies:....d. The restoration of Japanese Economy in order that the reasonable peacetime requirements of the population can be satisfied.”

3. In order to assist in the most efficient utilization of indigenous resources in promoting economic rehabilitation, the Japanese Government is directed to take the necessary steps to make a comprehensive inventory of all goods and materials designated as critical materials in lists 1 to 4 of reference 1 b, above presently held in industrial, commercial and distributive installations, including but not limited to factories, mills, mines, warehouses, storage yards and wholesale dealers’ establishments, except the following:

a. Goods and materials in transit.

b. Goods and materials under production.

4. Inventories prepared in accordance with paragraph 3 above will be submitted within 60 days after the date of this Memorandum by the Japanese Government to the Military Government Unit for the prefecture concerned. Inventories will be as of a single date for the entire country. A complete copy of the inventory of stocks at each installation will be retained at the installation concerned.

5. All materials and stocks whose inventory is required by paragraph 3 above and which are not registered will be subject to confiscation without compensation as illegally held goods.

6. Direct communication is authorized between the Economic and Scientific Section and competent Ministries of the Japanese Government for implementation of this Memorandum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1863)

WFM/TC/mc

6 February 1948

1. Recent reports from U.S. news correspondents, private persons and the Japanese Diet Committee on Excess and Hoarded Goods indicate the strong likelihood that large quantities of industrial stocks and certain types of consumer goods are being withheld from the domestic economy. These indications are supported by the results of Military Government spot checks in Kanagawa and Tokyo Prefectures and by inconsistencies of considerable magnitude in Japanese Government official statistics on production, consumption and stockpiles of critical materials.

2. The withholding of these stocks is both a deterrent to Japanese industrial recovery and a positive stimulus to inflation. At the same time such withholding results in general loss of effectiveness of U.S. appropriated funds expended for Japanese imports and in certain cases there is a distinct possibility that U.S. appropriated funds may be used to import the same items which Japanese may be hoarding.

3. The proposed inventory would locate excess stocks held legally and would permit detailed Occupational surveillance of Japanese Government actions to locate and redistribute excess stocks. It would be available as a basis for informal allocation and export-import planning. At the same time, since the Japanese law allows holding of stocks only in proportion to the rate of industrial production, it would stimulate the factory owner to put his stocks into production. Finally, the inventory would furnish a clear distinction between legally held excess stocks, which may be requisitioned under Japanese law with full compensation, and illegally concealed stocks, which may be confiscated.

4. The Japanese Government under existing law has the power to inventory critical stocks, so that no new Japanese legislation would be needed to implement this SCAPIN. The issuance of a SCAPIN is desirable in order to:

a. Establish the legal authority for direct Occupation action if deemed necessary later on to locate, seize or impound concealed goods.

b. Short-circuit existing consistent Japanese Government apathy, ineffectiveness and even sabotage of efforts to locate excess and concealed goods.

c. Coordinate effectively a variety of actions of Japanese government agencies of different levels and facilitate accurate, rapid and consistent reporting to Occupation prefectural and central authorities on a nationwide basis.

5. Command letter directs CG, 8th Army to receive and forward the inventories to GHQ, SCAP, to spot-check individual inventories for accuracy, to call the attention of competent Japanese officials to suspected excess inventories, and require reports of actions taken by them.

6. The interests of USAFIK are not involved.

7. Concurrences: G-3 (with respect to command letter only); GS, Gen. Whitney (13 Feb 1948) by c/n LS, Alva C. Carpenter

T. C.

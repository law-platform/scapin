MEMORANDUM FOR :Japanese Government
SUBJECT :Release of the Commercial and Industrial Bank of Taiwan from Supervision of the Closed Institutions Liquidating Commission

1. References are:

a. Letter P/C-l-15 from Closed Institutions Comptroller, Property Custodian Bureau, Ministry of Finance, Japanese Government, to General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, Economic and Scientific Section, Fair Trade Practices Division, dated 8 November 1950, subject: Re Application for Release of the Commercial and Industrial Bank of Taiwan from the category for the Closed Institutions.

b. Memorandum for the Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, AG 004.2 (6 Feb 46) ESS/FI, SCAPIN 707, 6 February 1946, subject: Closing the Offices in Japan of the Commercial and Industrial Bank of Taiwan and the Bank of Kanan.

2. Reference 1a recommends the cancellation of the designation, under reference memorandum 1b, of the Commercial and Industrial Bank of Taiwan as a closed institution.

3. On the basis of the facts as submitted by the Japanese Government, no objection is found to said recommendation.

4. Reference 1b is, therefore, amended so as to release from the application of its provisions the Commercial and Industrial Bank of Taiwan.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

/s/ K. B. Bush
/t/ K. B. BUSH,
Brigadier General, USA
Adjutant General

1. SCAPIN 707 designated the Commercial and Industrial Bank of Taiwan as a closed institution by bringing it within the coverage of SCAPIN 74, which directed the closure of the head offices, branches and agencies in Japan of certain specific institutions named therein, and of all other banks development companies and institutions whose foremost purpose had been the financing of colonization and development activities in areas outside Japan or the financing of war production by the mobilization or control of financial resources in colonial or Japanese occupied territory. The JG has requested the cancellation of designation of the Commercial and Industrial Bank of Taiwan as a closed institution (references: ltr from CI Comptroller, Property Custodian Bureau, M/F. JG, cited in para 1a of proposed SCAPIN and attached as Incl 1, and CILC Memo to ESS/Fl/Liq, C.I./11c – No. 6, 20 Jan 49, attached as Incl 2). The granting of such request would substitute for the procedure of SCAPIN 7317-A which is followed in disposing of residual proceeds from liquidation of closed institutions, that set forth in SCAPINs 1965 through 1965/4 as implemented by Cabinet Order No. 291 which is followed in regard to the same type of proceeds from branches in Japan of companies with head offices outside Japan.

2. The facts submitted reveal the CIT to be a Japanese juridical person organized in 1910 and engaged in the general banking business in Formosa. Its capital, promoted and supplied by indigenes, was used to render financial aid to small and intermediate local enterprisers in commerce, industry and agriculture. It had no connection with the economic combines, nor did it help finance overseas trade nor territorial improvement of newly occupied land, nor the conduct of war - all of which were types of activities conducted by the institutions enumerated in SCAPIN 74. The CIT had its Tokyo office in the Bank of Taiwan building. The size of that office - resident executive staff of one business managing clerk and one assistant - supports the conclusion that it was maintained merely for liaison purposes - adjusting balances of exchange and conducting accessory business with the Ministry of Finance, the Bank of Japan and the like - and not for banking business purposes. Although it had some connections with the Bank of Taiwan, a Japanese Government-dominated closed institution used for implementing colonial expansion, such relations did not involve those functions of the Bank of Taiwan which caused it to be closed under SCAPIN 74. The CIT would appear to fall rather in the category of the Shokai Bank, now being reorganized under Cabinet Order No. 291 than in that set forth in SCAPIN 74. When the Bank of Taiwan was ordered closed pursuant to SCAPIN 74, the building was put off limits and CIT also was automatically cut off from effective accessibility. Later, SCAPIN 707 applied SCAPIN 74 to the CIT and formally declared what already was an accomplished fact. The supporting M/R for SCAPIN 707 merely states that the CIT is a Formosan bank with a liaison office in Tokyo and gives no further reason for its closure. It is assumed that it was regarded mistakenly as a subsidiary of the Bank of Taiwan.

3. The status of the assets and liabilities of the CIT is approximately in the same position as it was on the date of its closure, except for the collection of ¥9,000,000 in loans and the liquidation of ¥20,000,000 in domestic bonds. There is little to be done with these assets other than to safeguard them for the benefit of overseas creditors and to distribute the balance to shareholders. Whether liquidation takes place under SCAPIN 7317-A or under SCAPINs 1965 - 1965/4 the same general principles concerning protection of overseas claims would be observed. However, while under SCAPIN 7317-A residual assets are put in the Bank of Japan for an indefinite period, under SCAPINs 1965 - 1965/4, if overseas liabilities can be determined, only such assets as are needed to cover them need be set aside and the balance of the assets is then available for disposal in accordance with Cabinet Order No. 291. As for the mechanics of the transfer, the CILC says that release from its jurisdiction can be easily accomplished upon amendment of SCAPIN 707. CPC has expressed a willingness to assume the responsibility for surveillance of liquidation and reorganization under Cabinet Order No. 291.

4. In view of the above, there is no objection to cancellation of the designation of CIT as a Closed Institution by issuing attached Memo to the JG amending SCAPIN 707.

5. This an administrative matter and surveillance by Civil Affairs Section is not required.

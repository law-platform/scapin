MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1846)

ESS/IN JZR/GEM/ECW/ms

9 January 1948

1. Reference is C.L.O. letter No. 9112 (CM), dated 25 November 1947, subject: Application for Permission to Construct Three Vessels and to Remodel Three Vessels, with one inclosure attached thereto.

2. The Japanese Government is asking permission to complete construction on two (2) 265 gross ton cargo vessels, one (1) 200 gross ton oil tanker, convert two (2) partially completed 70 gross ton tugs into 100 gross ton refrigerator vessels, and one (1) salvaged 376 gross ton soil carrier into a 570 ton cargo vessel.

3. The two (2) 265 gross ton cargo vessels referred to above were originally ordered for the Kure Naval Arsenal and are about 75% complete. The 200 gross ton oil tanker was originally ordered for the Japanese Navy and is about 90% completed. The two (2) 70 gross ton tug boats were originally ordered for Japanese naval arsenals and are about 70% completed. Construction on all of these vessels was suspended at the time of the Japanese surrender. It has been ascertained that these vessels have no military potential value and have been released for disposal to private companies. The Ministry of Transportation has designated new owners and now wishes to complete these vessels.

4. The 376 gross ton soil carrier referred to above was sunk by typhoon in September 1945. This vessel was salvaged and now the owners-wish to repair and convert her into a cargo vessel. The inspectors of the Japanese Maritime Bureau have passed her as being in satisfactory condition for conversion.

5. Materials for the completion and conversion for all the vessels as stated in paragraph 2, is on hand or in stock at the shipyards. "Abemaki" cork substitute is to be used for insulation in the two (2) refrigerator vessels. Fuel to operate the vessels was investigated and it was stated by the Ministry of Transportation that fuel would be allocated to the designated companies and they would, in turn, distribute the fuel to the most efficiently operating ships.

6. It was also pointed out by the Ministry of Transportation that these vessels will be completed for open sea operation and to withstand all weather conditions, as it is intended to use them along the outer coasts of Honshu and Hokkaido, moving food stuffs and general cargo out of the smaller ports. The converted soil carrier will be put in service moving general cargo between Osaka and Takamatsu. It is entended to put the oil tanker in service between Shimonoseki-Wakamatsu, Nagasaki and Kagoshima, fueling fishing vessels. The Ministry of Transportation further pointed out that as this vessel is already 90% completed and very heavily constructed, she can be finished to suit requirements with very little alteration and cost. It was also stated that the fishing fleets now have to make long runs from the fishing banks to procure fuel, but with the establishment of tanker service, these runs will be eliminated, thereby making a substantial saving in fuel and time.

7. Financing for each ship is being arranged between the shipyards and the assigned owners, with private banks. One concern is restricted and will come under the cognizance of Antitrust and Cartels, ESS, which office has been fully informed and will take necessary action when proper application is made.

8. Action taken: Memorandum is being forwarded to the Japanese Government approving application for permission to complete and convert miscellaneous vessels as stated and specified on inclosure attached thereto.

9. Concurrences: Natural Resources Section/Fisheries - Mr. Neville; Civil Transportation Section - Mr. Duane

10. Routine surveillance is required on this activity and will be reported in normal monthly Occupation Activities Reports.

11. The interests of USAFIK are not concerned.

12. Further questions may be directed to Mr. E. C. Wingo, ESS/IN, Shipyard Industries and Harbor Development Branch, Tel No. 26-8751.

J. Z. R.

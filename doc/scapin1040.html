MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Proposed Endowment from National Defense Contribution Fund to Establish an Independent Educational and Cultural Foundation.

1. Reference is made to Memorandum from the Imperial Japanese Government, CLO No. 2333 (EF), dated 15 May 1946, subject: “Endowment from National Defense Contribution to Establish an Independent Educational and Cultural Foundation (LO 520),” and attached memorandum, LO 520, from Ministry of Finance, dated 14 May 1946, same subject.

2. The proposal to appropriate ¥500,000,000 of the National Defense Contribution Fund for the endowment of an Independent Educational and Cultural Foundation is not approved.

3. The Imperial Japanese Government is directed to incorporate the remaining balances of the National Defense Contribution Fund into the general revenue accounts of the Government. Monies and securities transferred shall include, but are not limited to, all contributions made to the Army and Navy for scientific and technical research, servicemen’s welfare and national defense.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1040)

WKM/ATM/syl

25 June 1946

1. The Imperial Japanese Government has submitted a proposal to appropriate ¥500,000,000 of the National Defense Fund for the purpose of establishing and endowing an Independent Educational and Cultural Foundation. These funds were originally donated voluntarily by the Japanese people to the Army and Navy to supplement budgetary expenditures for armaments, war research and servicemen's relief and welfare. Due to the privation which the Japanese people are now experiencing as the direct result of the war, it is considered that the use of these funds for general relief and welfare would be far more appropriate and more in keeping with the present wishes of the Japanese public than use of the funds for an educational and cultural foundation. Further, it is recommended that the proposal be disapproved for the following additional reasons:

a. Basic plan previously has been disapproved. Memorandum of the Central Liaison Office dated 5 January 1946 (CLO No.43 (2.1) subject: "Endowments from the National Defense Contributions and Other Similar Contributions to Create an Institution for Scientific Research" submitted an application for approval of the Supreme Commander for the use of ¥500,000,000 of the fund to endow a Foundation for Scientific Research. Memorandum, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers to the Imperial Japanese Government File AG 120 (21 Jan 46) ESS/FI dated 21 January 1946, subject: "Disposition of Defense Contribution Fund," disapproved the request for the reason that public welfare had a much more urgent claim on all available public funds. Japanese Ministry of Finance officials admit that the present plan is the original proposal pressed up."

b. The leading individuals who are proposed to guide the Preparatory Committee of the foundation are undesirable persons.

(1) Keizo Shibusawa, former minister of Finance, came under the provision of the purge directive of 4 January 1946. In 1943 he was head of an Economic Policy Department and Economic Negotiations Department in the Bureau of Economic Warfare. According to informational data finished by CIS, Shibusawa is a representative of the more aggressive type of Japanese big business man and during the war he was intimately associated with the militarists.

(2) Shoji Arakawa presently President of the Yokohama Specie Bank and member of the board of the Bank of Japan, also came under the provision of the purge directive of 4 January 1946.

c. Even though the objectives of such a foundation may be desirable, if the initiation of the foundation and the nomination of members of the Board of Directors will be the responsibility of leaders, who were former advocates of militarism, it is hardly likely that the Allied Powers of the Japanese public will consider the foundation representative of new Japanese culture and thought. The real purpose of the Japanese Government in proposing such a plan at this time is subject to question in view of the fact that the Japanese Government knew for some months that Shibusawa and Arakawa were subject to the purge and now make a further effort to take care of them just when they are to be replaced. In view of the cultural and educational objectives of the proposed foundation, it would appear highly undesirable to staff the preparatory committee with any purged individuals or other persons suspected in any way.

d. Proposal is untimely.

(1) The principles governing present Japanese economy as indorsed by SCAP place primary emphasis on programs which increase or facilitate the production or distribution of basic necessities, notably food, clothing, fuel, and shelter. The Allied Council's recommendations stated that it would be desirable to have the balance of this fund devoted to relief and welfare. Even conceding that the proposed plan is a type of relief it does not meet the spirit of current relief principles.

(2) Materials are critical. Emphasis is presently being placed on programs using a minimum of materials and equipment that are in-short supply. Preference is given to plans that will produce commodities for consumption within the next year and ones that will directly contribute to economic recovery and physical reconstruction.

(3) Pending a more concrete determination of a basic educational and cultural policy by the Ministry of Educations such a high level independent foundation endowed by the Japanese Government appears to be premature.

(4) Press reaction would be unfavorable due to the nature of the fund, the leaders proposed, and present physical needs of the people.

2. It would appear that such an Independent Educational and Cultural Foundation as this would more appropriately be financed by current donations from public minded citizens rather than endowment from public funds. This would be more in keeping with the objectives contemplated by the foundation and would not occasion adverse criticism from the people.

3. The Imperial Japanese Government is directed to transfer the balance of the Defense Contribution Fund into the General Revenue Account so as to give the Ministry of Finance full Budgetary Control of the entire fund. Further this procedure is considered a necessary safeguard as it would require Diet approval on any contemplated use of the fund. Memorandum for the Imperial Japanese Government, LO 592, 27 May 1946, subject: "Present Amount of National Defense Contribution" made certain corrections in the amount of National Defense Contributions now under the control of the Ministry of Finance. Settlement of outstanding accounts, claims and partial accounting by field officials represented nature of present corrections and indicated that future changes would also be necessary.

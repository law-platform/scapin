MEMORANDUM FOR :Japanese Government
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo
SUBJECT :Alteration and Change in Construction Plans of Two (2) Combination Cargo-passenger Vessels

1. Reference is C.L.O. letter No. 8024 (CM), dated 15 October 1947, subject: Application for Approval of Changes in Construction Program Small-Sized Passenger Vessels.

2. No objection is offered to the change in construction of two (2) 2,000 gross ton ships, Hull Numbers 1402 and 1403, from combination cargo-passenger vessels into full freight ships. The construction of these vessels was authorized by Memorandum for the Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, AG 560 (30 October 1946) ESS/IN, (SCAPIN 1303), dated 30 October 1946, subject: Application for Permission to Construct Small Sized Passenger Vessels. These two ships are now under construction at the Mitsubishi Nagasaki Shipyard, Nagasaki, Japan:

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

/s/ M. J. Noyes
for /t/ R. M. LEVY,
Colonel, A.G.D.,
Adjutant General

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1808)

22 October 1947

1. Reference is C.L.O. Letter No. 8024 (CM), dated 15 October 1947, subject: Alteration and Change in Construction Plans of two (2) 2 000 gross ton combination cargo-passenger vessels.

2. Above reference requests permission to alter the design of hulls, Nos. 1402 and 1403 from combination cargo-passenger vessel into full freight ships. The subject vessels are now under construction at the Mitsubishi Nagasaki Shipyard.

3. Authorization for the construction of these ships was given on SCAPIN No. 1303, subject: Application for permission to Construct Small Sized Passenger Vessels, dated 30 October 1948. The orginal plans called for construction of accommodations for twenty (20) first class and one hundred and eighty (180) third class passengers. Later the plans were altered to increase the cargo space by eliminating the third class passenger quarters.

4. A study of the efficiency of the subject vessels, and the "Otaru-Keihin Route" on which they are designated to operate has been made. Considering the high construction cost, and the demand for cargo space, the assigned owners wish to further alter the plans to build the subject vessels into full freighters with passenger accommodations reduced to two (2).

5. Under the present plans the subject vessels would have a bale capacity of 2,300 cubic meters. Under the proposed plans recently submitted the bale capacity would be increased to 3,819 cubic meters.

6. The cruising radius would be reduced some owing to the fuel bunker capacity being reduced, but not enough to materially effect the operating efficiency.

7. The proposed change in plans does not increase the cost of construction or the amount of materials required for building. The construction of the subject vessels has not advanced beyond a point whereby an alteration in design would be impractical.

8. Action taken: By memorandum for the Japanese Government permission to alter design of hull, Nos. 1402 and 1403 is approved.

9. Concurrences: Civil Transportation Section - Mr. Duane.

10. The interests of USAFIK are not concerned.

11. Routine surveillance is required on this activity and will be reported in normal Monthly Occupation Activities Reports.

12. Further questions may be directed to Mr. E.C. Wingo, ESS/IN, Shipyard Industries and Harbor Development Branch, Tele. No. 26-6583.

J. Z. R.

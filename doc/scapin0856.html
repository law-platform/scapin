3 April 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Government Loan Guarantees and Borrowing by Government Instrumentalities.

1. Reference is made to the following memoranda of the Minister of Finance:

a. “Compulsory Loans for Payment of Wages, Retirement Allowance and Taxes, when Loans Cannot be Obtained by Ordinary Means” (LO 151), dated 17 January 1946, which requested approval for government guarantees of compulsory. bank loans to Industry for post-war activities, totalling ¥4,631,908,000.

b. “Issuing of Industrial Bonds under Government Guarantee to accommodate funds for the Restoration of Ammonium Sulphate Manufacturing Industry” (LO 243), dated 13 February 1946, which requested approval for government guarantee of a loan of ¥371,689,000 to thirteen fertilizer companies from the Industrial Bank of Japan.

c. “Request for approval of Issuing Debentures by Japan Electric Supply Company and Five other Corporations under Government Guarantee” (LO 251), dated 14 February 1946, which requested approval for government-guaranteed borrowing totalling ¥305,000,000 by six corporations, five of which are wholly or partially government-owned.

d. “Request for Approval of the Loan for Ship-building by Industrial Equipment Corporation” (LO 252), dated 14 February 1946, requesting approval for that agency, which is wholly government-owned to borrow ¥117,411,446 to finance its ship-building activities.

2. With regard to reference 1a, permission is granted for the issuance of Government guarantees for compulsory loan aggregating not more than ¥2,500,000,000 issued between 15 August 1945 and 24 November 1945.

3. Neither the Imperial Japanese Government nor any of its political subdivisions will make further guarantees of the obligations, of any nature whatsoever, of any corporations or agencies except political subdivisions of the Imperial Japanese Government and such financial institutions as may be specifically designated with the approval of General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers. Existing guarantees will not be renewed at the termination of the period for which they were granted.

4. Government financial assistance to any corporation, organization or agency other than a political subdivision or a financial institution specifically designated with the approval of General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, will henceforth be given only in the form of a subsidy. Such subsidies will be paid currently during the fiscal year as the need arises, rather than at the end of the year. The Imperial Japanese Government will not pay any subsidy which has not been duly authorized in the budget or equivalent financial measure for the year in which payment is to be made. The necessity for and extent of each subsidy will be determined at the time it is considered for inclusion in the budget or equivalent financial measure.

5. Government agencies and instrumentalities which are not part of the formal government structure of the Imperial Japanese Government or any of its political subdivisions, whether subsidized or not, may henceforth borrow from financial institutions without prior authorization from the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers. Their loans will not be guaranteed by the Imperial Japanese Government or any of its political subdivisions.

6. Reports submitted to General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers by the Bank of Japan and the Ministry of Finance differ widely as to the total amount of loans made by financial institutions with the guarantee of the Imperial Japanese Government. The Imperial Japanese Government is directed to submit within two week from receipt of this memorandum a description of the various types of loans which are considered to be guaranteed by the Imperial Japanese Government, together with the supporting evidence on which the claim for guarantee is based. Within one month after delivery of this data the Imperial Japanese Government will submit to General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, a statement of all guaranteed loans by types, held by each financial institution as of a currrent data. This statement will reflect the original amount of such loans and the unpaid balance as of the reporting date.

7. The requests made by LO 243,251 and 252 are returned for action in accordance with the provisions of this memorandum.

8. Direct communication between the Ministry of Finance and interested Staff Sections of General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers is hereby authorized to implement this memorandum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

B.M.FICHI
Brigadiar General,AGD,
Adjutant General

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 856)

WEM/CSH/rah

1. This memorandum prohibits future government guarantees of the obligations of any corporation, organization or agency except local governments and such financial institutions as may be designated with the approval of General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, and provides that existing guarantees will lapse with the expiration of the period for which granted As a partial substitute for the government support thus withdrawn it provides for the continuance of public fiscal assistance, where necessary, through the direct medium of a subsidy rather than indirectly by guarantee. Finally, it places those agencies which are not part of the formal structure of government but are to some degree owned or controlled by the government, on an equal basis with other corporations by withdrawing the government guarantee of their operations and permitting them to borrow without prior authorization from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

2. A large number of corporations and associations are at present underwritten by the government through guarantee of their debts, interest, dividends, or other obligations, sometimes in addition to a direct subsidy. The exact number is not known, pending submission of a complete report, but there are believed to be more than one hundred. Some are privately owned and managed, while others partake of the nature of government instrumentalities through government ownership of part or all their stock, or through other control devices such as government appointment of the principal officers. The finances of these corporations and agencies were brought under specific control of General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, by Memorandum, file AG 121.7 (24 Nov 45) ESS/FI, dated 24 November 1945, subject: "Elimination of War Profits and Reorganization of National Finances", which provided in part that no further government guarantees could be made without prior authorization from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, nor could government instrumentalities borrow without such permission. Only three requests for a government loan guarantee and a very few requests for borrowing by government instrumentalities were received from 24 November 1945 until 14 February 1946. On the latter date, however requests involving twenty different corporations were received, thus bringing the matter to a head and pointing up the need for an immediate expression of policy.

3. The present system of government guarantees is objectionable for two reasons:

a. It destroys the incentive for sound banking practices and efficient management of corporate finance. When the government guarantees a loan, there is no reason for the bank which lends the money to inquire closely into the business prospects or management practices of the borrowing corporation. Likewise a corporation which knows that its debts and dividends will be repaid, whether or not it is well managed and its operations are profitable, has no incentive to increase efficiency or to lowering of costs through increased production. A great many instances of loose banking practice and corporate inefficiency attributable to the existence of a government guarantee are matters of record in the files of General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

b. The system of government guarantees, by enabling an almost unlimited extension of national credit, also throws a tremendous potential strain on the government resources and constitutes a serious threat to national solvency. Furthermore, it negates the principle of democratic control of public finance through a budget subject to legislative approval, since, in those cases where the government is called upon to make good on its guarantee, that fact and the extent of the necessary payment are not known until the deficit has been incurred, leaving the budget-makers and the Diet confronted with an accomplished fact over which no control can be exercised. In actual practice, this type of ex post facto budgeting is performed by computing the deficit of the guaranteed agency at the end of the fiscal year, and including that figure as a subsidy in the next year's budget. The futility of legislative deliberation on such an item is apparent.

4. a. It is recognized that the action proposed in this memorandum may force an extensive revamping of ordinary banking practice and may necessitate the establishment of a special banking institution equipped to make loans to corporations or agencies performing essential functions but unable, because of extra risk or other factors, to utilize the resources of the ordinary banks. It is felt that these consequences, however drastic, are inevitable, desirable, and timely.

b. It is also recognized that the government is deeply involved in the financing of many private and quasi-public corporations and associations, some of which are performing important social functions, and that continued public aid to some of these enterprises may be necessary and desirable. Such aid, however, will in the future be provided only by direct government subsidy which has had prior legislative approval through the normal budgeting process, thus substituting a precise and measurable public obligation for the vague and unlimited assumption of liability characteristic of the present guarantee system.

c. Abolition of the guarantee system removes the need for General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers directly to control borrowing by government instrumentalities. The possible detrimental effect of such borrowing lay in the extent to which such agencies were not self-supporting and their deficits had to be financed by government subsides which added to the general government deficit. This factor can now be controlled directly by prior approval of each subsidy when it is submitted as an item in the budget in advance of its actual payment.

5. Memorandum from General Headquarters. Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, dated 17 December 1945, File 018.2(17 Dec 45) ESS/FI, subject, "Approval of Bank Loans by order of an Under Guarantee of the Government", states that General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers has no objection to the guarantee of bank loans by the Japanese Government prior to 1 November 1945, but withholds formal approval until full information concerning these loans has been submitted. Reference 1 a submits such information and further requests the extension of our approval to 24 November 1945. There can be no objection to granting this request since the loans were already approved and in various stages of disbursement previous to the issuance of the directive of 24 November 1945, cited in paragraph 2 above.

6. The Ministry of Finance has issued its instructions for compulsory loans on a blanket order basis, and it has not required submission of reports on loans of each type actually made by the banks to whom the instructions were issued. There exists considerable doubt, therefore, as to the amount of loans so guaranteed. The Ministry of Finance has variously estimated it at 4,854,000,000 yen and 7,354,000,000 yen while the Bank of Japan based on reports secured from the Big Five, estimates the total to exceed 30 billion yen. From the standpoint of the Government and the financial institutions it is essential that an agreement be reached on the total amount of guaranteed loans. It is a prerequisite to any plans that may be proposed by the Japanese Government for a reorganization of the banking system or for strengthening the national finances.

7. Concurrences: None required.

8. This is a policy directive but no surveillance by the army is required.

C. F. T.

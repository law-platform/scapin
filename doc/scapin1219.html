MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Responsibilities of Imperial Japanese Government for Preservation and Care of Plants’ Equipment and Records Taken Into Custody for Reparations Purposes by the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

1. Reference is made to memorandum for the Imperial Japanese Government from Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers dated 28 May 1946 (SCAPIN-987), subject: “Custody, Control and Protective Maintenance of Japanese Aircraft Plants, Arsenals and Laboratories,” together with inclosures and annexes thereto subsequently issued by the Supreme Commander, and to memoranda dated 13 August 1946 (SCAPIN-1029, 1030, 1031, 1032, 1033, 1034, 1035 and 1036).

2. The Imperial Japanese Government will be responsible for the servicing and repair of machinery, equipment and accessories, including auxiliary equipment, at each plant listed in custodial directives to the Imperial Japanese Government cited above to the extent that such servicing and repair is possible without the substantial purchase of replacement parts.

3. The servicing and repair referred to in paragraph 2, above, will include the inspection and testing of existing mechanical equipment and the assembly of accessories, without extensive replacement purchases, with a view to putting each item of equipment in good operating condition and facilitating its ready use when moved to another site. Basically the necessary servicing and repair should be performed by competent mechanics associated with the particular plant and should use only materials, parts, etc., available within the plant or readily available elsewhere. Care should be taken to retain an adequately skilled labor or maintenance force to avoid damage by personnel unfamiliar with equipment.

4. The Imperial Japanese Government will be responsible for the assembly and maintenance in good condition at each plant of such data, amplified below, as are domestically obtainable or as can be prepared in the respective establishments designated in custodial directives cited in paragraph 1. Instruction for preparation of technical data necessary for reparations transfers and not obtainable as specified will be given at a later date.

5. The informational data referred to in paragraph 4, above, will in general include:

a. Plant laycut.

b. Engineering data, including working drawings, blueprints and instruction booklets relating to the installation, operation, maintenance and disassembly of all pieces of plant equipment.

c. A record of major production difficulties encountered with equipment and the means of solution.

d. Other data that may be useful in the dismantling, moving, re-erection and operation of the facility.

6. Special Instruction for Industrial Categories: In addition to the general requirements set forth in the two preceding paragraphs, the following types of data are to be assembled and held available:

a. Ball and Roller Bearing Plants: Engineering data, speciffications and blueprints of types and sizes of bearings produced, chemical and physical specifications of special steels used.

b. Thermal Electric Power Plants:

(1) Records pertaining to construction, such as foundation plans, arrangement drawings, and detailed drawings of equipment.

(2) Facilities for which servicing is to be performed and data maintained include not only equipment installed in buildings but also yard equipment such as coal handling facilities, outdoor switchgear and transformers: also special instruments and tools required for operation and maintenance.

c. Chemical Industries (Sulfuric Acid, Soda Ash, Chlorine and Caustic Soda):

(1) Plant flow diagrams in block and line form showing: Operations, schematic equipment arragements, elevations, all rates and directions of flow, temperatures, pressures, material balances, and recording points.

(2) Detailed operating procedure of plant, giving explicit instrutions on start-up and shut down, including control methods and performance curves of equipment.

(3) Technical and safety data on storage and handling of products.

(4) Existing records showing consumption and production of principal materials shall be preserved.

d. Other industries: For other industries covered by custody directives cited, no special instructions, additional to paragraphs 4 and 5, above, are deemed necessary. This applies to:

(1) Aircraft. (2) Government Arsenals.

(3) Scientific Laboratories. (4) Machine Tools.

(5) Privately-Owned Munitions. (6) Shipyards.

(7) Iron and Steel.

7. Within seventy-two (72) hours of the receipt of this memorandum the Imperial Japanese Government will have qualified representatives report to the Commanding General, Eighth United States Army to receive detailed instructions to accomplish the purpose of this memorandum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1219)

ESS/IN JZR/NJM/aeb

29 August 1946

1. a. The first directive relating to preservation of industrial facilities and records was SCAP Directive No.3, 22 Sept. 45 (SCAPIN-47), of which paragraph 5 listed a number of industries and in general any firm "Which contributed to the Japanese War effort" for which plants, equipment, records and documents were to be maintained in good condition.

b. SCAP letter to Commanding General Eighth Army and COMNAVJAP, AG 004 (13 Jan 46) ESS/IN/GD, 13 January 1946, amplified the above policy and fixed responsibility with respect to physical facilities of the aircraft industry, government arsenals and laboratories. SCAP Memo to the Imperial Japanese Government (SCAPIN 629), 20 January 1946 conveyed instructions of similar purpose to the Imperial Japanese Government. Subsequent revisions have been made to these directives without change in scope.

2. The issuance of custodial directives for eight more industries listing plants designated for reparations has created the need for a directive broadening the industries covered by such instructions. These industries, and their SCAPIN numbers (to IJG), are: Soda Ash and Caustic Soda (1129), Iron and Steel (1130), Steam Electric-Power Generation (1131), Sulphuric Acid (1132), Machine tools (1133), Privately Owned Munitions Plants (1134), Shipbuilding (1135), Precision Bearings (1136). They are covered at present, as regards maintenance (oiling and greasing), by short Paragraphs in the "Information of General Application" letters accompanying the command letters, but not directive is given to preserve their records.

3. a. The attached directive to the IJG:

(1) Broadens the concept of "preservation by oiling and greasing" to one of maintenance and repair to such an extent as is possible "without substantial purchase of replacement parts."

(2) Requires the preservation and assembly of such specified engineering data, domestically obtainable, as are necessary to the removal, reerection and operation of the plant.

b. Command letter to Commanding General, United States Eighth Army and COMNAVJAP directs such surveillance as is necessary to assure compliance with the Memorandum to IJG.

4. Concurrences: CPC - H. A. B.

J. Z. R.

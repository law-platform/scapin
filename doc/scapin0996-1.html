APO 500

12 July 1949

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
SUBJECT :Illegal Import and Export Trade-Disposition of Critical Materials and Rationed Goods.

1. References are:

a. Memorandum for the Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, AG 091.31 (4 June 46) ESS/PC, SCAPIN 996, dated 4 June 1946, subject: Illegal Import and Export Trade.

b. Joint Ministerial Ordinance No. 2, subject: Concerning the Utilization of Surplus Materials, dated 23 March 1948.

c. Joint Ministerial Ordinance No. 65, subject: Regulation Concerning the Urgent Investigation of Inventories of Critical Materials, dated 27 March 1948.

2. This memorandum modifies reference 1a in so far as it applies to goods, wares and merchandise heretofore or hereafter seized which are subject to reference 1b and 1c.

3. The Japanese Government will forthwith place all such goods, wares and merchandise in regular channels of distribution and distribute them in accordance with applicable allocation and rationing procedures, provided that the money equivalent of such goods shall be subject to judicial determination in place of the items.

4. Except as modified in the preceding paragraph, all provisions of reference 1a shall continue to apply to these and all other goods.

5. Direct communication between the appropriate agencies of the Japanese Government and General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, Economic aud Scientific Section, is authorized to implement the provisions of this memorandum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY
Colonel, AGD
Asst Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 996/1)

FLW/RG/JBR/mel

23 June 1949

1. References are:

a. Memorandum for the Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, AG 091.31 (4 June 1946) ESS/PC, SCAPIN 996, dated 4 June 1946, subject: Illegal Import and Export Trade.

b. Joint Ministerial Ordinance No. 2, subject: Concerning the Utilization of Surplus Materials, dated 23 March 1948.

c. Joint Ministerial Ordinance No. 65, subject: Regulation Concerning the Urgent Investigation of Inventories of Critical Materials, dated 27 March 1948.

2. Reference 1a above directs the Japanese Government to take all necessary steps to stop illicit import and export trade and to seize and immerchandise except foodstuffs, which are in danger of spoilage, pending determination of judicial action affecting the property". Said reference further provides that upon judicial determination such goods are the subject of illicit import and export trade, they shall be confiscated and consumer goods placed in regular channels of distribution, confiscated money, precious stones, etc., turned over to the Bank of Japan for the account of the Japanese Government, other items and durable goods, such as machinery and heavy equipment, reported to General Headquarters for instructions as to disposition.

3. Reference 1b above provides procedure for the inventory, collection and redistribution of surplus and illegally held critical materials. In general this ordinance directs that such critical materials shall be transferred to the competent kodan for redistribution through legal channels.

4. Reference 1c was issued pursuant to AG 401.1 (21 February 48) ESS, (SCAPIN-1863), and provides for inventory, collection and redistribution of critical materials. Reference 1c further provides that materials which are subject to confiscation shall be seized and promptly turned over to the competent distribution agency without waiting for court decision for disposal and the price of them preserved to be returned to the owner in case of wrongful confiscation.

5. Reference 1a was issued 4 June 1946 prior to the establishment of formalized procedures for collection and redistribution of critical materials through the Industrial Reconstruction Kodan and other governmental agencies.

This directive requires the Japanese Government to hold all goods pending final judicial decision as to their disposition. This no longer offers a satisfactory method for handing such goods. Due to the large number of all types of cases pending before the courts and unavoidable delays in prosecutions and appeals, a substantial amount of critical materials have accumulated in the possession of the Customs Division of the Finance Ministry and the procurators office and have not been promptly returned for utilization in the Japanese economy.

6. The Office of the Attorney General and the Customs Division of the Finance Ministry have requested that remedial action be taken to the end that goods seized for the violation of import and export regulations be handled in the same manner as goods seized for violation of other economic laws such as reference 1b and 1c above. Such goods being impounded for periods up to two years suffer substantial deterioration and incur large custody and warehousing costs. The net income to the Japanese Government is greatly reduced and the goods made less desirable or even useless to industry.

7. The proposed memorandum will remedy this situation and comply with the request of Japanese authorities. Furthermore, no reason is apparent for a method of collecting and disposing of such critical materials, different from other cases. It will also avoid substantial costs of custody and warehousing and deterioration in the value of the goods and return them promptly to legitimate channels of distribution for utilization in the Japanese economy.

8. Surveillance by Eighth Army is unnecessary.

9. Concurrences: G-4

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 678 (30 Nov 46) ESS/IND

(SCAPIN-1920)

APO 500

9 July 1948

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
SUBJECT :Petroleum Storage Tank Facilities,

1. The Japanese Government is hereby directed to take such action as necessary to prevent construction, major repair, rehabilitation, dismantling, movement, or scrapping of any petroleum storage tank facilities in excess of 50 kiloliters capacity and their accessory equipment without prior approval of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

2. This directive does not modify or supersede Memorandum for the Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, file AG 600. 3 (30 Nov 46) CPC, SCAPIN 1370, dated 30 November 1946, subject: Presevation of Property in Japan Owned by United Nations and their Nationals,

3. It is further directed that a report be submitted to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers providing the following information pertaining to storage tank capacity,

a. Location.

b. Ownership.

c. User.

d. Number and capacity of tanks.

e. Purposes for which used.

f. Type of tank.

g. Present condition.

4. The required report will be submitted prior to 1 September 1948.

5. Direct communication between the Economic and Scientific Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, and the Japanese Government is authorized.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

(See reverse for Memo Info of General Application)

AG 678 (30 Nov 46) ESS/IND MEMORANDUM

APO 500

9 July 1948

SUBJECT Information of General Application Pertaining to SCAPIN 1920, file AG 678 (30 Nov 48) ESS/IND, dated 9 July 1948, subject: Petroleum Storage Tank Facilities

1. With reference to the above Memorandum to the Japanese Government, the following clarification and interpretation is published for the information of all concerned.

2. The purpose in issuing subject directive to the Japanese Government is to insure availability of excess petroleum storage tanks and their associated equipment for reparations removals or for scrapping as deemed necessary.

3. It is considered that repairs or rehabilitation to existing facilities and construction of storage tanks are not necessary by reason of excess storage capacity now available. It is desired that a system be established for periodically spot checking all storage tank facilities in excess of 50 kiloliters capacity and their associated equipment. In case of willful violation or neglect, report shoud be made through normal channels to General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

4. This directive does not modify or supersede Memorandum for the Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, file AG 600. 3 (30 Nov 46) CPC, SCAPIN 1370, dated 30 November 1946, subject: Preservation of Property in Japan Owned by United Nations and their Nationals. The Japanese Government is requested to include information pertaining to properties of United Nations and their Nationals as requested in paragraph 3, subject directive.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1920)

WSV/WT/HBO/AC/dge

1 July 1948

1. Proposed directive to the Japanese Government relative to construction, repairing, removing, dismantling or scrapping of petroleum storage tanks and their accessory equipment.

2. The action is based on the following:

a. Memorandum for the Japanese Government from GHQ, SCAP, file AG 463. 7 (21 Jan 46) GS, SCAPIN 640, dated 21 January 1946, sbuject: Crude Oil Imports, prohibits the importation of crude oil. Although the recommendations have been made to Washington to retain a refining capacity of 40,000 barrels a day, the importation of crude oil to Japan is not expected in the near future.

b. Memo for the JG from GHQ, SCAP, file AG 600. 3 (30 Nov 46) CPC, dated 30 November 1946, SCAPIN 1370, subject: Preservation of property in Japan Owned by United Nations and their Nationals, directs the Japanese Government to make minimum outlays as necessary to preserve and protect property in Japan owned on 7 December 1941 by Nationals of United Nations, so as to prevent further loss or deterioration.

c. Repair or rehabilitation of existing facilities is not necessary since existing storage capacity is sufficient for present requirements.

d. SCAP has recommended to Washington that ten (10) million barrels of storage capacity be retained in Japan (radio C-68626). This storage capacity provides more than a four months reserve of petroleum products based on annual future consumption of 18 million barrels.

e. Action taken will prevent the use of materials and transportation critically needed for direct use and rehabilitation of other industries.

f. In order to make oil storage available for reparations it is necessary to maintain present storage capacity at its present level. Washington states it may be possible to scale down other reparations if oil storage can be made available.

g. Information requested is necessary to augment and verify that previously obtained, so as to determine the excess to be made available for reparations.

3. Routine surveillance to insure compliance is requested of 8th Army, & Naval forces, Far East.

4. The interests of USAFIK are not concerned.

5. Concurrences: G-4 Petroleum (Col. Cruse); NRS, Petroleum, (Mr. Stach); REP, (Maj. Nicholas)

A. Chang - 26-8504

W. S. V.

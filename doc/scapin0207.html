GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 248 (29 Oct 45) ESS-FI

(SCAPIN - 207)

29 October 1945

MEMORANDUM FOR :Imperial Japanese Government
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo
SUBJECT :Payment of Savings and Allotments in Korea of Korean Laborers in Japanese Coal Mines.

1. It is the desire of this Headquarters to permit Korean nationals working in Japanese coal mines to remit savings from their wages to Korea. Moreover, this Headquarters desires to permit the resumption of payment of allotments to their families.

2. You are directed to make arrangements immediately for the periodic collection of the savings and allotments desired to be remitted to Korea and for their deposit into a special account with the Bank of Japan.

3. You will furnish to this Headquarters statements in quadruplicate setting forth the names and addresses of the laborers, the amount desired to be remitted on behalf of each laborer, and the name and address of the beneficiary in Korea. These reports will be submitted monthly.

4. You will notify immediately by telegraph all mining companies employing such Korean laborers, of these arrangements.

5. An acknowledgment of the receipt of this memorandum is directed.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

/s/ H. W. Allen
/t/ H. W. ALLEN,
Colonel, A.G.D.,
Asst. Adjutant General

MEMORANDUM : (SCAPIN - 207)

APO 500

29 October 1945

TO : Chief of Staff

I. PROBLEM:

Transfers of Pay and Allotments of Korean Laborers in Hokkaido Coal Mines

II. FACTS:

1. These laborers have been accustomed to save part of their pay and remit it to Korea.

2. The Japanese had an arrangement by which the families of these laborers in Korea were paid certain periodic allowances.

3. The regulations directed to be imposed by this Headquarters prevent such remittances and payments.

4. Dissatisfaction of the Korean laborers at not being able to continue these remittances is causing a decline in coal production in Hokkaido.

5. These arrangements are designed as temporary measures only and instructions will be given to the army commanders that they are not to be permitted to delay the repatriation of Koreans.

III. RECOMMENDATIONS:

1. That arrangements be made to permit any savings which these laborers make from their pay to be remitted to Korea and converted to Korean currency.

2. That arrangements be made to continue payment of the allotments to their families in Korea in Korean currency.

3. That the yen received in Japan for such savings be paid into a special account in the Bank of Japan for later disposition.

4. That the amounts disbursed in Korea be deducted from the proceeds of coal shipments to the Korean Military Government.

5. That the attached Memorandum to the Japanese Government be signed and dispatched.

R. C. KRAMER,

Colonel, G.S.C.,

Chief, Econ & Sc Section

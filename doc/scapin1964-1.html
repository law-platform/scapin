GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 311.3 (30 Aug 48) CCS

(SCAPIN - 1964/1)

APO 500

18 April 1949

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
SUBJECT :Regulations Covering the Provision of Telephone Services in Japan for Certain Nationals of the United Nations.

1. Reference is made to the following.

a. Memorandum for the Japanese Government, file AG 386.3 (22 Apr 48) CPC/FP, SCAPIN 1880, dated 22 April 1948, subject: Procedures for Restoration of Property in Japan to Nationals of the United Nations.

b. Memorandum for the Japanese Government, file AG 676.3 (30 Aug 48) CCS, SCAPIN 1964, dated 17 January 1949, subject: Instructions Pertaining to Treatment of Telephone Rights and Prohibition of Resale of Public Telecommunications Services.

2. Effective immediately, the Japanese Government is directed to take such action as may be required, in accordance with the provisions and regulations of Inclosure 1 of this directive to provide service and restore telephone subscriptions upon demand to those nationals of the United Nations who were conducting business and/or were living in Japan prior to 7 December 1941, whoso telephone equipment and subscriptions were confiscated and sold by the Japanese Government.

3. Direct communication between the Civil Communications Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers and the Ministry of Communications and/or the Ministry of Finance regarding matters within the scope of this memorandum is authorized.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

REGULATIONS COVERING THE PROVISION OF TELEPHONE SERVICES IN JAPAN TO CERTAIN NATIONALS OF THE UNITED NATIONS

1. In connection with the prosecution of the war against the Allied Powers, the Japanese Government took over and disposed of certain telephone services, equipment and facilities which were or had been serving foreign individuals and organizations who conducted business and/or lived in Japan prior to and during the war and who were or had been subscribing for telephone service. These individuals and organizations involved had property "rights" in the telephone lines for which they had subscribed. In some cases, these lines were terminated at their premises in telephone apparatus and equipment which are described in the regulations of the Ministry of Communications as Class A and Class D telephone systems. The Class A telephone systems consisted of subscriber-owned (a) private branch exchange switchboards and/or switching equipment, and associated apparatus, (b) private branch exchange telephone stations distributed throughout the subscribers premises, and (c) telephone wires and cables connecting the said PBX telephone stations with the PBX switchboard and/or switching equipment. The Class B systems consisted if subscriber-owned (a) switch-key devices, (b) one or more telephone extension stations distributed throughout the subscribers premises and (c) wires and cables connecting the said extension telephones with the switch-key devices, the operation of which permitted telephone calls to be sent or received by the extension stations over the central office trunk lines.

2. Many of the individuals and organizations described in paragraph 1 above, have returned (or will be returning) to Japan to resume activities and business operations and will probably be looking to the Japanese Government to immediately make available to them the telephone services which they enjoyed prior to the outbreak of war. Presently, the Japanese Government is restoring the trunk line services to such individuals and organizations, but it is not restoring any associated apparatus and equipment which was privately owned by these subscribers and connected for Class A and Class D services except where the individual and/or organization involved can locate and identify the associated apparatus and equipment which he formerly possessed. For each trunk line restored, the Japanese Government is supplying one telephone set which normally goes with a trunk line. In those cases where the associated apparatus and equipment can be located and identified, the service is being restored by the Japanese Government under the same arrangements between the Ministry of Communications and the subscriber as obtained before the war.

3. It is directed that for the fiscal year beginning 1 April 1949, and for subsequent fiscal years, the Japanese Government shall make suitable arrangements to permit the Ministry of Communications to install for each of the certain class of subscribers described under paragraph 1 above (a) trunk lines (b) private branch exchange switching systems and (c) extension station systems of the same scope and capacity for rendering telephone service as the system which was taken over from the foreign individual or organization and disposed of by the Japanese Government.

4. In connection with the installations described in paragraph 3, it is directed that the Ministry of Communications install the same number of central office lines and the same number of telephone stations as were previously subscribed for by the subscriber, (if the same amount of service is necessary or required). It is not necessary, however, that previously hold numbers be assigned to the restored service. If the line numbers taken over were sequence numbers and equipped for rotary operation if the subscriber involved can elect to have installed a private branch exchange system of smaller size and capacity than he had before if in his opinion and in the opinion of the Ministry of Communications the smaller board will meet his needs for service for a three year period subsequent to the time the installation is effected. It is also directed that the Ministry of Communications can elect to install a private branch exchange system of larger size and capacity than that of the previous system if it finds that the subscriber involved will require immediately or within a three year period from installation, a switchboard of larger size and capacity, In cases where a Class B system was taken over and disposed of by the Japanese Government, it is directed that the Ministry of Communications install switch-key devices of similar type and operation.

5. Any private branch exchange switchboard and/or switching equipment and switch-key devices installed in accordance with paragraph 3 and 4 above, will be the property of and will be maintained by the Ministry of Communications, and will not come under the ownership or maintenance of the subscriber. The subscriber for whom such services are furnished will have "property rights" existing and recognized prior to 15 February 1949, in each of the central office telephone lines restored to him totaling up to the number of such lines which were taken over by the Japanese Government and disposed of. This property right or "telephone right" will not include the previously hold telephone numbers, if such numbers are now assigned to others and in use.

6. Regularly established rates and regulations governing the provision of private branch exchange system services and extension station system services by the Ministry of Communications(under the conditions of its owning and maintaining the facilities used in connection with these systems) will be applicable to the services.

7. The full restoral of central office telephone lines in accordance with this directive will fulfill the Japanese Government's obligation in this respect, but since the private branch exchange switchboards and/or switching equipment and switch key devices and telephone stations installed by the Ministry of Communications under the provisions of this directive will be the property of the Ministry of Communications. Such installation will not abrogate or waive the right of any such subscriber to file a claim against the Japanese Government for property losses sustained in connection with those telephone facilities previously owned by the subscriber and used by him for securing Class A/ or Class B telephone services (exclusive of the central office telephone lines connected to his systems).

8. Nothing contained in the directive will have any bearing on or abrogate or eliminate the claims against the Japanese Government of any foreign individual or organization whose telephone services were taken over and disposed of by the Japanese Government subsequent to 6 December 1941, who presently is in Japan or elsewhere, and who does not elect to secure telephone services under the provisions of this directive.

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS

SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

APO 500

18 April 1949

MEMORANDUM

SUBJECT: Information of General Application Pertaining to Directive SCAPIN 1964/1, file AG 311.3 (30 Aug 48)CCS, this Headquarters, dated 18 April 1949, subject: Regulations Covering Provision of Telephone Service in Japan to Certain Nationals of the United Nations

1. With reference to the subject memorandum the following is published for the information of all concerned.

2. The subject memorandum is issued for the purpose of enabling nationals of the United Nations who conducted business and/or lived in Japan prior to and during the war whose telephone equipment and subscriptions were confiscated and sold by the Japanese Government to secure telephone services and have telephone subscriptions restored without the purchase of telephone bonds.

3. The Individuals and organizations coming under the description contained in paragraph 2 above had property rights in the telephone lines which they subscribed for, and these lines were terminated at their premises in some cases in telephone instruments, in some cases in Class A systems (private branch exchange switchboards and associated apparatus owned by the subscriber) and in other cases in Class B systems (extension station systems made up of a switch-key device and extension stations owned by the subscriber).

4. Under present controlling regulations, the Japanese Government will restore upon order from SCAP telephone lines and one instrument with each line, but will not restore any other telephone equipment and apparatus to the parties whose property was confiscated and sold by it unless the parties can locate and identify the actual equipment and apparatus they formerly possessed. In most cases this is impossible. Under the subject directive the Japanese Government will permit the Ministry of Communications to include in its annual budgets a sufficient amount of money to finance the purchase of necessary materials to affect installation of services for such of these subscribers as desire reinstallation of service under the provisions of the directive.

5. These subscribers will pay the established recurring charges for any restored lines, but will not be required to pay any registration or installation charges for the restored lines. The subscriber will have subscription property rights in the restored lines. Subscriber will pay regularly established installation charges for private branch exchange systems and extension station systems and the regularly established systems and extension station systems and the regularly established monthly recurring charges and the Ministry of Communications will furnish, own and maintain the facilities involved in rendering these services.

6. Reinstallation of services which are made under the provisions of the subject directive will not abrogate or waive the rights of the subscribers to file claims against the Japanese Government for property losses sustained in connection with those telephone facilities previously owned by them and used by them for securing private branch exchange and/ or extension station systems services (exclusive of the central office lines which have been connected to their systems).

A J REHE

Major, AGD

Asst Adj Gen

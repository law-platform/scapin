GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 411.5 (11 Feb 46) ESS/AC

(SCAPIN-728)

APO 500

11 February 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Dissolution of Iron and Steel Control Association.

1. Reference is made to memorandum C. L. O. No. 85 (2.3) subject: “Dissolution of Iron and Steel Control Association,” dated 8 January 1946, together with attached documents. The aforementioned memorandum requests authorization for the establishment of the Japan Iron and Steel Council.

2. This Headquarters has no objection at this time to the formation of the Iron and Steel Council and the dissolution of the Iron and Steel Control Association, providing, however, that such actions do not interfere with the production of essential consumer’s goods as previously directed.

3. A complete report of all actions taken by the Imperial Japanese Government in connection with the above matter shall be submitted to this Headquarters within five (5) days after such actions are taken. Six (6) copies in English of all ordinances and regulations repealed or modified by such actions shall be furnished this Headquarters. Pursuant to such actions, the Imperial Japnese Government shall insure the preservation and safety of the full and complete records of the Iron and Steel Control Association and affiliated organizations.

4. This memorandum shall not be construed by the Japanese Government as a directive to dissolve control associations generally, or repeal general legislation, ordinances or regulations affecting Control Associations in other than the Iron and Steel Industry.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

B.M.FICHI
Brigadiar General,AGD,
Adjutant General

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN-728)

31 January 1946

1. Acting upon the advice of the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, the members of the iron and steel industry have held several voluntary meetings for the purpose of reorganizing the controls within the industry in anticipation of the dissolution of the Iron and Steel Control Association. As a result of these meetings, and conferences with this office, a plan proposed new organization called "The Iron and Steel Council" has been submitted to the Japanese Government. By C.L.O. No. 85 (2.3) dated 8 January 1946, a copy of the plan and related documents was passed to Headquarters with a request for approval.

2. Several conferences with government officials and members of the industry have stressed the need for some strong agency to dominate the iron and steel industry with power to:

a. Adjust supply and demand

b. Stabilize prices

c. Secure and control or allocate the raw materials

d. Plan for and direct the industry generally in relation to the present economic necessity. One of the considerations stressed by members of the proposed Council and the government officials is that it is psychologically necessary to rid the industry of its stigma as a "war industry." It is thought that one of the most effective ways of doing this would be to dissolve the Control Association which was created by the government for the purpose of consolidating the industry during the war. At the same time, it is also realized that it is necessary to retain the same kind of controls for the sake of efficiency in production and the conservation of the extremely limited supplies of raw materials. In this connection, it is pointed out that the Pauley Commission report has placed a pall over operations within the industry and that the Control Association mechanism is now almost completely inoperative.

3. Consistent with the policy of this office, and in compliance with the Joint Chief of Staff directive, the plan as submitted provides for reducing the government control over the iron and steel industry to a necessary minimum after dissolution of the Control Association. The Iron and Steel Council, by exercising the public functions now exercised by the Control Association, will assume the nature of a public agency. It is realized, of course, that The Iron and Steel Council is a tempory measure only to accomplish the dual purpose of dissolving the Control Association and yet retaining necessary controls over industry. Further liberalization of the industry is anticipated when the economic situation becomes less acute. Eventually, it is contemplated that this Council itself will be abolished in favor of a more democratic method.

4. To facilitate the formation of the Iron and Steel Council and to provide for the orderly dissolution of the Control Association, the attached memorandum to the Imperial Japanese Government has been prepared.

5. Concurrences have been received from Legal and Industry Divisions.

S. W. W.

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

APO 500

12 September 1947

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
SUBJECT :Reduction of Governmental Expenditures.

1. Effective immediately the following steps will be taken to reduce governmental expenditures:

a. Only prices legally established by the Japanese Government for any product will be recognized, approved, or used in:

(1) Computing and establishing legal prices for any other product.

(2) Determining and making payments for goods, work done, or services rendered whether paid directly by the Japanese Government or indirectly as reimbursements or payments to contractors or manufacturers,

b. Legal prices for materials used in projects or activities demanded by the Occupation Forces will be the same as for similar materials used in other projects or activities.

c. Expenditures, payments and reimbursements by the Japanese Government on account of labor costs, whether the labor be employed directly by the Japanese Government or engaged by contractors, manufacturers, private companies, or public agencies in the accomplishment of any project, goods, or services for the Japanese Government or the Occupation Forces, shall not exceed in amount the actual wage payments directly received by the workers, plus prior deductions authorized by law.

d. In the following categories of labor, actual wage payments shall, as a rule, equal, and in no case shall exceed, prevailing wages for comparable employment in the locality as determined by the Japanese Government in accordance with methods which shall be subject to the review of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers:

(1) Labor employed by the Japanese Government in response to “Labor Requisitions” by the Occupation Forces.

(2) Labor engaged as the result of “Procurement Demands” by the Occupation Forces or Memorandums to the Japanese Government for the accomplishment of construction, building maintenance and associated services, and operation of Occupation Forces installations. Wage rates in this category shall not exceed those in category (1).

(3) Labor engaged in the accomplishment of public-works projects financed in whole or in part by the Japanese Government.

e. Adequate inspection and supervisory systems will be established over all activities involving expenditures by the Japanese Government to eliminate waste of labor or materials and to assure against the payment of padded or false cost claims.

2. Direct communication between the Ministry of Finance and interested Staff Sections of General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, is hereby authorized to implement this Memorandum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMORANDUM FOR: Chief of Staff (SCAPIN - 1775)

SUBJECT: Reduction of Japanese Government Expenditures

WFM/WKL/EMR/AMM/dc

19 June 1947

1. Attached staff study discusses the necessity of eliminating blackmarket purchases and excessive wage payments by the Japanese Government and proposes a directive to accomplish this objective.

2. The total deficit of the Japanese Government in the 1947-48 fiscal year (1 April 1947-31 March 1948) will amount to an estimated 100-200 billion yen unless immediate action is taken to reduce expenditures and increase revenues. New currency necessary to finance such a deficit would double the currency presently in circulation thereby greatly enhancing the inflation and nullifying any attempt to stabilize the economy.

3. An estimated saving of \23,500,000,000 (\9,500,000,000 for Occupation construction projects and \14,000,000,000 for other Japanese Government activities) would result from requiring the Japanese Government to limit expenditures to legally established prices and prevailing wages. Further, no stabilization can be hoped for unless the Japanese Government discontinues its present widespread blackmarket procurement activities and implements its control and distribution machinery whereby optimum distribution of the available materials can be made to essential users.

4. The condition of the Japanese economy is of direct concern to SCAP since it directly affects the cost of Occupation; namely, the volume of imports necessary to prevent disease and unrest, the ability of Japan to export goods to pay for such imports, the number of troops necessary to insure order, and the length of Occupation. Further, the JCS directive to SCAP states that necessary steps be taken to prevent servious inflation.

5. Previous directives have mentioned these malpractices. No directive, however, has required the Japanese Government to pay only legal prices and prevailing wages. Since the Japanese Government has been negligent in controlling inflation, it is recommended that SCAP take positive action.

W. F. Marquat

Brigadier Gen., U.S. Army

Chief, ESS

MEMORANDUM FOR: Chief of Staff (SCAPIN - 1775)

SUBJECT Reduction of Japanese Governmental Expenditures

WFM/WKL/EMR/AMM/dc

19 June 1947

THE PROBLEM

1. Blackmarket purchases and excessive wage payments by the Japanese Government are estimated to result in unnecessary expenditures of about \9,500,000,000 for Occupation construction projects and \14,000,000,000 for other Japanese Government activities during the remainder of 1947-48 fiscal year at the prevailing ratio of such payments to legal price payments. These excessive expenditures will have a very serious inflationary effect on the economy.

FACTS BEARING ON THE PROBLEM

2. The total deficit of the Japanese Government in the 1947-48 fiscal year (1 April 47-31 March 48) will amount to an estimated 100-120 billion yen unless immediate action is taken to reduce expenditures and increase revenues. Such a deficit would double the amount of currency presently in circulation thereby greatly enhancing the inflation and nullifying any attempt to stabilize the economy.

3. The saving of about \23,500,000,000 this fiscal year resulting from the elimination of blackmarket purchases and excessive wage payments is most necessary in any attempt to minimize this deficit.

4. Blackmarket purchases by the Japanese Government are inconsistent with any attempt to establish a control over the allocation and distribution of critical materials to essential users. An effective control of this nature is vital to the continued operation of the economy.

5. The Japanese Government has admitted such payments in many instances. In an informal letter to SCAP, subject: Termination of War Expenditures, which it is understood was personally submitted to the Supreme Commander by Prime Minister Yoshida on or about 3 December 1946 Tab A, it was stated that blackmarket prices were utilized for about 90% of materials used for troop housing and general Occupation construction and about 40% for dependent housing. Further conferences with Government programs are purchased at higher than legal prices.

6. A formal indication of SCAP policy regarding such payments was given by SCAPIN 967, 18 May 1946, subject: Japanese Budget For Fiscal Year 1946, Tab B, in which the Japanese Government was ordered to use only legal prices in preparing its 1946-47 budget estimates. SCAPIN 1430 30 December 1946, Tab C, called attention to the fact that the Japanese Government was making excessive expenditures due to blackmarket price payments and many other factors and directed a system of supervisory inspection and auditing procedures adequate to control Government expenditures.

7. No directive, however, has required the Japanese Government to pay only legal prices and prevailing wages. Previous SCAP directives on the subject have ignored this point and can be interpreted as condoning blackmarket payments. Therefore, the Japanese Government has been recklessly permitting any price payment for materials and wages with little regard to inflationary effect. Since the blackmarket is fully utilized for procurement, no real attempt has been made to implement its control and distribution machinery regardless of continued pressure and direction by SCAP to make such machinery effective.

8. The condition of the Japanese economy is of direct concern to SCAP since it directly affects the cost of Occupation; namely, the volume of imports necessary to prevent disease and unrest, the ability of Japan to export sufficient goods to pay for such imports, the number of troops necessary to insure order, and the length of Occupation. Further, the JCS directive to SCAP directs that necessary steps be taken to prevent serious inflation.

DISCUSSION

9. A proposed directive was prepared by ESS in December, 1946 directing the Japanese Government to limit any payments to legally established prices and prevailing wages. Each interested SCAP staff section, except G-4, Tab D, concurred in the proposed action although the Chief Engineer objected to the retroactive nature of the draft, Tab E. The retroactive clause was eliminated from the proposed draft and it was re-submitted with further arguments for concurrence.

10. Concurrence in the proposed action has now been received from each interested section of SCAP. The necessity for taking such action in any attempt to control inflation is recognized by all concerned. However, G-4 and the Chief Engineer point out that the directive will cause some delays in the Occupation construction program. ESS recognizes that unavoidable temporary dislocations in the supply of materials will probably result while the Japanese distribution machinery is being made effective. However, the Japanese Government can meanwhile use its authority to confiscate materials at legal prices in order to alleviate critical shortages.

11. The long run effect of the directive should be most beneficial to the accomplishment of Occupation construction projects as well as to the other objectives of the Occupation by contributing greatly to the stabilization of the economy, insuring an increased and more steady flow of goods as a result of improved distribution of raw materials to producers, and by preventing the flow of available materials to other Japanese Government projects through the blackmarket.

12. The other suggestions by the Chief Engineer and G-4 regarding the increased utilization of war stocks returned to the Japanese Government and to permit adequate legal prices to provide for continued production will be implemented by ESS. The latter suggestion will be much more feasible after the proposed directive is issued. The greatest difficulty producers are presently facing in keeping costs of production down to official price levels has been the failure to receive adequate distribution of raw materials at legal prices. The effect of the directive in implementing the Government distribution machinery will be to considerably alleviate this problem for essential producers. Further, the withdrawal of the largest consumer of blackmarket goods, the Japanese Government, from the blackmarket will be a considerable deterrent to blackmarket activities.

13. Lump-sum contractors competing under the bid system should not be hampered by this directive providing the contract is based on legal prices and prevailing wages. Minor variations in actual contractor expenditures would be possible as long as the total cost did not exceed the contract based on such prices and wages. The directive is only prohibitive to the Japanese Government.

CONCLUSIONS

14. Since the Japanese Government has been negligent in controlling inflation, it is recommended that SCAP take positive action, as required by JCS directive, and in order to minimize costs of Occupation.

RECOMMENDATIONS

15. It is recommended that the attached proposed directive ordering the Japanese Government to pay only legal prices, to eliminate any legal price differentials for Occupation Forces projects, to pay only actual labor costs and to pay no higher than prevailing wages to labor be approved and dispatched.

CONCURRENCE

16. Concurrences have been obtained as follows:

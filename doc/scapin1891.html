GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 311.23 (3 May 48) CCS

(SCAPIN - 1891)

APO 500

3 May 1948

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
SUBJECT :Radioteletype Tests on International Radiotelegraph Circuits.

1. Informal discussions between Ministry of Communications officials and the Civil Communications Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, have indicated the desirability of conducting radioteletype transmission and reception tests on existing commercial circuits. Willingness to conduct tests of this nature has been expressed by the Ministry of Communications.

2. Tests of the nature stated in paragraph 1 above are authorized under the following stipulations:

a. The tests are authorized with the object of determining whether present Japanese radio terminal and associated equipment are adequate for overseas radioteletype operations.

b. The Ministry of Communications will prepare a complete plan of the tests and will submit same to the Civil Communications Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers for approval and for coordination with designated circuit terminals in foreign countries.

c. The United States Army will make available a certain amount of teletype equipment to be used under the general supervision of United States Army personnel to insure safekeeping and protection thereof. The period of use of this equipment may not exceed six (6) months from the date these tests begin.

d. Some technical assistance from United States Army communications personnel may be available to aid in an expeditious and efficient handling of the project.

e. Installation, operation, and maintenance of the equipment involved in the tests are the responsibilities of the Ministry of Communications with such assistance from the Eighth Army as the latter may consider practicable and necessary.

f. These tests will be conducted during such periods when the circuits are clear of paid traffic. Under no circumstances will paid traffic be handled on the equipment used for the tests. Care will be exercised to insure that the movement of paid traffic on the regular traffic circuits is neither impeded nor interrupted.

g. The expenses involved in the tests will be borne by the Ministry of Communications and will not be chargeable to termination of war expenses or otherwise to the occupation forces.

h. At the completion of the tests the United States Army equipment will be returned immediately to the issuing authority.

i. A complete report of the result of the tests will be submitted to Civil Communications Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers within ten (10) days after completion.

3. Direct communication between the Civil Communications Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers and the Ministry of Communications within the scope of this memorandum is authorized.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

APO 500

3 May 1948

MEMORANDUM

SUBJECT: Information of General Application Pertaining to Directive Number SCAPIN 1891, file AG 311.23 (3 May 48) CCS, this Headquarters, dated 3 May 1948, subject: Radioteletype Tests on International Radiotelegraph Circuit

1. With reference to the subject memorandum, the following is published for the information of all concerned.

2. Operation results achieved on the existing radiotelegraph circuits between Japan and the United States, while satisfactory for radiotelegraph operation, do not conclusively indicate whether those circuits, with the associated terminal equipment now available and in use in Japan, are adequate to permit efficient radioteletype operation.

3. Queries have been received from various United States carriers as to whether it would be possible to convert the present international circuits to radioteletype operation instead of radiotelegraph. Much of the Japanese equipment being very old and of more or less obsolete design it has not been possible to conclusively answer the queries without first conducting radioteletype tests under actual operating conditions.

4. Data made available to the Civil Communications Section, as well as the Ministry of Communications, will be of considerable value to both with respect to technological improvements, mechanization of circuits, research, etc.

5. Operational and technical standards applied to radiotelegraph operation are not suitable for radioteletype operation, therefore it is necessary to subject the terminal equipment involved to actual tests using teletype equipment for activation before it can be conclusively stated that such terminal equipment is adaptable for radioteletype operation.

A. J. REHE

Major, AGD

Asst Adj Gen

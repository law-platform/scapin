GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 400 (18 Jun 46) ESS/IN

(SCAPIN-1028)

APO 500

18 June 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :THE IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
SUBJECT :Rubber Control.

1. Reference is made to the following Memoranda for the Imperial Japanese Government.

a. AG 091.3 (25 Sep 45) ESS (SCAPIN 58), subject: “Operation of Manufacturing Industries.”

b. AG 400.291 (23 Mar 46) ESS/IN (SCAPIN 799-A), subject: “Inventory of Rubber and Metal Stocks in Japan.”

c. AG 091.31 (14 Apr 46) ESS/I-E (SCAPIN 994-A), subject: “Preparation of Crude Rubber, Tin and Antimony for Export.”

2. It is directed that all stocks of the following grades of crude rubber, including current factory stocks reported under inventory provisions of SCAPIN Directive 799-A (23 Mar 46), be impounded immediately by the Japanese Government:

a. General Standard Classification:

Chinese Prime or better,

Good Fair Average Quality,

Fair Average Quality.

b. All stocks reported on inventory as “unknown” or “various grades” will be considered as being in one of the three grades listed in 2a, above, until definite grading has been established and the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers so notified.

3. The above grades of rubber will be released for use only upon the specific approval of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

4. Permission is hereby granted for the immediate release of 1,000 metric tons of crude rubber of Good Fair Average Quality. This 1,000 tons of crude rubber is to be used only in the production of medical goods, rice thrasher rolls and conveyor and transmission belts. The exact location of the rubber used for this purpose will be reported to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers without delay.

5. Continued compliance with SCAPIN Directive No. 994-A will have priority on all grades of rubber mentioned in paragraph 2, above.

6. The use of any remaining part of the 6,000 tons released under provisions of AG 091.3 (25 Sep 45) ESS, (SCAPIN 58), and not yet processed for manufacture, will be governed by paragraph 2 and 3, above.

7. The use of all grades lower that Fair Average Quality is not restricted but complete reports will be made to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers of all allocation and uses of these stocks.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1028)

GTW/HBO/mdb

1. Subject: ''Control Measures and Allocation Prodecures Applicable to Crude Rubber Stocks."

2. Memorandum being issued to Japanese Government directing them to immediately impound all grades of rubber of the first four grades. This action, recommended by G-4, is necessary to prevent the following:

a. Use of the top grades of crude rubber in domestic production rather than for export (Memorandum SCAPIN 994-A, 14 April 1946, directs the Imperial Japanese Government to prepare 10,000 metric tons of crude rubber for shipment. Not less than 5,000 metric tons will be grade No.1, Superior Quality rubber and the remainder shall be grade No.2).

b. Use of certain grades of rubber that may involve restitutional claims in the future. (The recent inventory (SCAPIN 799-A, 23 May 46) revealed original source of 83 metric tons as being Dutch East Indies and 4,385 tons as being British Malay.) Repeated efforts to have original source determined of all stocks of rubber in Japan have been unsuccessful (SCAPIN 266, 1 Nov 45, and reply by IJG, C. L. O. No. 563 (1) 14 Nov 45).

c. Misuse and waste of rubber. Rubber is one of the most important raw materials in the rebuilding of Japanese industry and its use will require close supervision by the Occupational Forces.

d. Curtailment of dependent housing program, due to lack of rubber for wiring, hose, etc.

3. Prior to this time, the only rubber that has been frozen or impounded has been the 10,000 tons of former military stocks (SCAPIN 58, 25 Sep 45, which released 6,000 tons of 16,000 tons of reported military stocks) (with no grade specified). There has been no curtailment on the use of other rubber stocks, but allocation and consumption reports have been made to ESS, Industrial Division.

4. The result of the inventory of rubber (SCAPIN 799-A, 23 May 46) showed the following stocks of various grades:

Chinese Prime or better 606 tons

GFAQ #1 5,398

FAQ #2 6,232

#3 sheets (deteriorated #1 & 2) 6,150

Standard 158

MQ 699

Unknown, various grades, ungraded 5,937

Crepe #1 1,093

Crepe #2 4,019

Latex 304

Tire scrap 368

Lopping rubber 77

Flat bark 33

Gutta Percha 83

31,157

The proposed directive would impound the first three grades listed, a total of 12,236 tons, and those listed as ''various" and "unknown" 5,937 tons -- a grand total of 18,174 tons, 10,000 tons of which is to be exported.

5. The stocks listed below are considered adequate to meet the requirements of industry until such a time as application can be made for the release of additional amounts:

a. Immediate release of 1,000 tons, GFAQ grade.

b. Stocks less than ten tons that were not inquired under provisions of inventory directive.

c. Those grades not impounded (12,983 tons).

d. Any part remaining of 6,000 tons released under SCAPIN 58, 25 September 1945.

6. Upon application for additional amounts of rubber, the IJG will be required to submit proposed manufacturing plan. Upon approval, rubber will be released.

7. A complete record of all stocks of rubber reported on inventory has been made showing location, grade, tonnage and type of packing, and will enable a fairly accurate running inventory to be kept at all times.

8. Concurrences: G-4 - Informal concurrence (Comdr Ryan)

OCPC - Mr. Blake

I & E - Mr. Ryan

9. This is an administrative matter and no military surveillance is necessary.

G. T. W.

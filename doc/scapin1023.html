AG 004 (15 Jun 46) ESS/IN

(SCAPIN-1023)

APO 500

15 June 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :THE IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
SUBJECT :Physical Inventory of Industrial Installations.

1. The Imperial Japanese Government is notified that a physical inventory of certain designated Japanese plants and installations will be initiated immediately upon receipt of this memorandum and accomplished in the shortest possible period of time consistent with accuracy and thoroughness.

2. This physical inventory will be carried out, by Japanese custodial personnel at each of the designated plants, under direction of the Commanding General, Eighth United States Army, and the Commander, Naval Activities, Japan, and in strict accordance with inventorying instructions to be laid down by them.

3. Within seventy-two (72) hours of the time of receipt of this memorandum, the Imperial Japanese Government will have qualified representatives report to the Commanding General, Eighth United Staes Army, and the Commander, Naval Activities, Japan, to receive instructions in regard to specific plants and facilities affected and measures which will be taken to effect this physical inventory.

4. It is directed that the Imperial Japanese Government make available to the Commanding General, Eighth United States Army, and the Commander, Naval Activities, Japan, all personnel, equipment and supplies deemed necessary by them for an expeditious and effective accomplishment of the inventory.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1023)

GTW/rzc

23 May 1946

1. In order that the agency of the Far Eastern Commission, having responsibility for allocating shares of Japanese reparations to claimant nations, may have accurate information on which to base such allocation, an inventory and catalog of Japanese industries must be made.

2. Radio CA-57993 from SCAP to WARCOS (JCS) dated 18 February 1946 stated in part 4: "This headquarters preparing inventory catalog of machinery and equipment subject to reparations removal under interim program. Will advise date available. Recommend such inventory catalog be submitted to claimant nations and such nations submit estimates of equipment to be claimed."

3. A preliminary catalog of data available from Japanese sources is being prepared covering aircraft factories, arsenals and laboratories. However, it covers only two-thirds of aircraft factories presently under custody and control, none of the laboratories, and about one-half of the arsenals.

4. Letter to CG Eighth Army and COMNAVJAP, AG 900 (25 Mar 46) ESS/IN (see Tab B), advised in paragraph 3 that" * * detailed plans would be prepared by this headquarters which will include the following industries * * *". As pointed out in these letters a physical inventory of Japanese plants, laboratories, arsenals and other installations is an essential requirement to any sound reparations program since it will provide the only accurate factual means of collecting data for the reparations catalog. This catalog is, in turn, essential because it will provide the only practical means of facilitating the selection and allocation (from outside Japan) of reparations items by whatever reparations policy agency may be set up by the Allied Powers.

5. In order to determine a practical method whereby an inventory could be accomplished within the scope of means presently available to the army, a conference was held in GHQ during the period March 4-8 with representatives from Eighth Army, G-4 and the Reparations Branch of the Industrial Division. At this conference a detailed inventory program was evolved which was considered practical of execution. It was the recommendation of the conference that this program be forwarded along with any letter of instructions to the Army to be used as a guide for the Army to prepare its command instructions to accomplish inventory.

6. The basic feature of the inventory program is that the inventory will be accomplished by Japanese plant custodial personnel under the direction of occupation force units to be designated by CG Eighth Army and Commander U. S. Naval Activities in Japan.

7. The attached memorandum for the Imperial Japanese Government directs them to made personnel available to CG Eighth Army and Commander U. S. Naval Activities in Japan to accomplish the inventory as directed by those commanders.

8. The attached letter of instructions to the Army and COMNAVJAP directs them to take immediate action to effectuate the inventory and forwards the inventory program as developed for information and guidance in issuance of command instructions. Authority is granted to the Army and COMNAVJAP to use tactical troops as required to accomplish the inventory.

9. The inventory program in detail is appended to the letters of instructions to CG Eighth Army and Commander U. S. Naval Activities in Japan for their information and guidance.

10. Information of general application to the directive will accompany copies of the directive sent to all military government units and advise them of the basic features of the program.

11. It is recommended the above memorandum to the Imperial Japanese Government and letters of instructions to CG Eighth Army and Commander U.S. Naval Activities in Japan, together with enclosures, be approved and dispatched.

12. Concurrences: G-4.

W. F. M.

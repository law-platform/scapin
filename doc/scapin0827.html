GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG260 (19 Mar 46) ESS/FI

(SCAPIN-827)

APO 500

19 March 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Pensions and Benefits

1. Reference is made to the following memorandum:

a. Memorandum, Central Liaison Office, for General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, file CLO No. 539 (2. 1), dated 5 February 1946, subject: “Pensions and Benefits.”

b. Memorandum, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, for the Imperial Japanese Government, file AG 260 (24 Nov 45) ESS/FI, dated 24 November 1945, subject: “Pensions and Benefits.”

2. Compliance with reference 1b above requires termination, as of 1 February 1946, of all pensions and living aid allowance certificates labelled “Fighting Service.”

3. In the case of pension and living aid allowance certificates marked “Civil Serice” it will be the responsibility of the Japanese Imperial Government to complete not later than 1 July 1946, a preliminary investigation to determine which certificates are “clear,” that is, do not include any period of military service on the part of the holder, and which certificates are “doubtful,” that is, may be based partially on a period of military service. Payments due during the month of April 1946 may be made on certificates in cases where the Pension Bureau files have not been examined. However, no payments will be made after 30 April on any except “clear” certificates or certificates which have been revised to eliminate any period of, or credit for, military service. The April payment shall not be made on certificates found “doubtful" prior to that date, and any excess April payments on certificates which are subsequently revised shall be deducted from future payments.

4. Payments on military pensions terminated by reference 1 b above are permitted after 1 February 1946 and prior to 30 April 1946 only in situations where payment was scheduled prior to February but was delayed by a mechanical or technical error. In no event shall any paymsnt be made on military pensions which the Pension Bureau had not approved by 1 February 1946.

5. The Japanese Government will reduce all disability benefits as directed in reference 1b above. Payments due in April 1946 may be made under existing schedule until 30 April 1946, but the entire schedule must be revised prior to any subsequent payments, and the excess of April payments over the altered rates will be deducted from future payments.

6. It is the responsibility of the Imperial Japanese Government to make and complete the above investigations and revisions in certificates and payment schedules and to take such other action as may be necessary to effect complete compliance with reference 1 b above as promptly as possible. A detailed report shall be submitted on the 10th day of each month covering action taken and progress made during the preceding month.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

B.M.FICHI
Brigadiar General,AGD,
Adjutant General

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN-827)

CFT/ STB/ ajy

1. The Japanese Imperial Government was directed on November 24, 1945, AG 260 (24 Nov 45) ESS/FI, to take the necessary steps to terminate, not later than 1 February 1946, all payments of military pensions and other benefits arising out of military service with the exception of disability pensions which can be continued at a reduced rate.

2. The right of recipients to receive pensions, living aid allowances and similar benefits is evidenced by certificates issued by the pension bureau. The payments are made quarterly by the post offices upon presentment of the certificates.

3. Representatives of the pension bureau have had numerous conferences with respect to practical difficulties attendent upon compliance with the directive by 1 February 1946. Their chief difficulty lies in the necessity of examining more than 100,000 civil service pension records, in addition to all of the disability pension files, and then to call in and readjust the certificates to eliminate any credit given for military service time. The pension bureau, because of air-raids, moved from Tokyo to Odawara and decentralized its files. Many of its files were impounded by the occupation forces and only recently have been released. They originally requested a year's extension of time to complete such investigation and readjustments, but the request was refused.

4. The Japanese Government now proposes, after discussing various possibilities with Staff Sections of General Headquarters Supreme Commander for Allied Powers, the following:

a. To cancel all certificates labelled "Fighing Service." This, it is understood, should eliminate over nine-tenths of the military pensions and allowances.

b. To complete preliminary examination by 1 July 1946 of ordinary government "Civil Service" pension files, in order to determine which certificates are "clear," that is do not include any period of military service and which are "doubtful:" that is may be based partly on a period of military service, Pension payments may be made during the month of April on certificates where the pension bureau files have not been examined. However no payments will be made on any certificates classified as "doubtful," nor will any payments be made after the 30th of April on any except clear certificates or certificates revised to eliminated any periods of military service.

c. To make April payments on disability pensions at existing rates, but to revise them prior to future payments. In view of difficulties mentioned above, an immediate readjustment is impossible.

d. To make payments on pensions after 1 February only in cases where mechanical errors were involved and the payment was scheduled prior to that date.

5. These proposals are generally acceptable with additional qualification that any overpayment of pension or disability pensions will be deducted from future adjusted payments.

6. This is an administrative matter and as such does not require surveillance by the occupation forces.

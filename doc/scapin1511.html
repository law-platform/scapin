MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Construction and Repair of Coal Miners’ Houses

1. Reference is Central Liaison Office Memorandum No. 175 (LO 11/1403), dated 16 January 1947, subject: Application concerning Plan of Construction and Repair of Coal Miners’ Houses, forwarding an application for allocation of credit on behalf of eight (8) Restricted Concerns for construction and repair of dwellings for coal miners.

2. No objection is offered to the above application, provided that:

a. Amounts to be allocated to the respective companies shall be as follows:

Name of Cpmpany Amount

Mitsui Mining Company, Ltd ¥87,300,000.

Mitsubishi Mining Company, Ltd 36,120,000.

Hokkaido Colliery and Steamship Company, Ltd 10,500,000.

Nittetsu Mining Company, Ltd 8,280,000.

Furukawa Mining Company, Ltd 19,800,000.

Joban Mining Company, Ltd 17,160,000.

Ube Mining Company, Ltd 10,200,000.

Seika Mining Company, Ltd 4,440,000.

b. There shall be submitted to the Antitrust and Cartels Division, Economic and Scientific Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, an application by each of the companies, showing details of the proposed expenditures, prior to beginning the rehabilitation work and expenditure of funds.

c. Materials used in execution of this program shall be purchased at not to exceed official ceiling prices.

d. This memorandum shall not be construed as an approval for priority of raw materials, equipment, or facilities to carry out the plans for which this loan is made. The established regulations, policies, procedures and priorities instituted by the Imperial Japanese Government shall prevail.

e. Direct communication is authorized between interested staff sections and General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied powers and agencies of the Imperial Japanese Government concerned to implement all provisions of this memorandum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1511) 3 February 1947

1. CLO Memorandum No.175 (LO 11/1403, dated 16 January 1947, forwards an application of the Imperial Japanese Government for construction and repair of coal miners' houses. The application involves borrowing of funds by several Restricted Concerns.

2. Paragraph 2g of SCAP Memorandum AG 300. 8 (8 Dec 45) ESS/AC, (SCAPIN-408), subject: "Regulations Affecting Restricted Concerns, provides, except as otherwise authorized or directed by SCAP, that: " . . no loan of any other character will be obtained, except in the normal course of business."

APO 500 6 February 1947

3. a. This application has been made by the Board of Coal of the Imperial Japanese Government and is one phase of the general rehabilitation program of the coal mining industry in Japan. This particular application pertains only to the construction and repair of coal miners' houses. The second application, which will be submitted in the near future, will pertain to the rehabilitation of coal mining machinery. Due to the great need for increased coal production, an overall plan for the industry has been prepared through the cooperation of the Board of Coal, the Board of Reconstruction, the Economic Stabilization Board and the various Ministries concerned. This application sets forth the minimum requirements for construction and repair of coal miners' dwellings.

b. The program, as approved by the Economic Stabilization Board, involves the construction of 1, 500 new houses and the repair of 3,920 houses. The following table shows the Restricted or Limited Concerns involved and the amount to be borrowed by each for both new construction and repairs:

¥193,800,000.

c. Several non-restricted companies also will borrow sums so that the total amount to be borrowed for repairs and construction of houses will be ¥231,120,000. However, the sum of ¥193,800,000 represents the amount to be borrowed by the concerns whose activities are limited or restricted. These sums of money are necessary to rebuild coal miners' houses. This phase of the coal mining rehabilitation program is considered the most urgent of all the problems confronting the industry. Since a blanket application was submitted, it is desirable to grant permission for an allocation of credit for the amounts as listed. While permission will be granted only to the various companies to be allocated credit in the amounts as listed above, each company will in addition be required to submit informally an application showing proposed expenditures. This will prevent a blanket authorization to the companies and will give SCAP a check on the exact details of the expenditures. It will also prevent the companies from resorting to the black market for materials. This procedure will not be cumbersome inasmuch as the informal applications can be expeditious y handled as long as they come within the limits of the overall approval of credit and will not require a separate directive in each case.

4. Under these circumstances, no objection is offered to the application.

5. This is an administrative matter and no surveillance by the Eighth Army is required.

6. The interests of USAFIK are not concerned.

7. Concurrences: ESS/FT (Lt. Col. D. H. Jennings); ESS/IN (Mr. J. Z. Reday); ESS/PC&R. (Mr. H. F. Alber); ESS/LE (Mr. H. T. Straitiff);

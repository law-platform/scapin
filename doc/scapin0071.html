OFFICE OF THE SUPREME COMMANDER
FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 560 (28 Sep 45) ESS

(SCAPIN-71)

APO 500

28 September 1945

MEMORANDUM FOR :THE IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Availability and Utilization of Non-Combatant Japanese Shipping.

1. You will take all necessary steps to insure the maximum availability and utilization of all non-combatant shipping resources as are at your disposal, including, but not limited, to the following:

a. Direct that all ship yards and other agencies engaged in the repair of ships, in the completion of non-combatant ship construction, in the construction or repair of barges, tugs, fishing boats, and other craft, work twenty-four (24) hours a day, seven days a week.

b. Direct that the loading and unloading of all cargo vessels continue twenty-four (24) hours a day, seven days a week.

c. Take such action as is necessary to secure adequate labor, and to prevent absenteeism, to the end that maximum utilization of Japan's shipping resources will be realized.

2. An acknowledgment of this directive is requested.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

/s/ Harold Fair
/t/ HAROLD FAIR
Lt Colonel, A.G.D.,
Asst. Adjutant General

DISTRIBUTION:

G-4

Hil Govt

Civil Info& Educ Sec

Navy Liaison

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 71)

28 September 1945

1. With the extreme scarcity of merchant ships available to the Japanese, it is imperative that every step be taken to insure that available shipping be worked as efficiently and as fully as is humanly possible. Discussions with Japanese indicate the following conditions exist:

a. Discharge and loading of ships is only done during the daylight hours with one shift of ten or twelve hours each.

b. The ship yards repairing ships and completing the construction of unfinished ships are only working six days a week with one shift.

c. The work of loading, discharging, and ship repair is greatly handicapped by the scarcity of labor.

d. The efficiency of the workers employed in these activities is greatly restricted by insufficient food.

2. G-4, the Navy Liaison Officer, and the Civil Information and Education Section concur in the dispatch of the attached directive to the Japanese Government directing necessary corrective action.

3. The list of interested agencies has been checked prior to submission of this paper.

R. C. K.

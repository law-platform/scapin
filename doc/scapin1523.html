GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 710 (12 Feb 47) PH

(SCAPIN-1523)

APO 500

12 February 1947

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Prevention and Control of Typhus Fever in Japan.

1. SCAP memoranda to the Imperial Japanese Government AG 710 (21 Nov 45) PH (SCAPIN 331) dated 21 November 1945, subject: Prevention and Control of Typhus Fever in Japan; and AG710(29 Nov 45)PH, (SCAPIN 368) dated 29 November 1945, subject: Prevention and Control og Typhus Fever in Japan, are hereby rescinded and the following memorandum substituted in place thereof.

2. Due to the continued threat of typhus fever - epidemic (louse-borne) and murine (flea-borne) - in Japan, it is desired that the responsible Japanese agency institute the following measures for the prevention and control of typhus fever in Japan:

a. Adequate control measures (including rodent control) against typhus fever in cities, towns, villages and areas where cases of typhus fever occur.

b. Routine disinfestation with insecticide powder, louse, of all civilians crossing the straits of Tsugaru from Hokkaido to Honshu, and from Honshu to Hokkaido.

c. Routine disinfestation and immunization of all foreign repatriates embarking from Japanese ports to their homeland.

d. Rgutine disinfestation and immunization of all repatriate Japanese nationals debarking at Japanese ports as covered by memorandum of 5 November 1946 for the Imperial Japanese Government, subject: “Repatriation” (Reference: AG 014.33 (5 Nov. 46) GC (SCAPIN 927/10) dated 5 November 1946.)

e. Routine disinfestation of railway coaches, railway stations, public conveyances, theaters, etc., as contained in the memorandum of 13 December 1946, subject: “Rickettsicidal (Typhus Control) Spray Program” (Reference PHMJG - 3 dated 13 December 1946).

f. Disinfestation by use of Insecticide, spray, DDT residual effect at 30 day intervals:

(1) All railway cars assigned for use of all United Nations nationals, military and civilian, including dependents.

(2) All ferries operating between Aomori and Hakodate assigned for use by all United Nations nationals, military and civilian, including dependents.

(3) Such other public conveyances that may be assigned for use of all United Nations nationals, military and civilian, including dependents.

(After each disinfestation, all conveyances will be plainly and uniformly marked as directed by the military command concerned to show date and place of treatment.)

g. Imperial Japanese Government is responsible for providing necessary supplies, equipment and funds required for typhus control operations.

h. Japanese civilian personnel will be provided by the Japanese agency concerned to carry out required typhus control procedures.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS

APO 500

12 February 1947

AG710 (12 Feb 47)PH

MEMORANDUM

SUBJECT : Information of General Application Pertaining to Directive Number (SCAPIN 1523), file AG 721.6 (12 Feb 47)PH, GHQ, SCAP, 12 Feb 47, subject: Prevention and Control of Typhus Fever in Japan, to the Imperial Japanese Government.

1. With reference to the subject memorandum, the following is published for the information of all concerned.

2. Subject memorandum rescinds two SCAP memoranda to the Imperial Japanese Government as follows: AG 710 (21 Nov 45)PH, (SCAPIN 331), dated 21 November 1945, subject: Prevention and Control of Typhus Fever in Japan; and AG710 (29 Nov 45)PH, (SCAPIN 368) dated 29 November 1945, subject: Prevention and Control of Typhus Fever in Japan. Subject memorandum further presents a new directive to replace the two rescinded memoranda.

3. The information contained in subject memorandum on typhus fever control deals with the latest control measures designed to inhibit the spread of murine typhus as well as epidemic typhus.

4. Command instructions will follow through Command channels pertaining to the execution of surveillance. The following procedure is suggested:

a. Maintain periodic surveillance to be certain that proper typhus control measures are being accomplished.

b. Advise General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, of any corrective action that should be taken at the national level.

R. G. HERSEY

Lt. Col. AGD,

Asst Adj Gen.

MEMORANDUM FOR :Imperial Japanese Government
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo
SUBJECT :Supplemental Instructions Relating to Surrender Foreign Exchange Assets

1. Reference is Memorandum for the Imperial Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, AG 131 (29 Nov 46) ESS/FI, (SCAPIN-1368), dated 29 November 1946, subject: Surrender of Foreign Exchange Assets.

2. In order to implement the provisions of reference memorandum direct communication between agencies of the Imperial Japanese Government and interested Staff Sections of General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, is authorized.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

/s/ A. J. Rehe
for /t/ JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel,A. G. D. ,
Adjutant General

MEMO FOR RECORD : (SCAPIN - 1422)

WKL/JHH/gwh

19 December 1946

1. Memo to Imperial Japanese Government from GHQ, SCAP, AG 131 (29 Nov 46) ESS/FI, (SCAPIN 1368), dated 29 November 1946, subject: Surrender of Foreign Exchange Assets, directs the IJG to take immediate action to require all persons within Japan except non-Japanese nationals to surrender to the Bank of Japan against individual receipt all foreign currencies and foreign exchange instruments. This surrender must be completed by 31 December 1946. Detailed reports indicating type and value of currencies and instruments surrendered must be submitted to the Civil Property Custodian weekly. Complete reports will be submitted by 31 January 1947.

2. In order to comply with the provisions of reference memorandum, the Bank of Japan must issue individual receipts to persons surrendering foreign currency and foreign exchange instruments. Two copies of each receipt issued are to be forwarded weekly to GHQ, CPC. If these receipts are forwarded through the CLO it will require from two to three weeks for them to reach the Civil Property Section. In order to expedite the flow of these reports is considered that direct communication should be authorized. Further, it is anticipated that the implementation of the surrender program will give rise to numerous inquiries from the IJG, which will necessitate formal replies from ESS/FI, GHQ. Inasmuch as Washington is desirous of completing this program as rapidly as possible, it is considered that such inquiries should be answered by direct communications rather than by formal memorandum through normal channels of GHQ and CLO.

3. This is an administrative matter and no surveillance by the Eighth Army is required.

4. The interests of USAFIK are not concerned.

5. Concurrences not necessary.

W. K. L.

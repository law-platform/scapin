GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 800.217 (21 Jun 48) CCS

(SCAPIN - 1913/1)

APO 500

14 October 1948

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
SUBJECT :Radio Communications for the Japanese Whaling Fleet.

1. Reference is made to:

a. Memorandum for the Japanese Government, file AG 676.3 (14 Jul 47) CCS, SCAPIN 1744, dated 14 July 1947, subject: Control of Radio Communications, as amended.

b. Memorandum for the Japanese Government, file AG 458 (25 Jun 48) GD, SCAPIN 5822-A, dated 15 July 1948, subject: Radar for Vessels of Antarctic Whaling Expedition 1948-49.

c. Memorandum for the Japanese Government, file AG 800.217 (21 Jun 48) NR/FI, SCAPIN 1913, dated 21 June 1948, subject: Japanese Whaling Operations in the Antarctic in the 1948-1949 Season.

2. The radio frequencies for use by the 1948-1949 Japanese Whaling Expedition will be as authorized in inclosure 1.

3. The Bureau of Fisheries of the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry will act as the Communications Agent in Japan for all official messages to and from the whaling fleet including the Taiyo Gyogyo (Ocean Fishing Company), the Nippon Suisan (Marine Products Company) and those observers accompanying the whaling fleet with the authorization of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

4. Any communications between Japan and the whaling fleet, except personal messages which will be handled in a manner hereinafter described, will be transmitted or received by the Japanese coastal radio service only when addressed through the Communications Agent for the Whaling Fleet named in paragraph 3. A file copy of all messages sent or received through the Communications Agent will be retained in a readily accessible file by that Bureau.

5. Personal messages are authorized to and from members of the crews of the Japanese whaling fleet ships and their families in Japan. In Japan all such messages will be received by or transmitted from Radio Choshi or Radio Nagasaki of the Ministry of Communications Coastal Radio system. Copies of these messages will be retained in separately maintained files at these two stations. The Ministry of Communications will be responsible for the proper filing of all such personal messages at Radio Choshi and Radio Nagasaki.

6. a. Personal messages addressed to any crew member of the whaling fleet ships will be accepted at any regularly designated filing point in the Japanese Domestic Telegraph system and dispatched via the :Japanese coastal radio system through Choshi or Nagasaki.

b. Personal messages from crew members of the whaling ships to addresses in Japan will be accepted by the ship of origin and transmitted to either Radio Choshi or Radio Nagasaki by the ships authorized in inclosure 1 on regularly maintained schedules. Such personal messages will then be delivered to any address in Japan through the normal domestic communications channels.

7. Communications, including personal messages, to and from authorized Allied observers accompanying the whaling fleet will be forwarded to or received from the Natural Resources Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, in Tokyo via the Communications Agent named in paragraph 3 above, and will be handled without charge.

8. Communications including personal messages, between Japanese nationals, to and from the whaling fleet, will be subject to all existing rules and requirements of censorship.

9. Messages pertaining to the navigation of Japanese whaling vessels, meteorological observations, and fishing data may be sent in the following codes:

a. Telegraph code of the Bureau of Fisheries, Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry.

b. The Nihon Suisan's telegraph code for fishery information.

c. The Taiyo Gyogyo's telegraph code for fishery information.

▲ ▲ of these cedes will be deposited with the Civil Censorship ▲, C-2 Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers and with the Bureau of Fisheries, Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry.

10. With respect to the navigation of vessels, meteorological observations, and fishing data, the Japanese whaling fleet is authorized radio communication with the following:

a. Other vessels of the authorized Japanese whaling fleet.

b. Radio stations of the Japanese coastal radio service.

c. Whaling vessels of other nations.

11. The meteorological reports observed by the Japanese whaling fleet will be broadcast by the following ships, on the following schedule, using International meteorological broadcasting methods and procedure.

SHIP NAME CALL SIGN BROADCASTING TIME FREQUENCY & EMISSION

Hashidato-maru JWPM GMT 0530 Day Al, 11040 night Al, 8325

Nisshin-maru JOSJ GMT 0535 do

Hedhidate-maru JWPM GMT 1730 do

Nisshin-maru JOSJ GMT 1735 do

12. The Japanese whaling fleet will answer immediately any communications addressed to it by United States Navy vessels or Naval vessels of the other Allied Powers.

13. The Japanese whaling fleet will answer interrogations of non-Japanese coastal radio stations when so interrogated. All such communications will be brought to the immediate attention of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers' representative accompanying the expedition.

14. The Japanese whaling vessels may establish contact with other vessels or coastal radio stations to handle distress traffic in accordance with the General Radio Regulations (Cairo Revision 1938 and Atlantic City Convention revision effective 1 January 1949).

15. Communication is permitted with any radio station when authorized by the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers' representative accompanying the whaling expedition.

16. Direct communication between the Civil Communications Section, the Natural Resources Section, or any other interested section of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, and the Ministry of Communications and the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry, regarding matters within the scope of this memorandum, is authorized.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

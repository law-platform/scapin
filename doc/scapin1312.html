GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 260 (5 Nov 46) ESS/FI

(SCAPIN-1312)

APO 500

5 November 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Pensions and Benefits.

1. References are the following memoranda:

a. Memorandum from the Imperial Japanese Government, CLO No. 3924 (EF), 9 August 1946, Subject: “Pensions and Benefits for Former Members of Certain Dissolved Companies.”

b. Memorandum to the Imperial Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, AG 260 (24 Nov. 45) ESS/FI, SCAPIN 338, subject: “Pensions and Benefits.”

2. Those dissolved organizations referred to in reference 1 - a may be exempted from the provisions of paragraph 1 b of reference 1 b, providing that:

a. No pensions, benefits, or other emoluments shall be paid to any persons who at any time between 7 July 1937 and 2 September 1945 held an executive or policy-making position such as those of director, managing director, manager, governor, vice-governor, chairman, councillor, president, vice-president, advisor, auditor, partner, managing partner, and including all positions of equivalent or superior importance in any of these organizations; and,

b. Pensions and retirement allowances shall not exceed those customarily given by the organizations concerned prior to 1945.

3. All payments made under the provisions of this memorandum shall be reported to General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1312)

WKL/JEW/ihr

15 October 1946

1. Paragraph 1-b of memorandum to the Imperial Japanese Government, from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, File AG 260 (24 November 43) ESS/FI, subject: Pensions and Benefits (SCAPIN 338), prohibited all pensions or other emoluments awarded to individuals because of "membership in, or other service to, any association, society, or other organizations dissolved or suspended" by SCAP memoranda. The basic directive in dealing with this subject refers to "nationalistic, terroristic, or secret societies" and to policy-making officials of "public and private financial institutions" which played an important part in military aggression.

2. It would appear that the JCS directive, in ruling out pensions of "key" personnel of colonial banks, etc., would by implication also refer to the equivalent personnel of other organizations, such as Koeki Eidan, which also played an important part in the military and economic aggression of Japan.

3. Up to the present time only two exceptions have been made from paragraph 1-b of the Pensions and Benefit Directive (SCAPIN 338): the silk control agencies and agencies concerned with civil air transportation. In the case of the former, it was believed undesirable government personnel would be individually removed--rather than applying mass restrictions covering ordinary employees as well. The latter agencies were exempted because of the nature of their business, i.e., they had been dissolved simply because civil aviation had ceased to exist in Japan.

4. The Imperial Japanese Government has now submitted a further request pertaining to the exemption of dissolved agencies from the Pensions and Benefit Directive. These agencies include the Koeki Eidan and its subsidiary, the Nippon Zakka Boeki Shinko Kaisha, and the Japan Lumber Company, Ltd., as well as local lumber companies. The attached memorandum in answer to this request does not permit the exemption of policymaking personnel. The principal reasons for this have been indicated above. However, it is not believed that ordinary employees should be discriminated against, providing that retirement allowances paid to them do not exceed those customarily given.

5. Anti-Trust and Cartels (T.K. Wright) has indicated further reasons for denying pensions and benefits to the key personnel of the Koeki Eidan and the Japan Lumber Company:

(a) That such allowances might be utilized to dissipate excessively the assets of these agencies.

(b) That in recent dealings with the above organizations these personnel have shown themselves utterly untrustworthy.

6. This is an administrative matter and no surveillance is necessary. The interests of USAFIK have been considered and are not affected.

7. Concurrences have been received from the following: ESS/AC - TKW

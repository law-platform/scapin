SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 095 (1 Oct 46) ESS/AC

(SCAPIN-1244)

APO 500

1 October 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Repeal of the East Asia Shipping Company Act and Dissolution of the East Asia Shipping Company, Ltd., (Toa Kai-un K. K.).

1. In order to remove certain wartime legislation and prevent the reestablishment of a monopoly in the shipping industry, the Imperial Japanese Government is hereby directed to:

a. Repeal the East Asia Shipping Company Act (Law No. 68, 1941) and all laws, ordinances and regulations based thereon, within sixty (60) days from the date of this Memorandum.

b. Dissolve the East Asia Shipping Company, Ltd., within sixty (60) days from the date of this Memorandum.

c. Provide for the liquidation of the East Asia Shipping Company, Ltd., in accordance with the Commercial Code of Japan and existing price control and currency regulations, provided, however, that all transfers of property and equipment will have the prior approval of the Civilian Merchant Marine Committee and the Commander of Naval Activities in Japan, and that such liquidation will be completed within one (1) year from the date of this Memorandum.

d. Preserve the records of the East Asia Shipping Company, Ltd., in the office of the supervising bureau, subject to further use by the Imperial Japanese Government or further directions from the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

2. The Imperial Japanese Government will submit to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, in quintuplicate, typed in English on 8" x 11" paper, a report of the action taken pursuant to the provisions of paragraph 1, above, within ninety (90) days from the date of this Memorandum.

3. Direct communication is authorized between the interested Staff Sections of General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, and agencies of the Imperial Japanese Government concerned to implement all provisions of this Memoradum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1244)

WFM/JMcIH/GFS/gg

20 August 1946

1. The action directed by the attached Memorandum is in accordance with JCS Directive 1380/15, 3 November 1945.

2. The East Asia Shipping Company Ltd., was organized in 1939 as an amalgamation of all but two (2) of the Japanese owned shipping lines operating at that time between Japan, Korea and China, and thus became a virtual monopoly in the field. It was given official status by a special law effective 28 August 1941, which made it a government sponsored monopoly within its sphere of operation. This law provided further that:

a. The government might invest in the Company (however, this was never done).

b. The government guaranteed six (6) percent dividends on all privately owned stock.

c. The Company was authorized to engage in undertakings other than the shipping business, subject to government approval or orders. (It became, in effect, an agency for furthering political, economic and military penetration into China.)

d. The president and vice-president were appointed by the government, and the directors were subject to governmental approval.

e. The government was authorized to cancel any resolution of the shareholders or dismiss any director.

f. Debentures could be issued in excess of the legal limits of the Commercial Code of Japan and were guaranteed by the government.

g. The Company was exempted from certain other provisions of the Commercial Code.

3. The largest stockholders in order of their holdings are:

4. a. The purpose of the East Asia Shipping Company primarily was to further imperialistic expansion, The COMNAVJAP states in a letter to SCAP, dated 1 August 1946, that the Company was established to provide: "logistic support of Japanese forces in China and at the same time further the economic penetration and control of parts of China not actually under Military Control." The then Prime Minister stated in a speech at the reception given upon the establishment of this Company, 9 October 1939, inter alia: "At present the most important problem for Japan is a solution of the China Incident and the establishment of the New Order in East Asia...... Since the China Incident, the shipping in Eastern Asia has been recognized as very important, and the shipping rights in China have especially been expected to be expanded and strengthened immediately...... and now I express my congratulations to all of them (the Companies who formed this combine) for ... joining together in going along with the national policy by establishing the East Asia Shipping Co., Ltd.... Fortunately, if the plans and management of your company will succeed, the real power for the expansion of shipping rights in China my get into your hands...... Now the situation in Europe is threatening and our country faces many difficulties. For the purpose of accomplishing every national policy our shipping circle is exerting all of its efforts. Therefore the establishment of this company has a deep significance ..." The Minister of Transportation, speaking on the same occasion said: "It is an urgent necessity that the communications between China and Japan be strengthened in order to cooperate with China through politics, culture and such. This company has been established for the execution of that important mission ..."

b. All of the remaining uncaptured and unsunk ships belonging to this Company are being operated by the Civilian Merchant Marine Committee.

c. It is obvious that the East Asia Shipping Company, Ltd., is a monopolistic, imperialistic combination and, therefore, the attached Memorandum directs its dissolution.

5. This is an administrative matter, and no surveillance by the Eighth Army is required.

6. The interests of USAFIK are not concerned.

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 091.1 (14 Oct 46) CCS

(SCAPIN-1268)

APO 500

14 October 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Rehabilitation of Postal Savings Branches.

1. The Imperial Japanese Government is directed to plan and execute a program for prompt rehabilitation of Postal Savings Branches in order to accomplish the following objectives:

a. Provision of adequate personnel, space, equipment and supplies to bring all postings to ledgers, interest computations, and other work current, and permit the resumption of necessary safeguards to protect the savings of depositors.

b. Restoration of depositors’ postal savings, postal transfer, and bond custody ledgers, and individual bond custody cards, destroyed during the war.

2. This directive is issued for the purpose of securing proper and prompt action in restoring and maintaing records relating to facilities which are essential to the current national economy of Japan. It is not to be construed as authorizing action extending beyond the scope of this general purpose.

3. Direct communication between the civil Communications Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, and the Ministry of Communications is authorized concerning all matters within the scope of this memorandum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS

SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

APO 500

14 October 1946

MEMORANDUM:

SUBJECT: Information of General Application Pertaining to Memorandum Number (SCAPIN-1268), file AG 311.1, (14 Oct 46)CCS, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, dated 14 October 1946 subject: "Rehabilitation of Postal Savings Branches"

1. With reference to subject Memorandum, the following is published for the information of all concerned.

2. The Japanese Postal Savings System provides six types of accounts for the deposit and withdrawal of savings by the public at post offices. Accounting is made by daily reports from post offices to District Communications Bureaus. The Bureaus forward deposit and withdrawal slips and related vouchers to Postal Savings Branches for posting to individual ledgers of accountholders. The Branches are responsible for maintenance of accurate, current ledgers and other records, computation of interest payments, and verification of all deposits, withdrawals, and balances. The related services of postal transfer, which is similar to bank checking system, and bond custody, which provides for purchase, custody, and sale of bonds, are handled in a similar manner. Savings Branches are administered directly by the Ministry of Communications and are not subordinate to District Communications Bureaus.

3. The Postal Savings System is universally used by the Japanese people for the deposit of savings in small amounts. There are approximately 180 million accounts with 53 billion yen on deposit. The maintenance of complete and up-to-date ledgers and records is essential to its proper functioning and ability to meet the current economic needs of the people. Destruction of ledgers and records during the war and failure to keep the work current has created chaotic conditions in some of the Branches, which might cause a breakdown in the system as a whole. In order to reduce the volume of work during the war years, the Japanese Government suspended various procedures in regard to payments of withdrawals to depositors, computation and payment of interest, certification of balances, and other safeguards. It is necessary for the maintenance of minimum economic standards that the Japanese Government initiate and carry out prompt corrective measures to restore destroyed ledgers and records and keep the work of the branches current so that the normal safeguarding procedures may be followed.

4. Appendix A lists the Postal Savings Branches, the number of postal savings, postal transfer, and bond custody ledgers maintained at each, the number of ledgers destroyed, and the number restored as of the dates shown. In addition, each Branch is engaged in reconstruct on of individual bond custody cards for each bond entered in the bond custody ledgers as all of the cards, formerly maintained at Tokyo, were destroyed during the war. The number of cards destroyed and the number restored at each Branch are shown in Appendix A.

5. In surveillance of the directive, the responsibilities of the Occupation Forces are as follows:

a. Determine whether Branches are receiving from the Ministry of Communications necessary instructions for rehabilitation work and assistance in obtaining required personnel, space, equipment and supplies.

b. Ascertain the progress made monthly in rehabilitation of Branched and restoration of destroyed ledgers and records, and submit report by the tenth of the following month for the information of General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers (Attention: Civil Communications Section). Sample form of report is attached as Inclosure 2.

6. Command instructions pertaining to execution of surveillance will follow through command channels.

R. G. HERSEY

Lt. Col. AGD,

Asst Adj Gen.

AG387.6 (2 Dec 46) ESS/IN

(SCAPIN-1375)

APO 500

2 December 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Reorganization of the Precision Bearing Industry of Japan.

1. References are the following Memoranda for the Imperial Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers:

a. File AG 387,6 (14 Aug 46) ESS/IN, (SCAPIN 1136), dated 14 August 1946, subject: Reparation Selections Within the Precision Bearing Industry.

b. File AG 387.6 (6 Sep 46) ESS/IN,(SCAPIN 1184), dated 6 September 1946, subject: Revision of SCAPIN 1136.

2. Reference is letter for General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers from Central Liaison Office No. 5496 (ECI - F) dated 18 October 1946, subject: Request Concerning Reparations Selections Within the Bearing Industry.

3. Concentrating control of the precision bearing industry in the hands of one company is undesirable. To avoid a bearing monopoly that would hinder the healthy development of a peacetime industry the Nippon Seiko Company, Ltd has been placed under surveillance by Memorandum for the Imperial Japanese Gover ment from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers file AG 095 (2 Dec 46) ESS/AC, 2 December 1946 (SCAPIN 1374) subject: Limitation of Activities of and Requiring of Reports from Nippon Seiko Company, Ltd. This will necessitate financial as well as industrial reorganization of the industry.

4 Request stated in paragraph 5 of CLO 5496 (ECI - F) paragraph 2 cited above is being studied. The Imperial Japanese Government will later be directed which plant of Amatsuji Steel Ball Manufacturing Company may be permitted to continue production of non-precision steel balls.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1375)

JZR/CDR/blf

19 November 1946

1. Reference SCAPIN 1136 which listed 32 bearing plants for reparations. Two factories belonging to Nihon Seiko Company, Ltd, the Fujisawa and Tomagawa, were exempted as their combined capacities were judged best suited to meet the FEC standards established for the industry.

2. a. The Imperial Japanese Government objected to the announced program on the basis that it would leave a single company incomplete control of an industry critical to the entire mechanical development of Japan.

b. The possible dangers of such a monopoly are recognized. In addition it is undesirable for SCAP to place itself on record approving such a concentration of control when it can be avoided.

c. CLO note of 18 October 1946 also requested retention of one steel ball plant to manufacture non-precision type balls for bicycles, etc. The designation of such a plant will be announced through regular channels when all information is available.

3. This directive notifies the Imperial Japanese Government that the Nippon Seiko Company, Ltd has been placed under the surveillance of this Headquarters, thus necessitating a financial as well as industrial reorganization of the bearing industry.

4. Concurrences: ESS/AC

J. Z. R.

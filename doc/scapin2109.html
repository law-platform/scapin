GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 004.03 (27 Jun 50) ESS/IND

(SCAPIN-2109)

APO 500

27 June 1950

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
SUBJECT :Rescission of SCAPINS 1512, 1562 and 1646

1. Reference the following memoranda for the Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers:

a. AG 004.03 (7 Feb 47) ESS/TD, SCAPIN 1512, 7 February 1947, subject: Cotton Textile Capacity.

b. AG 004.03 (8 Mar 47) ESS/TD, SCAPIN 1562, 8 March 1947, subject: Limitation on Size of Spinning Companies.

c. AG 004.03 (2 May 47) ESS/TD, SCAPIN 1646, 2 May 1947, subject: Limitation on Size of Spinning Companies.

2. The above SCAPINS pertaining to limitations set on the cotton textile industry spindle capacity are hereby rescinded.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

K.B.BUSH
Brigadier General, USA
Adjutant General

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 2109)

WFM/WTR/lf

27 June 1950

1. SCAPIN 1512, 7 Feb 47, 1562, 8 Mar 47, and 1646, 2 May 47, enumerate the so-called "Big Ten" cotton spinning companies and fix the number of spindles each company shall rehabilitate within an overall interim level of 4, 000, 000 units, which was approved by SCAPIN 1512. SCAPINS 1562 and 1646 restrict the placement of orders for new equipment and confine deliveries of such materials to independent cotton spinning companies and control the acquisition of spare parts and replacement units.

2. The companies alluded to are now considered to be free from restrictive measures and the present condition of the internal economy warrants freer operation. Five of the "Big Ten" companies have already reached their quota and the others are progressively attaining their allotment with complete fulfillment scheduled within the next few months. The independent companies have installed their allotment and for the most part own too few to be operated competitively, so have a need to expand for economic operation.

3. Reference to the record indicates that the rehabilitation program authorized by SCAPIN 1512, 7 February 1947, was predicated largely upon the availability of funds. Furthermore, as indicated in its reference, this SCAPIN was based on CLO memorandum No. 5505 (ECI), 18 October 1946, covering a "Three-year Textile Industry Rehabilitation Plan." This three-year period expired in 1949.

4. Under present conditions the 4,000,000 spindles by SCAPIN 1512 are insufficient because:

a. Limitation is only one-third of pre-war capacity.

b. Quantity gauged on a per capita basis with other textile producing countries is totally disproportionate despite the fact that greater dependence must be placed on exportable volume.

c. Higher population increase ratio and the consequent need for ample domestic volume which cannot be furnished by the present limitation and provide for expanded exports as well.

d. Westernization tendency in costuming which enhances cotton fabric utilization.

5. FEC determined on 23 January 1947 that as a matter of policy the peaceful needs of the Japanese people should be defined as being substantial the standard of living prevailing in Japan during the period of 1930-1934. As compared with this period the March 1950 textile production was only 28.6 percent.

6. Raw cotton import requirements as revised in March 1950 were established on the basis of increasing the level of cotton textile consumption to 2.2 pounds per person for household consumption. Army Memorandum for Record, 29 December 1949, commits the DA to increasing production for domestic consumption to 2.9 pounds per person for FY 50 and 3.4 pounds for FY 51 to achieve a real base for economic stabilization in Japan. It is believed that the industry is fully capable of self control to the extent that installation of additional spindles will not be such as to result in over capacity relative to available domestic and export markets for production.

7. Radio C 56768, 24 June 1950, (SCAP to Senator Eastland) indicates that SCAP is taking steps to remove any ceiling limit on the number of spindles available to Japan. The attached SCAPIN executes such directions.

8. This is an administrative matter and no surveillance by the Eighth Army is required.

9. Request release of SCAPIN to Japanese Government without delay and prior to regular courier circulation.

10. Concurrences: None required.

W. T. R.

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
SUBJECT :Reduction of Governmental Expenditures.

1. Reference is made to Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers Memorandum for the Japanese Government, file AG 120.01 (12 Sep 47) ESS/FI, SCAPIN 1775, subject, “Reduction of Governmental Expenditures,” dated 12 September 1947.

2. Effective 1 April 1949 the provisions of this directive are applicable to unit-price and lump-sum contracts entered into by the Japanese Government for the purpose of obtaining supplies, materials, construction and/or services.

3. The purpose of this directive is to expedite payment by the Japanese Government to lump-sum and unit-price contractors and thereby create an incentive for contractors to submit bids on a lump-sum or unit price basis so that expenditures of the Japanese Government may be reduced through:

a. A predetermination and control of the total costs of contractual services.

b. Reduction or elimination of the added costs of costs-plus-a-fixed-fee contracts.

c. Reduction of administrative costs of the Japanese Government through reduced demands for audits of cost-plus-a-fixed-fee contract.

4. A competitive bid by a contractor at a specified total price or specified unit price will, upon acceptance by the Japanese Government, be recognized as a price legally established by the Japanese Government for the purpose of making payments on the particular contract involved. Payments may be made to contractors in excess of the total price (lump-sum) or the unit price specified in the contract where costs to the contractors for performance of the terms of the contract are increased due to increased wage scales and/or increased price of materials provided:

a. Provision for payments in excess of the total price or the unit price specified in the contract is included in the terms of the contract, amendments thereto, or in a supplemental contract.

b. The increased scales and/or prices are legally established or recognized by the Japanese Government to be legal.

c. The increase or increases, in scales and/or prices for which excess payment is claimed by the contractor, occured subsequent to the submission of the bid on which the contract is awarded and under which excess payment is claimed and were applicable during the period of operations under that contract.

d. The contractor amply justifies the excess expenditure claimed by him but in so doing is not required to justify any cost factors not contributing to the expenditures for which excess payment is claimed.

5. Any amendments or supplements to a contract which are written on a unit-price or lump-sum payment basis will be governed by the provisions of this directive.

6. For the purpose of determining the adequacy and fairness of bid prices, the Japanese Government, prior to asking for bids on a lump sum or unit price contract, will compute the cost factors upon which the lump sum or unit price should be based, including legally established prices and prevailing wage scales and will compare bids when received with the costs so computed by the Japanese Government. Bids which are in excess of the costs as computed by the Japanese Government will be rejected.

7. For the purpose of compliance with paragraph 1a of the Memorandum for the Japanese Government referred to in paragraph 1 above, prevailing prices as determined by the Japanese Government may be used as legally established prices for goods or commodities where a published official price does not exist for such goods or products. Methods for determination of the prevailing price shall be subject to the review of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1775/2)

GD/R&P/GD/hv

8 March 1949

1. This directive amends SCAPIN 1775, dated 12 Sep 1947, subject as above, to instruct the Japanese Government to recognize as a legally established price an accepted competetive bid at a specified total price or specified unit price.

2. The present Japanese Law 171 adopted to implement SCAPIN 1775 effectively precludes a bid system by requiring each contractor or supplier to justify each bill on the basis of a detailed itemization of the labor and materials components and the legal prices thereof.

3. The proposed memorandum instructs the Japanese Government to reject any bids which are in excess of legally established prices and wages for the computed cost factors. Once accepted by the government, payments of the bid price are to be made without requiring a justification of the cost factors. Payments in excess of the bid price will only be made when provided for in the contract, or supplemental contract, and must be separately justified.

4. Payments to contractors should be considerably expedited under the terms of this memorandum. The procedure provided is fundamental to present G-4 policy to place Occupation Force service contracts on an end-item or end-service basis to permit better control over Japanese Government expenditures under such contracts.

5. Within policy.

6. Concurrences: Govt Section ("N. E x O"), ESS (WKI)

7. Completes action on G-4 Journal No. 78607.

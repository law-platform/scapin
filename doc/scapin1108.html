APO 500

6 August 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Dissolution of Control Associations (Tosei Kai) and Authorization to Establish Government Allocation Agency together with Necessary Control Organs within Specific industries.

1. In order to release industry from certain wartime production controls and to establish more democratic methods of increasing the production of essential materials and commodities for the reconstruction of a peacetime economy, the Imperial Japanese Government is directed to take the neccessary action to accomplish the following within ninety (90) days from date of this Memorandum:

a. Dissolve existing Control Associations (Tosei Kai) and repeal or rescind all laws, ordinances, regulations and ministerial ordinances relating thereto.

b. Preserve the complete records of such Associations within the respective bureaus of the Government now supervising such Associations.

c. Concurrently with the actions taken in accordance with the provisions of subparagraph 1a, above, estabish such public agencies, divisions and procedures within or under the Economic Stabilization Board as may be necessary to effectuate, under the coordinated direction and supervision of said Board and in corporation with the particular ministries concerned allocations to and within such specific industries as may be selected by such Board for the assured production of essential materials and goods, and to assure that such allocations are adjusted to meet desired production schedules; supervise the establishment and operation of democratically organized and operated trade associations to be used as temporary stabilization devices within the specified industries selected by the Economic Stabilizalion Board for the purpose of allocating essential raw and semiprocessed materials within those industries.

2. The Imperial Japanese Government will furnish the following information to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, in quintuplicate, typed in English on 8" x 11" paper, within ninety (90) days from the date of this Memorandum.

a. Complete list of all laws ordinances, regulations and ministerial ordinaces repealed or rescinded in compliance with the provisions of subparagraph 1a, above.

b. Names of the responsible bureaus preserving the records of dissolved Control Associations (Tosei Kai) in compliance with the provisions of subparagraph 1b, above.

c. Name, structure, composition and method of operation of the specified agencies and description of procedures established in accordance with the provisions of subparagraph 1c, above.

d. List of the specific industries selected by the Government pursuant to authority granted in subparagraph 1c, above, to receive allocations of essential raw materials and goods.

e. Report of actions taken by the Government to establish the necessary trade associations to be used as temporary stabilization devices pursuant to the provisions of subparagraph 1c, above, togother with full information as to the structure, methods of operation and specific functions of each such trade association, and the measures taken to assure full, non-discriminatory participation in the decisions of such trade associations on the part of all small, medium and large-sized enterprises involved.

3. The existence of all industry trade associations as temporary stabilization devices within selected industries, and the public agencies established by the Imperial Japanese Government in compliance with the provisions of this Memorandum shall be limited to a period of one (1) year from date of this Memorandum, except as authorized by the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

4. This Memorandum will not be construed as affecting in any respect whatsoever, the continued functioning of the Civilian Merchant Marine Committee, as created pursuant to Memorandum for the Imperial Japanese Goeernment from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, file AG 334 (9 Nov. 45) GD (SCAPIN-256), subjcct: “Appointment of Civilian Merchant Marine Committee.”

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1108)

WFM/JMcIH/GFS/kf

19 July 1946

1. Control Associations (Tosei Kai) were established under the Important Industrial Bodies Ordinance (Juyo Sangyo Dantai Rei) 29 August 1941, revised 4 July 1945, promulgated in accordance with Article 18 of National General Mobilization Law (Kokka Sodoin Ho) revised, Law No.19, 1941. The purpose of such Associations was to bring under Government control the key industries of Japan to facilitate the war effort. In operation each of the Associations in its respective industry had the authority to fix prices, make allocations, control expansion, consolidate plants, and provide for the equipping of industry, under Government direction, for war purposes. The following list shows the industries and names of the Associations which were in existence 15 August 1945:

Automobile Control Association (Jidosha Tosei Kai)

Cement Control Association (Semento Tosei Kai)

Chemical Industry Control Association (Kagaku Kogyo Tosei Kai)

Coal Control Association (Sekitan Tosei Kai)

Electric Machinery Control Association (Denki Kikai Tosei Kai)

Fibre Control Association (Sen-i Tosei Kai)

Fuelgas Control Association (Gasu Tosei Kai)

Hides and Leather Control Association (Hikaku Tosei Kai)

Industrial Machinery Control Association (Sangyo Kikai Tosei Kai)

Iron and Steel Control Association (Tekko Tosei Kai)

Light Metals Control Association (Keikinzoku Tosei Kai)

Metal Industry Control Association (Kinzoku Kogyo Tosei Kai)

Mining Industry Control Association (Kozan Tosei Kai)

Petroleum Control Association (Sekiyu Tosei Kai)

Railway and Tramway Control Association (Tetsudo Kido Tosei Kai)

Rolling Stock Control Association (Sharyo Tosei Kai)

Rubber Control Association (Gomu Tosei Kai)

Shipbuilding Control Association (Zosen Tosei Kai)

2. Following the surrender, a number of these Associations became virtually inoperative. However, with the exception of those Control Associations directed to be dissolved by specific memoranda for the Imperial Japanese Government from the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, all of these Associations have a legal existence. They are, to a certain extent, operating as the only authorized economic controls in Japanese industry at the national level. The Silk Control Association, the Iron and Steel Control Association, and the Rubber Control Association have been ordered by Memoranda for the Imperial Japanese Government from SCAP to be dissolved and are now in the process of dissolution. In conformity with Joint Chiefs of Staff directive 1380/15, 3 November 1945, and the policies of SCAP, it is desired to dissolve these remaining Control Associations as soon as practicable.

3. Owing to a dearth of raw materials, as well as a serious shortage of consumers goods, it is necessary to direct the allocations of such materials as are available, to the production of essential materials and consumers goods.

4. The suggestion of continuing the Tosei Kai in these functions during the reconversion period was abandoned because:

a. In operation they cannot adequately exercise control over allocations.

b. The organizations were so corrupt, from a managerial point of view, that industry was refusing to comply with the orders of the Associations.

c. The Tosei Kai performed so many undesirable functions that mere refurbishment to perform allocation functions only would necessarily be more difficult than the creation of a new allocations system.

d. The Tosei Kai, having been organized for war production pruposes, are poorly suited to peacetime production.

e. It is psychologically desirable to direct the abolition of these purely wartime control agencies and to allow industry to reconvert with as few restrictions as possible.

5. Many conferences with Japanese Government officials and industrialists indicate that important stimulus can be given to the production of necessary materials and goods, if industry is permitted to reorganize itself along democratic lines, also, that such a move will assist the essential industries in stabilizing themselves, by assurance of materials, thus allowing a fixing of wage policies, standards and prices. It is expected that the internal operations of such control organs within industry can be so directed by the responsible Government Bureaus, under the supervision of SCAP, that they will not, in this interim period, operate to the detriment of the general public by withholding reconversion, but will rather contribute to orderly reconversions as allowed by SCAP policies.

6. To accomplish these necessary strong measures of control, the establishment of priority allocations to specific industries by the Government, will throw into the hands of the latter the reins for guiding production along certain lines. This is in accordance with Joint Chiefs of Staff Directive 1380/15, 3 November 1945, and stated SCAP policies.

7. The attached Memorandum to the Imperial Japanese Government has been drafted with a view to:

a. Dissolving the Tosei Kai as undesirable wartime control mechanisms.

b. Permit re-establishment of any public controls over the allocation of raw materials as are necessary to produce certain specific materials and goods, but at the same time leaving non-essential production activities uncontrolled as to material allocations.

c. Establish, within the specific industries, a method by which industry itself can participate in the execution of the Government orders for allocation, the preparation of industrial plans, and industrial stabilization, with a minimum of Government direction.

8. The interests of USAFIK have been considered. This action does not affect the Korean area.

9. This is considered an administrative matter.

10. Concurrences: ESS/LE (Lt. Col. E. R. Minnich); GS (Dr. C. H. Peake); ESS/PC (Mr. W. S. Egekvist); ESS/IN (Mr. J. Z. Reday); NRS (Col. H. G. Schenck).

11. Approved by Deputy Chief of Staff, 31 July 1946.

J. McI. H.

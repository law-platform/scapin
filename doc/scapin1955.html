GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 471.86 (7 Dec 48) ESS/IND

(SCAPIN - 1955)

APO 500

5 January 1949

MEMORANDUM FOR :Japanese Government
SUBJECT :Manufacture and Use of Industrial Explosives

1. References are:

a. Memorandum for the Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, AG 471.83 (29 Dec 47) ESS/IN, SCAPIN 1836, subject: Manufacture of Industrial Explosives and Accessories, 29 December 1947.

b. Letter, CLCO No. 4050 (2P), to General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, subject: Application for Permission for Manufacture and Use of Industrial Explosives and Accessories for 1949, 7 December 1948.

2. Manufacture of the following types and quantities of explosives, to be used for industrial purposes only, is authorized for the period from 1 January 1949 to 31 December 1949:

Type of Explosive Quantity Authorized

Gelatin Dynamite and Hagi Dynamite 8,700 metric tons

Ammonia Dynamite and Ammonium Nitrate Explosives 9,100 metric tons

Carlit 2,500 metric tons

Reprocessed Military High Explosives 95 metric tons

Black Powder 510 metric tons

Smokeless Powder 10 metric tons

TOTAL 20,915 metric tons

A maximum of 15 metric tons of black powder may be utilized in the production of fireworks and party devices.

3. Authorization is granted to manufacture the following types and quantities of explosives accessories during the period from 1 January 1949 to 31 December 1949:

Type of Accessory Quantity Authorized

Fuse 33,000 kilometers

Blasting Caps for Use with Fuse and Electric Detonators 100,000,000 pieces

Electric Detonators 40,000,000 pieces

Percussion Caps 6,400,000 pieces

Friction Tubes for Whaling 22,500 pieces

Det mating Fuse 480 kilometers

Flame Signal Fuses for Railways 250,000 pieces

Signal Detonators for Railways 250,000 pieces

Signal Firing Devices for Ships 19,000 pieces

Caps for Starting Signal Cap Pistols 20,000,000 pieces

Shotgun Shells for Pest Extermination 450,000 pieces

4. The Japanese Government will submit to the Commanding General, Eighth Army, application for authority to operate plants necessary for the manufacture of the explosives and accessories authorized by this memorandum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

/s/ A. J. Rehe
for /t/ R. M. LEVY,
Colonel, A.G.D.,
Adjutant General

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1955)

ABO/WRS/CAR/mn

18 December 1948

1. Directive No. 3, SCAPIN 47, 22 Sep 45, requires the Japanese Government to submit applications for the manufacture of essential industrial explosives. Authority to manufacture 18, 900 metric tons of industrial explosives and various quantities of explosives accessories was given in SCAPIN 1836, AG 471.86 subj: Manufacture of Industrial Explosives and Accessories, 29 Dec 47. This authorization expires 3.1. December 1948. Ltr, CLCO 4060 (2P), subj; Application for Permission for Manufacture and use of Industrial Explosives and Accessories for 1949, 7 Dec 48, requests authorization to manufacture 20,449 metric tons of explosives plus various quantities of accessories during 1949.

2. Authorization for manufacture of the industrial explosives indicated in this memorandum is based on the following justifications:

a. Intended use will fill important requirements of the Japanese economy.

b. Amounts authorized are considered minimum requirements.

c. The Japanese system of controls and licensing facilitates any desired checks on the manufacture. A detailed plan, listing the plants to be used and the amounts scheduled for each plant, has been prepared. This plan is based on a Japanese proposal, modified by this Division.

3. This memo authorized the manufacture of 20,915 metric tons of explosives, an eleven percent increase over the amount authorized for 1948, in addition to various quantities of accessories. The principal explosives authorized are based on the following proposed uses:

a. Mining industries: 40,000,000 metric tons of coal

3,500,000 metric tons of lignite

9,400,000 metric tons of metal ores

10,000,000 metric tons of non-metal ores

b. Public works: road construction, river improvement, tunnels, erosion control.

c. Civil works: harbor development, quarry.

d. Forestry and agriculture: Forest roads, embankments, stump removal, pest extermination, irrigation, land reclamation.

e. Other uses: rail and ship transportation, hydroelectric powers, fishery, steel industry, research uses, fireworks and party devices, and starting pistol caps.

4. Ltr to 8th Army includes a list of approved plants and maximum quantities to be produced by these plants.

5. Special surveillance by 8th Army is required to assure that amounts specified in production plan are not exceeded.

6. Concurrences: G-4 (Plans and Policies) - Lt. Col. G. O. De Young.

C. A. Rackus - 26-8639

APO 500

11 June 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
SUBJECT :Payment of Interest on Loans Made in Connection with Blocked Accounts.

1. Reference is made to Memorandum for the Imperial Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, file AG 121.7 (17 May 46) ESS/FI, dated 17 May 1946, subject:“Payment of Interest on Blocked Accunts.”

2. Paragraph 3 of referenced memorandum is amended to permit payment of interest on any portions of loans made by banks or other financial institutions to any company, organization or governmental agency which were paid out to claimants in cash or by withdrawals from blocked accounts.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1011)

WKL/JCS/cab

5 June 1946

1. Memorandum for the Imperial Japanese Government from GHQ, SCAP, file AG 121.7 (17 May 46) ESS/FI, dated 17 May 1946, subject: "Payment of Interest on Blocked Accounts" prohibited, effective 20 May 1946, payment of interest on loans made by banks or other financial institutions to insurance companies when the purpose of the Loan was to pay claims arising from production or supply of war materials, from war damage or from construction or conversion of war plants.

2. Insurance companies and the Central Association of Damage Insurance Corporations were entrusted by the Japanese Government with the payment of such insurance money. These insurance companies borrowed funds from their respective banks and paid 5,000 yen per claim in cash, the remainder being deposited in blocked account. These companies have been paying four per cent interest to the commercial banks who in turn borrowed from the Bank of Japan or other banking institutions to whom interest must be paid. Hence, as a matter of equity in such instances, payments of interest to banks should be continued. When the claim was paid into a blocked account, however, it is equitable that interest should be stopped or both the blocked bank account and on any loans which created the account.

3. The interests of Korea have been considered. This action does not concern the Korean area.

4. This is an administrative matter and does not require surveillance of the Eighth Army.

W. K. L.

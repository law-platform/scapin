GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 323.31 (14 Mar 46) ESS/AC

(SCAPIN-815)

APO 500

14 March 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Dissolution of Koeki Eidan and Nippon Zakka Boeki Shinko Kaisha.

1. The Imperial Japanese Government is directed to liquidate the Koeki Eidan (National Trade Corporation) and its subsidiary organization, The Nippon Zakka Boeki Shinko Kaisha (Japan Miscellaneous Goods Trade Encouragement Company). In liquidation, however, no property in the following category will be sold disposed of or transfered, without approval of this Headquarters.

a. All property in the custody of Koeki Eidan or Nippon Zakka Boeki Shinko Kaisha, formerly belonging to governments or nationals of the United Nations, which was acquired after 7 July 1937 by any Japanese agency through duress, forced sale, confiscation, as booty of war, or transferred through the enemy property custodian.

b. All property held for or received from nationals or governments at war with the United Nations after 2 September 1939, which is now in custody of Koeki Eidan or Nippon Zakka Boeki Shinko Kaisha.

2. A complete inventory of the properties in the above-named categories showing the source from which each item was obtained, as well as the name of the prior owner, shall be submitted to this Headquarters on or before 15 May 1946 in quintuplicate, typed in English.

3. Such materials and goods as do not fall within the above-named excepted categories shall be distributed through the normal distribution channels in accordance with the present method of effecting such distribution.

4. The staffs of the named organizations shall be used for the purpose of liquidation under the direction of competent government authority. The name of such competent government authority shall be furnished this Headquarters on or before 20 May 1946. All records of the named organizations shall be preserved by the competent government authority directing the liquidation.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

B.M.FICHI
Brigadiar General,AGD,
Adjutant General

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN-815)

6 March 1946

1. In connection with the establishment of the Boeki Cho, an organization within the Japanese Government for the purpose of implementing import-export programs, and in conformity with JCS directives to dissolve government control organs, it is necessary to liquidate the organizations set up by the government for wartime control of imports and exports. There are two (2) such organs, the Eidan and the Nippon Zakka Boeki Shinko Kaisha.

2. Koeki Eidan was established in July 1943 for the purpose of taking over the functions of the Essential Materials Supervision Corporation and, in addition, to handle all shipments into and out of Japan. This agency has the authority of stockpiling raw materials and the duty of purchasing materials coming into Japan, as well as the handling of goods for export. By comparison, its functions resemble those of the United States Commercial Company, the Alien Property Custodian, and some of the Reconstruction Finance Corporation's subsidiaries, as we know them in the United States. It is a large cumbersome organization now operating only for the purpose of distributing through normal channels such materials as it had stockpiled during the war. Conversations with Mr. Ishida, president of Koeki Eidan, and Mr. Mukai, president of Boeki Cho, have indicated that liquidation can be handled in an orderly fashion by depleting the stockpiles through normal distribution channels. The new Japanese Government organization, Boeki Cho, has absorbed the functions of the Koeki Eidan. Hence, liquidation of Koeki Eidan would be under the direction of Mr. Mukai. It was indicated by both the aforementioned officials that liquidation of Koeki Eidan would not interfere with or unduly burden the operation of Boeki Cho.

3. The Nippon Zakka Boeki Shinko Kaisha is a subsidiary corporation of Koeki Eidan, having to do with the purchase of sundries goods for export, as well as the handling of certain sundries goods for distribution within Japan. As a subsidiary corporation depending entirely upon Koeki Eidan for its existence, it must be liquidated at the same time. Likewise, the manner in which it presently deals with sundries goods is not satisfactory to either Boeki Cho or the Import-Export Division of ESS, SCAP. It is no longer a useful public agency and should, therefore, be liquidated.

4. Inasmuch as Koeki Eidan and its subsidiary received property from all sources, it is deemed necessary to block transfers or dispositions of any properties turned over to them which may have been acquired through duress, confiscation, or as booty of war. Likewise, certain property was turned over to Koeki Eidan by the German Government and Nationals after the surrender in Europe. Transfers or liquidations of such properties should be blocked. It is deemed essential, however, that other legitimately acquired goods and commodities to sustain the Japanese economy be allowed to flow freely into the channels of distribution, since this is one of the primary stockpiles in Japan.

5. Both of the above-mentioned organizations proposed for liquidation are in the nature of import-export and commercial controls which should be dissolved as quickly as possible to facilitate the operations of the new agency, Boeki Cho. Such liquidation is in accordance with the policy outlined by the Joint Chiefs of Staff for the elimination of government controls in commerce and industry.

6. This is considered an administrative matter.

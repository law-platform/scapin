GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 014.33 (13 Jan 47) GA

(SCAPIN-1462)

APO 500

13 January 1947

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Repatriation of German and Austrian Nationals

1. References:

a. Memorandum from the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, file AG 370.05 (7 May 46) GC (SCAPIN 927), dated 7 May 1946, subject: “Repatriation,” as amended.

b. Nominal rolls of German and Austrian nationals to be repatriated (Inclosure No. 1.).

c. Medical and Sanitary Procedures (Inclosure No. 2).

d. Property Regulations and Procedures (Inclosure No. 3).

2. The Imperial Japanese Government will carry out instructions contained in this memorandum and inclosures thereto to effect the repatriation of persons listed in Inclosure No. 1 as follows:

a. Appoint an English-speaking Japanese custodian for each family subject to repatriation and for each individual subject to repatriation but not a member of a family subject to repatriation. Custodians will be notified by officers in charge of Eighth United States Army inspecting teams when to move in and live on designated premises, at which time custodians will move in and assume custody of all property, movable and immovable. Custodians will guard and conserve property and process it in accordance with instructions contained in Inclosure No. 3. The Imperial Japanese Government will be responsible for maintenance of custodians during the period they are on duty.

b. Furnish police guards to maintain surveillance over each residence until movement of repatriates to reception center is complete, in order to prevent removal of property.

c. Provide transportation for repatriates listed in Inclosure No 1 and baggage authorized to accompany them (See Inclosure No 3) from their present addresses to the reception center at Uraga, Kanagawa in accordance with the provisions of Annex 111, reference 1a above. Transportation will be arranged so that the repatriates will arrive at the reception center in numbers and on dates as specified by the Commanding General, Eighth United States Army.

d. Subsist, maintain and provide adequate medical care for all repatriates during movement to and during their stay at the reception center. Initially, a minimum of two weeks supply of western style rations for each repatriate will be available at the reception center and additional amounts furnished as required.

3. Request for guards for the movement of repatriates should be made by local Japanese Government officials in accordance with the provisions of paragraph 5, Annex No. 111, of reference 1a above. Local Allied military authorities have been directed to place guards aboard trains scheduled specifically to carry repatriates to the reception center only when, in the opinion of such authorities, the number of repatriates warrants such guards.

4. Transportation, processing and outloading of repatriates will, insofar as practicable, be governed by the provisions of reference 1a above.

5. The Commanding General, Eighth United States Army, has been authorized to exempt from repatriation those nationals who, upon examination by United States Army or Allid medical authorities, are considered physically unfit to travel. Immediately upon notification of repatriation, each repatriate will be informed of this, and if unfit for travel will be directed to report to the nearest military government unit for physical examination. In the event that any member of a family is exempted due to physical or mental infirmities, all members of that immediate family, including mother, father, dependent children and other dependents will be exempted also.

6. The Imperial Japanese Government will carry out the intsructions contained in this memorandum and inclosures thereto under the supervision and administrative direction of the Commanding General, Eighth United States Army. A liaison agent from the Central Liaison Office will be designated to report to the Commanding General, Eighth United States Army, upon receipt of this memorandum to effect direct liaison between the Imperial Japanese Government and the Eighth United States Army until the completion of repatriation of the foreign nationals listed in Inclosure No. 1.

7. Upon completion of this repatriation, the Imperial Japanese Government will report immediately to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers the names of all persons repatriated and persons whose names appear in Inclosure No. 1 but who were not repatriated, with reasons therefor in each case.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 306 (21 Oct 46) ESS/FI

(SCAPIN-1282)

APO 500

21 October 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Guarantee of Deposits of United Nations Nationals.

1. Reference is Memorandum for Imperial Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, AG 091.112 (13 Sep 45) MG, 13 September 1945, subject: “Protection of Allied and Axis Property.”

2. The Imperial Japanese Government is hereby directed to grarantee in full the deposits of United Nations nationals in financial institutions notwithstanding the revised regulations of the Emergency Financial Measures Ordinance, 11 August 1946, or any other associated legislation.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1282)

WKL/TEB/flg

2 October 1946

1. Memorandum for Imperial Japanese Government from GHQ, SCAP, AG 091.112 (13 Sep 45) MG, 13 September 1945, subject: "Protection of Allied and Axis Property" requires the Japanese Government to preserve and protect the property of United Nation and Axis Nationals.

2. The Japanese Government at the present time is introducing in the Diet legislation providing for the reconstruction and reorganization of financial institutions. The object of such legislation is to provide procedures for the liquidation and reorganization of financial institutions adversely affected by War Indemnity cancellations. As a specific example, the revised regulations of the Emergency Financial Measures Ordinance, 11 August 1946, provides for the separation of all deposit accounts in financial institutions in Japan into 1st and 2nd Restricted Accounts. 1st Restricted accounts, with a maximum limit of ¥15,000 per individual or ¥32,000 per household, continue to be subject to an anti-inflationary measure. 2nd Restricted accounts, funds in excess of the limits for 1st Restricted accounts, are subject to legislation, now before the Diet, which provides for bank reorganizations. Such legislation provides for allocation of losses, first to surplus and reserves, then to capital account and finally to creditors and depositors. In order to protect the small depositors a guarantee of a minimum deposit (1st Restricted Account) is proposed by the Japanese Government.

3. Although United Nation nationals will not be subject to Japanese extraordinary taxes, the proposed system of guarantee of bank deposits may cause money losses to such persons unless special instructions are issued by SCAP.

4. By Check Sheet from CPC/FP to ESS/FI, 27 August 1946, subject: "Protection of Bank Deposits in Japan of United Nation Nationals, " the question is raised whether SCAP should require the Japanese Government to guarantee the deposits of United Nation nationals in full instead of the minimum guarantee afforded to depositors generally. CPC recommends the insertion of an exempting clause in the proposed legislation for deposits belonging to the nationals of the United Nations. It was suggested by CPC that this question be referred to WARCOS for clarification and guidance from the FEC.

5. CPC advised ESS that an estimate of the total amount of the yen, property of United Nation nationals, that is on deposit in Japan and subject to the provisions of the proposed plan for reorganization of financial institutions is ¥50,000,000. Total yen deposits in Japanese banks amount to ¥163,354.3 millions of yen plus postal savings deposits, ¥53,224.7 millions of yen, total of both 216,579 millions of yen. This estimated amount of United Nations nationals' deposits is only 1/40 of 1% of total deposits in Japan which should have little effect on the proposed reorganization.

6. The guarantee does not apply to Axis nationals, the accounts of United Nation governments, puppet governments, or governmental financial institutions. Deposits of this type (except Axis nationals) should be considered as governmental claims and as such should be included as part of their over-all claim against Japan. These claims will probably be settled by a Reparation Commission, the FEC or some other similar body in the future. A guarantee in full now would give these claims preferential treatment and would be hard to justify by SCAP. A specific example of such accounts are:

a. Special Yen Exchange Accounts maintained in the Yokohama Specie Bank, Tokyo Office by

(1) Banque de l'Indochine, Saigon Branch

Balance.............................¥1,300,000,000

(2) Central Reserve Bank, Pekin

Balance.............................¥ 63,000,000

(3) Federal Reserve Bank, Shanghai

Balance.............................¥ 90,000,000

These accounts are the result of arrangements made on a governmental level for foreign exchange transactions between the areas of the above mentioned banks. These banks, acting in their capacity as central banks for their areas, established special yen exchange accounts through which all official foreign exchange transactions were cleared. ESS has been informed by the Yokohama Specie Bank that the greater part of their present balances are the result of wartime financial transactions.

b. Puppet Government Accounts, such as the account maintained by the Puppet Philippine Government with the Yokohama Specie Bank for ¥3,000,000.

7. Washington radio WCL-30316, 25 July 1946, from JCS furnished statement of policy to effect that no capital levy should be imposed in respect to property owned by United Nation nationals and that if exceptional circumstances warranted imposition of capital levy against such property that the matter should be referred for consideration to the FEC.

8. SCAP radio C-63938, 8 August 1946, in reply advised that: Capital levy was being imposed on individuals only excluding United Nation nationals, there was being imposed concurrently a 100% tax on war indemnity claims against the government; such measures together with guarantee of small deposits and establishment of government Finance Agency are designed to reorganize banking system and stabilize Japanese Economy generally and safeguards would be achieved to protect allied interests in connection with the program.

9. Insofar as the tax legislation was concerned, Washington radio WCL-30316 stated that no capital levy should be imposed in respect to property owned by United Nation nationals. The proposed bank reorganization should be considered in the same light as the taxation measures. This legislation provides for reorganization of all banks, and without any legal obligation or otherwise, simply in order to protect small depositors, the Japanese Government has guaranteed up to a total of ¥32,000. per household. The losses in question arise from the overall economic conditions now prevailing in Japan which necessitate the reorganization of the banks. It is considered that guaranteeing deposits of United Nation nationals is no different in principle to the policy established on the payment of capital levy taxes or extraordinary taxes which exempted United Nation nationals from such taxes. This extraordinary war tax was for the purpose of paying for the cost of the war and as such United Nation nationals should rightfully be excluded. Losses arising from a general bank reorganization may also be considered to be of an extraordinary character as they are the result of cancellation of all war indemnities and claims.

10. SCAP is charged with the responsibility to preserve and protect the assets of United Nation Nationals. To date, SCAP has done all that is required of it to preserve and protect such assets. With this in mind ESS prepared a radio for dispatch to WARCOS, subject: "Protection of deposits of United Nation Nationals" requesting authority to require the Japanese Government to guarantee deposit of United Nation nationals. Check Sheet from ESS to C/S, file No. 123.3 (16 Sep 46) ESS/FI, 16 September 1946, subject: "Protection of Deposits of United Nation Nationals" outlined the reasons for sending proposed radio to WARCOS. Paragraph 3 recommended that if the Chief of Staff decided that this question may properly be resolved by SCAP, a memorandum be issued to the Japanese Government directing it to guarantee the deposits of United Nation nationals.

11. Check Sheet from C/S to ESS, 18 September 1946, approves the issuance of this memorandum to the Japanese Government.

12. This is an administrative matter and no surveillance by the EIGHTH Army is required.

13. The interests of USAFIK are not concerned.

14. Concurrences: CPC (F. Sibert, W. W. S., D. H. B.)

W. K. L.

ADDENDUM:

If draft memo is approved it will not be issued to the IJG until after the diet passes all tax measures. Finance Division is aware of this proper timing.

W. R. Exec. Off.

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 121.7 (17 May 46) ESS/FI

(SCAPIN-965)

APO 500

17 May 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
SUBJECT :Payment of interest on blocked accounts.

1. Reference is made to memorandum for the Imperial Japanese Government from General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, file AG 121.7 (24 Nov 45) ESS/FI, dated 24 November 1945, subject: “Elimination of War Profits and Reorganization of National Finances.”

2. Effective 20 May 1946, no interest will be paid on amounts credited to blocked bank accounts in connection with payments made on claims arising from the production or supply of war materials, from war damage, or from construction or conversion of war plants.

3. Also effective 20 May 1946, no interest will be paid on loans made by banks or other financial institutions to insurance companies when the purpose of the loan was to supply funds to pay claims arising from the production or supply of war materials, from war damage, or from construction or conversion of war plants.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

B.M. FITCH,
Brigadier General, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 965)

WFM/JCS/fae

1. Japanese banks are paying interest on balances of certain accounts blocked under the provisions of Memorandum for the Imperial Japanese Government from GHQ, SCAP file AG 121.7 (24 Nov 45) ESS/FI dated 24 November 1945, subject "Elimination of War Profits and Reorganization of National Finances". Accounts blocked under reference memorandum are claims arising from the supply or production of war materials, from war damage and the construction or conversion of war plants. The only blocked accounts on which payment of interest has been stopped are balances arising from the supply or production of war materials, from war damage and the construction or conversion of war plants. The only blocked accounts on which payment of interest has been stopped are balances arising from war risk damage claims. This has been done by action of the Japanese Government.

2. Payment of interest on these accounts should be discontinued immediately. Owners of such accounts have received settlement of their claims before any final disposition of war indemnity claims has been determined. Furthermore it is most probable that these balances will be substantially recaptured through taxation, and payments constitute an advance of funds to meet debts or tax liabilities and consequently should not cause a drain on the banks. Creation of these deposits does not increase the income yielding assets of the banks, as payment is effected through non-interest bearing certificates. At the same time interest should be stopped on any loans made by banks or other financial institutions to insurance companies against such claims.

3. This action does not affect Korea.

4. This is an administrative matter and does not require surveillance by the Eighth Army.

5. No concurrences necessary.

F. H. J.

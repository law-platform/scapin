29 March 1950

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
Subject :Illegal Import and Export Trade

1. Rescissions:

a. Memorandum for the Japanese Government, AG 091.31 (4 Jun 46) ESS/PC, SCAPIN 996, 4 June 1946, subject: Illegal Import and Export Trade.

b. Memorandum for the Japanese Government, AG 383.8 (4 Jun 46) ESS/PD, SCAPIN 996/1, 12 July 1949, subject: Illegal Import and Export Trade-Disposition of Critical Materials and Rationed Goods.

2. The Japanese Government will continue its responsibility for the prevention of illicit import and export trade and will dispose of goods seized in such trade in accordance with the laws and orders of the Japanese Government and/or the regulations and memoranda of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

K.B.Bush
Brigadier General, USA,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 2090)

ECH/MWH/JTL/ftk

10 March 1950

1. The proposed SCAPIN rescinds SCAPINS 996 and 996/1 in order to remove impediments to expeditious disposal of seized illegal trade goods by the Japanese Government in accordance with existing laws and regulations.

2. SCAPIN 996 requires the Japanese Government to:

a. Store, protect and preserve all goods, wares and merchandise, except foodstuffs which are in danger of spoilage, pending determination of judicial action affecting the property.

b. Upon judicial determination that such goods are the subject of illegal imports or export trade, they shall be confiscated and consumer goods placed in regular channels of distribution.

c. Confiscated money, precious stones, etc., turned over to the Bank of Japan for the account of the Japanese Government.

d. Other items and durable goods, such as machinery and heavy equipment, reported to General Headquarters for instructions as to disposal.

3. SCAPIN 996 was issued 4 June 1946 prior to the promulgation of the Code of Criminal Procedure. The Code of Criminal Procedure became effective 1 January 1949. Chapter 9, Section 122 of the Code provides as follows:

"If there is apprehension that any articles seized which are liable to confiscation may be lost, destroyed or damaged, or if they are inconvenient to be held in custody, they may be sold by a court and the proceeds held in custody."

This provision in Japanese law established a procedure different from SCAPIN 996, Acting under SCAPIN 996 and due to the crowded conditions of court calendars and time-consuming appeals, the Japanese Government has accumulations of seized commodities exceeding \50,000,000 in value which are in danger of serious deterioration and which incurs storage and preservation charges disproportionate to its value. Therefore, by rescinding SCAPIN 996 the Japanese Government may, under the Code of Criminal Procedure, dispose of these accumulations.

4. All other provisions of SCAPIN 996 and 996/1 are covered by SCAPIN 2083, 2070 and 2055, Japanese Government customs laws, regulations covering utilization of surplus material and inventories of critical materials, tabs. C, D, E, I, S and K.

5. Rescission of SCAPIN 996 by the proposed SCAPIN would not affect the intent and purpose of the SCAPINS attached as tabs H, L, M, N, and O in which reference to 996 is made.

6. The proposed SCAPIN is in accordance with the recommendations of Legal Section, Check Note 2, concurred in byG-4, Check Note 3, tab P.

7. Concurrences: ESS/PD

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Application for Permission to Construct Small Sized Passenger Vessels.

1. Reference is CLO Letter No. 4730 (CM) dated 16 September 1946, subject as above, with Inclosure 1 and 2 requesting permission to construct twenty-eight (28) small passenger ferries as shown on Inclosure 1 and to convert two ex-Naval craft to passenger ferries as shown on Inclosure 2.

2. Permission is granted to construct the twenty-eight (28) passenger vessels as listed on Inclosure 1 of this letter. Permission to convert the two ex-Naval coastal defense boats to passenger ferries is not authorized at this time.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

1. Incl:

Construction Plan of Passenger Ferries.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1303)

ESS/IN JZR/GEM/mh

24 October 1946

1. Reference is C.L.O. letter file No. 4730 (CM) dated 16 September 1946, subject: "Application for Permission to Construct Small Sized Passenger Vessels," requesting authorization be given to construct twenty eight (28) New Passenger Ferries and to convert two (2) ex-Coastal Defense Vessels to Ferries."

2. Consideration is being made of a construction plan for 76 new Passenger Vessels, aggregating a total of 72,300 gross tons to relieve the transportation bottlenecks at various inter-island passenger ports. There are at present, 46 passenger ferries in operation in Japan with a total of 17,132 gross tons hauling approximately 221,300 passengers per month. According to transportation statistics on passenger ferry service there are approximately 674,700 persons unable to obtain transportation by passenger ferry each month. The proposed plan to construct 76 new ferries, when completed, will carry approximately 412,000 passengers per month or 51 percent of the alleged passenger ferry shortage.

3. Due to shortage of materials and financial difficulties the program is to be submitted in several construction plans, as materials and finances become available. This is Plan No. 1 of the Ferry Vessel Construction Program.

4. At a conference between ESS/IN (Mr. Valentine) and Transportation Section (Mr. Powell) various points concerning the Japanese ferry transportation service were discussed. The proposed passenger ferry construction program is to increase passenger service on present lines and to renew pre-war ferry routes which were discontinued due to war losses. At this time it was recommended that no new ferry routes be approved.

5. At a conference between ESS/IN (Mr. Valentine) and ComNavJap (Capt. Dunning and Capt Leach) the passenger ferry vessel construction program was discussed as well as the matter of tonnage, shaft horsepower and maximum speed of the subject vessels. A Conference also was held with G-2 (Capt. Meals) and favorable action on the request was recommended.

6. Permission will not be given at present for the conversion of the two ex-Naval Defense ships, as disposition of these vessels cannot be made until a decision is arrived at by the Joint Chiefs of Staff, on recommendations now in Washington for approval.

7. Action Taken: The Imperial Japanese Government is instructed to construct the twenty-eight (28) passenger vessels as listed on Inclosure 1 of the Memo to I. J. G.

8. Concurrences: G-4 (Cmdr. Hendrichs); G-2 (Capt. Meals USCG)

Transportation Section (Mr. Powell); ComNavJap (Capt. Dunning and Capt. Leach)

9. Routine surveillance is necessary on this activity and will be reported in normal weekly "Occupation Activities Reports."

J. Z. R.

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
Subject :Manufacture of Industrial Explosives.

1. Reference is made to memorandum to the Imperial Japanese Government, AG 471.86 (12 Nov 45) ESS/IN, dated 12 November 1945, subject, “Manufacture of Explosives for Use in Production of Coal.

2. You are hereby further authorized to manufacture explosives as shown in paragraph 3, below. These explosives will be used for industrial purposes as needed.

3. Manufacture of the following types and quantities of explosives is authorized for the period 31 January 1946 through 30 June 1946.

Types of Explosives and Accessories Quantities Authorized

Gelatine Dynamite and Hagi Dynamite 7,000 metric tons

Ammonia Dynamite and Ammonium Nitrate Explosives 2,300 metric tons

Carlit 1,900 metric tons

Black Powder for Blasting and Fuse 440 metric tons

Fuse 50,000 kilometers

Blasting Caps for Fuse and Electric Detonators 60,000,000 pieces

Electric Detonators 16,000,000 pieces

Detonating Fuse 220 kilometers

4. The Japanese Government will submit to the Commanding General, Eighth United States Army, applications for authority to operate the plants necessary for the manufacture of the authorized explosives.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

B.M.FICHI
Brigadiar General,AGD,
Adjutant General

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN-727)

ESS/IN RCAP/lom

1. The Imperial Japanese Government requested authority to manufacture industrial explosives in CLO No. 380, dtd 26 October 1945, subject, "Application for Manufacture and Use of Industrial Explosives." This request was studied in detail and a SCAP memorandum concerning explosives was issued (Memo to IJG, AG 471.86 (12 Nov 45) ESS/IN, dtd 12 Nov 45, subject, "Manufacture of Explosives for Use in Production of Coal"). This memorandum authorized immediate manufacture of small quantities of explosive for coal mining only, because of the critical shortage of coal. The memorandum for record for the November 14 memo indicated that a later directive would be issued concerning manufacture of explosives for all industrial purposes.

2. The manufacture of explosives as shown by the present memorandum is authorized because of these considerations:

a. The uses for which these explosives are intended will fill important needs of the Japanese economy. These uses include coal mining, metal mining, quarrying (principally for cement manufactures and reconstruction from war damage), construction and reconstruction of roads and bridges, and agricultural uses (irrigation and reclamation).

b. Quantities authorized are believed to be minimum needs. These quantities are somewhat lower than the latest Japanese estimates of their requirements, which are, in turn, below original Japanese figures.

c. Any desired checks on the manufactures can be easily carried out. The Japanese maintain a detailed system of controls and licensing at all levels, and a detailed plan for manufacture and use of the explosives has been prepared. The plan includes a listing of the plants to be used and the amount of production scheduled for each. An inherent control factor results from the fact that the plants to be used must operate at about 90% of capacity to reach the production authorized. The plants scheduled for use are the same plants authorized by the Sixth and Eighth Armies to manufacture explosives in accordance with the SCAP memorandum of 12 November, plus two factories required for manufacture of an additional type of explosive (Carlit).

3. The detailed plan for manufacturing the subject explosives is based on a Japanese proposal, only minor changes having been made by this division. The plan is being forwarded by separate correspondence to the Commanding General, Eighth Army for his use in considering the applications to be submitted by the Japanese Government in compliance with paragraph 4 of the attached memorandum.

4. The attached Memorandum to the Imperial Japanese Government was sent forward in its present form for approval by ESS on 26 December 1945.

5. The above information was forwarded as the original memo for record, 26 Dec. G-4 Section subsequently requested additional details of the use of the subject explosives. Detailed information is presented in par. 5 below. The major portion of this data is further substantiated by Natural Resources check sheets, inclosures B and C.

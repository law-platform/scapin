GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 676.3 (30 Aug 48) CCS

(SCAPIN-1964)

APO 500

17 January 1949

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
SUBJECT :Instructions Pertaining to Treatment of Telephone Rights and Prohibition of Resale of Public Telecommunications Services.

1. It is directed that on and after 15 February 1949 the Japanese Government cease to recognize as valid the transfer between users, of contracts, agreements, or registrations issued on or after that date which cover telecommunications services or facilities furnished from Government-owned telecommunications systems. The agreement entered into on or after 15 February 1949 by agencies of the Government to furnish telecommunications services or facilities to an individual or organization will be binding only insofar as the original recipient is concerned and will not be recognized nor honored if transferred from the original recipient to a second party by agreement between the two parties. This paragraph does not apply to such documents issued prior to 15 February 1949, or to their subsequent transfer or registration, or to telecommunications services furnished thereunder.

2. On and after 15 February 1949 the Japanese Government will not approve a greater number of applications for telecommunications service than can reasonably be expected to be fulfilled within three months after the date of approval of the application by the Japanese Government.

3. On and after 15 February 1949 telecommunications services furnished by the Japanese Government and any document evidencing or calling for the same, other than those referred to in the last sentence of paragraph one above, shall have no value, under Japanese law, as real or personal property.

4. All presently unused and unreserved telecommunications facilities of the Japanese Government will immediately be made available to provide telecommunications service to those making proper application for immediate service under the terms of current laws and regulations of the Japanese Government. The same procedure will be followed for telecommunications facilities which become available in the future. Regulations of the Japanese Government covering installation priorities will be strictly adhered to.

5. All telecommunications facilities presently reserved by the Japanese Government to fulfill eventual demands for telecommunications service to the holders of telephone rights acquired prior to 15 February 1949, will be held available for such service to such right holders only until 1 April 1949. The Japanese Government will so notify all holders of such rights within thirty days after official receipt of this memorandum.

6. The recipient of telecommunications service or facilities furnished by the Japanese Government is prohibited from reselling, subletting, renting, or otherwise transferring or disposing of such services or facilities. This does not prohibit the simultaneous transfer of telecommunications services and the telephone right thereto, acquired prior to 15 February 1949, provided said simultaneous transfer is made to the same party.

7. The terms of this memorandum do not prohibit the Japanese Government from entering into special contracts with parties, firms, or others for the purpose of providing special service deemed to be in the public interest. This special service may include special arrangements such as those normally made for semipublic telephone service or for the provision of private branch exchange and toll service for hotels. The Ministry of Communications will furnish to the General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, a list of all such special services or facilities where resale of telecommunications service is authorized by the Japanese Government, and, by appropriate revision thereto, will keep up to date this list for the General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

8. Arrangements will be made by the Japanese Government to establish, through existing organizations, necessary administrative procedures required to insure compliance with this memorandum.

9. Direct correspondence is authorized between interested staff sections of General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, and agencies of the Japanese Government, concerning technical matters within the scope of this memorandum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMORANDUM

SUBJECT : Information of General Application Pertaining to Directive Number SCAPIN 1964, file AG 676.3 (30 Aug 48) CCS, this Headquarters, dated 17 January 1949, subject: Instructions Pertaining to Treatment of Telephone Rights, etc

1. With reference to subject memorandum, the following is published for the information of all concerned.

2. This memorandum is being issued to eliminate, in the future, the "telephone right" which has arisen out of the trading in telephone registration certificates issued in connection with the provisions of subscriber telephone service obtained from the Japanese system. This "right" has no basis in law, is highly restrictive to proper development, and precludes the establishment of a democratic equitable and public spirited communication system.

3. Command channel instructions regarding the subject memorandum will be forwarded to Headquarters, Eighth Army by General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

GEORGE R. CONNOR.

Colonel, A. G. D.,

Asst. Adjutant General.

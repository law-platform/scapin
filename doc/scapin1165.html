GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 676.3 (29 Aug 46) CCS

(SCAPIN-1165)

APO 500

29 August 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
SUBJECT :Radio Wave Monitoring Stations.

1. The Ministry of Communications' plan for supplementing the four radio wave monitoring stations now operating at Tokyo (Iwatsuki), Sapporo (Kotoni), Osaka (Kire) and Fukuoka (Koga) with six additional stations is approved. Inclosure 1 is a list of the approved stations and includes a list of the equipment authorized for installation at the respective stations. Upon completion of the Hatsuse location, use of the Tokyo (Iwatsuki) station will be discontinued. No additional equipment is to be installed without prior approval of Civil Communications Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers. The transmitters authorized are to be used only for inter-communication between stations comprising the radio wave monitoring network. Their use will be discontinued as soon as satisfactory wire telephone or telegraph service can be established. Thereafter the transmitters will be used for emergency service only.

2. Construction and installation work will be scheduled so as to assure completion of the project by 1 July 1947. A monthly progress report of construction and installation will be submitted on or before the tenth day of each month to the Civil Communications Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

3. The scheduling of the installation of the equipment will be so arranged as to make the services available in the following order: Receiver monitoring, frequency monitoring, inter-station communication (transmitter), field intensity measuring, and direction finding.

4. Monitoring stations will make and retain complete and accurate records of all monitoring operations. Civil Communications Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, will indicate to the Ministry of Communications the reports and records of operation that will be required by General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

5. The Japanese Government will maintain a continuous twenty-four hour monitoring service to insure that no unauthorized stations are allowed to operate and that all stations are operated in accordance with (SCAPIN 1166), subject: "Control of Radio Communications", dated 29 August 1946, and other directives of the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers as well as current Japanese laws and regulations.

6. Direct communication between the Civil Communications Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers and the Ministry of Communications is authorized concerning all matters within the scope of this memorandum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS

SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

APO 500

29 August 1946

MEMORANDUM:

SUBJECT: Information of General Application Pertaining to Directive Number (SCAPIN- 1165), file AG 676.3 (29 Aug 46)CCS, this Headquarters, dated 29 Aug 1946, subject: "Radio Wave Monitoring Stations".

1. With reference to subject memorandum, the following is published for the information of all concerned.

2. Numerous Japanese radio stations have been causing interference to both military and civil communication circuits and facilities. This resulted primarily from the lack of sufficient and properly equipped monitoring stations.

3. The purpose of this directive is to have the Japanese Government construct facilities for monitoring the radio spectrum so they can adequately police their own stations, thereby correcting and preventing interference with military and civil communication circuits. In addition, these facilities can be utilized to furnish data to the Civil Communications Section.

4. Japanese monitoring stations will be required to maintain a complete and accurate record of all their operations. These records will be available for analysis of the results accomplished by the monitoring program. Statistical reports of the number of interference reports, number of illegal stations discovered and similar matters will prove valuable in assessing the effectiveness of the program.

5. Missions assigned to these monitoring stations and the scope of authorized operation will be kept under strict surveillance arranged for by Civil Communications Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers. Inspections by occupation troops to insure that only authorized radio monitoring stations are constructed or operated is required. Pertinent instructions will follow through command channels.

R. G. HERSEY

Lt Col., AGD.

Asst Adg Gen.

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 439 (30 Jan 48) ESS/PC

(SCAPIN-1854)

APO 500

30 January 1948

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
SUBJECT :Distribution of American Tobacco as Incentive Goods.

1. The Japanese Government is authorized to:

a. Distribute cigarettes from American tobacco products in the custody of the Japanese Government as incentive goods to encourage coal production and the delivery of rice to the official collection system.

b. Offer 250,000 packages (2 Oz.) of pipe tobacco as incentive goods to coal miners.

c. Utilize not be exceed 1,000 pounds of pipe tobacco and cigars to conduct operating tests as to the feasibility of utilizing these products in the manufacture of cigarettes for distribution as incentive goods.

d. Release all chewing tobacco for use as raw material in the manufacture of insecticides for farmers.

2. Each package of cigarattes and pipe tobacco will be clearly and distinctively marked to indicate that these products are authorized for legal release and to distinguish them from American tobacco used by Occupation Forces.

3. The Japanese Government will take all necessary action to assure that this tobacco and any products manufactured therefrom, are distributed only through official channels and do not appear in public, unauthorized sales.

4. Detailed distribution plans and prices to be charged for these commodities will be submitted to General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers for approval prior to actual distribution.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1854)

WFM/HFA/JRB/HGK/mgm

19 January 1948

1. The accompanying proposed SCAPIN authorizes the Japanese Government to distribute surplus American tobacco as incentive goods to rice farmers and coal miners.

2. Under date of 7 May 1947 EDGSP/D5 400. 312 the War Department notified SCAP that surplus American tobacco products would be made available for distribution in Japan, Korea and the Ryukyus as incentive goods in the following quantities;

Cigarettes pckgs 14,652,000

Cigars pcs 5,000,000

Pipe Tobacco pckgs (2 oz.) 2,500,000

Chewing Tobacco pckgs 6,950,000

The attachment to the latter of 7 May 1947 states in Note 3, "These items are from excess stocks in ZI with no charge for procurement against GARIOA funds or proceeds from exports." The record is not clear as to whether the Japanese Government will eventually be charged for these materials, but apparently any such charges will be a matter for determination in the peace negotiations along with similar charges for other surplus items made available to Japan. Shipping expenses are to be paid out of GARIOA funds. The amount of such charges cannot be accurately estimated at this time but on the basis of preliminary data on the delivery costs for 691 long tons of surplus tobacco shipped from Hawaii, the total transportation charges should be in the neighborhood of $120,000.

3. To date 20 shipments totalling about 1600 long tons have been received and stored in Yokohama and Kobe warehouses under the custody of the Japanese Government. As far as can be determined from present information (with only part of the shipment inventoried) this is 100% of the tobacco products made available in the Memo 7 May 1947. According to WCL 42510 none of these products is for use in Korea. Transshipment of share of tobacco to Ryukyus was proposed by ESS in CX 57263 11 Dec 47 and not objected to by WARX 92894 24 Dec 47.

According to the same radio further shipments of the following amounts of tobacco products for incentive purposes are expected during January from Hawaii:

Cigarettes pckgs 19,081,000

Cigars pcs 9,335,806

Pipe Tobacco pckgs (2 oz.) 4,347,960

Chewing Tobacco pckgs (2 oz.) 4,159,013

All these further shipments are for Japan proper and will be integrated into the incentive plans on a similar basis as the first group of products.

4. Of the 14.7 million packages of cigarettes, the Japanese Government proposes to distribute 4 million packages to farmers as incentive goods for the delivery of the 1947 rice crop and 7 million packages to coal miners over a 6 month period to encourage production of coal. In addition these programs include daily necessities, such as soap, rubber shoes, and utensils: textiles, such as work clothes, bedding, and towels; and food items. The remaining 3.7 million packages are being transshipped .to the Ryukyus according to CS 57566 24 Dec 47.

5. It is intended that the cigarettes to be distributed in Japan be priced at 15 yen per package of 20 (the same price charged for the highest priced Japanese cigarettes distributed as rationed goods) to cover handling and distribution expenses. Distribution of the cigarettes will begin in January 1948 according to proposals submitted by ESB. The Japanese Government is required to mark each package of cigarettes or pipe tobacco to distinguish this release from American tobacco from other sources, and to take all feasible measures to prevent diversion of these incentive goods to the blackmarket.

6. Japanese officials declare that miners and farmers do not like cigars, pipe and chewing tobacco and that these items are not suited for incentive purposes. However 250,000 packages (2 oz.) of pipe tobacco will be offered to coal miners as incentive goods at ¥15 per 2 oz. package in order to establish definitely whether or not there is an appeal. Tests will be made to determine whether cigars and pipe tobacco can be processed into cigarettes. If this proves feasible, the cigarettes so manufactured will be used for incentive purposes. The use of chewing tobacco as such is practically unknown in Japan and it is unfit for processing into cigarettes. Accordingly, it is proposed to use the chewing tobacco as raw material in the manufacture of insecticides for farmers.

7. Natural Resources Section and Industry Division of ESS have been joint sponsors, with PC&R Division, of the incentive goods programs.

8. Details of the distribution of the tobacco products will be reviewed by ESS (PC&R Div.).

GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 463.3 (10 Mar 49) ESS/EX

(SCAPIN-1984)

APO 500

10 March 1949

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
SUBJECT :Stabilization of the Coal Industry.

1. Reference is letter from the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers to the Prime Minister, 19 December 1948.

2. The coal industry is the foundation of the entire Japanese economic rehabilitation and stabilization effort. In order to establish Japan’s self-support and fully to implement reference letter, it is essential that the coal industry be rapidly and effectively improved in efficiency to provide a minimum of 42 million tons of coal for the JFY 1949 - 50 without increase in the present consumer coal price or subsidies and without resorting to deficit financing.

3. The Japanese Government will therefore take the necessary steps to insure that:

a. All contractual cash wage obligations for time actually worked be paid when due and in full and that such obligations constitute a first claim after taxes on coal company revenues.

b. Fictitious overtime payments will be eliminated immediately. Overtime work and payment therefor will be limited to that actually justified by production requirements. To this end, no overtime work or payment therefor will be permitted to surface or office workers except where emergency conditions require. No overtime payments will be made to underground and supervisory personnel except for work in excess of eight hours in one day or forty-eight hours in a week. Except where special circumstances justify, overtime work will not be scheduled for any day unless the total work schedule of the employee for the week is in excess of forty-eight hours.

c. No further hirings of surface workers, clerical or administrative personnel, except where necessary to open new mines, will be permitted until the overall ratio of underground to surface workers for the entire industry has reached a 60 - 40 ratio. Transfers from underground to surface employment in case of disability or similar reason shall be made only as replacements.

d. Priority, after wages, will be accorded payment for expendable operating supplies and equipment. Mine equipment inventories and purchase programs will be reviewed to insure the elimination of purchases of excessive items.

e. The coal mine companies will rapidly adopt a uniform and accurate accounting system in mines, regional and head offices which shall clearly detail all costs and revenues. In the case of designated mines, this system shall be instituted prior to or on 1 April 1949.

4. If necessary to insure implementation of the foregoing measures, the Japanese Government will direct the Coal Distribution Kodan to pay revenue from coal sales into special blocked bank accounts for payment of taxes, legitimate wages, supplies of operating mine equipment and other supplies. Payment from such blocked accounts will only be made pursuant to regulations approved by the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers.

5. Monthly reports on action taken to implement this Memoradum will be submitted beginning 1 April 1949. Direct communication between the Economic and Scientific Section and concerned agencies of the Japanese Government is authorized for the implementation of this Memorandum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1984)

WFM/SMF/lf

8 March 1949

1. Reference is letter from the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers to the Prime Minister, 19 December 1948.

2. Recent developments in the operation of the coal industry threaten the prospects of maintaining current coal output levels as well as realizing the 1949-1950 fiscal year coal goal of 42 million tons. In recent months most of the operating coal companies have failed to:

a. Achieve their monthly output schedules;

b. Meet contractual payroll obligations;

c. Pay outstanding bills to suppliers for material and supply deliveries essential to continuation of mining activities;

d. Fulfill their tax obligations. The operators allege that their depleted cash position due to current operating deficits makes the foregoing financial delinquencies unavoidable.

3. There is ample evidence warranting wholesale criticism of the manner in which the coal industry is presently operating. Management has not exercised its responsibility to mine coal efficiently and maximize output. Costs have been considerably inflated through gross inefficiencies and mismanagement while many cost elements presented by the operators are manifestly fictitious. The present actions of the coal operators suggest that their objective is:

a. To force an upward adjustment of coal prices;

b. To compel the adoption of direct control of wages.

4. The absence of a satisfactory standard cost accounting system on the part of the coal operators makes difficult a detailed assessment of the actual facts prevailing in the industry. However, on the basis of an overall analysis of the operating position of the entire industry, it has been concluded that the present price structure and revenue return are adequate and that to accord the demands of the operators for an upward price adjustment would be unwarranted and prompt a rise of the price level with consequent serious disruption of the present overall stabilization effort.

5. Repeated attempts have been made to persuade coal operators and the responsible Ministries involved to correct the widespread malpractices prevailing in the industry. Unfortunately, no adequate response has been forthcoming and the coal operators appear to be perfectly prepared to sacrifice the production goals and accept the consequent irreparable damage to the present industrial recovery effort to achieve their ends.

6. The present directive has been prepared to produce a correction of the more important malpractices prevailing in the coal industry. It should insure against a walk-out by coal miners because of non-payment of wages. It should also eliminate all fictitious and inflated overtime wage payments. It should eliminate surplus surface workers, clerical and administrative personnel and bring about a more economic ratio of surface workers to underground workers. Action recommended should maintain continuity of supplies and equipment through insuring payment. The institution of a uniform cost accounting system should eliminate current misrepresentation and reveal the actual circumstances of the operators.

7. Proposed SCAPIN also provides necessary legal authorization in the event that operators do not meet contractual payments, for effecting payments essential to continue full operation of the mines by means of diverting coal sale revenues into special blocked bank accounts from which such payments will be made pursuant to approved SCAP policy.

8. No concurrence required.

Approved by c/s 4 Mar.

S. M. F.

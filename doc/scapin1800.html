AG 420 (13 Oct 47) ESS/PC

(SCAPIN-1800)

APO 500

13 October 1947

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
SUBJECT :Distribution of U. S. Used Combat Boots and Service Shoes.

1. Reference is CLO letter Number 6749 (EG) dated 27 August 1947, Subject: Application for Permission to Distribute Boots and Shoes Released by the U. S. Army.

2. No objection is offered to the plan of the Japanese Government to distribute boots and shoes released by the U. S. Army provided that:

a. Each boot and shoe has the approved mark “SAL” branded on the leather near the top to identify it as a salvaged item.

b. Sale prices are those fixed by the Price Board in relation to present boot and shoe prices.

c. No import duty or commodity tax is levied upon these boots and shoes.

d. Distribution is limited to eligible farmers who meet full staple food delivery quotas and who have not previously received shoes through the U. S. Import Distributon Plan.

e. This special distribution does not affect normal ration allowances for rubber boots and jikatabi.

3. In conformity with SCAPIN 1337-A, the Japanese Government will require prefectural authorities to keep accurate records of all boots and shoes received and distributed by them under this plan and will submit accounting reports on this distribution to the Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers as soon as practicable.

4. Direct communication between the responsible agencies of the Japanese Government and the Economic and Scientific Section, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, regarding the operation of this plan is authorized.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1800)

HFA/JRB/LEP/dml

30 September 1947

1. The accompanying memorandum for the Japanese Government releases for distribution 53 104 pairs of used combat boots and service shoes which were salvaged in Japan from U.S. Army soldiers returning to the United States.

2. Physical inspection by Miss Zahn and Mr. McLeaod of PC&R revealed the shoes to be classified into three categories:

a. Those which can be distributed as they are (36,518 pairs).

b. Those which can be distributed after repairing (12,000 pairs).

c. Those which are unrepairable and unserviceable (4,586 pairs).

The boots and shoes requiring repair (12,000 pairs) are in the hands of manufacturers to be returned to the Board of Trade. Brand marking on the outside of each shoe is required to identify it as authorized for Japanese use. The accepted and approved mark is "SAL" (for salvaged U.S. shoes).

3. The price has been determined by the Japanese Price Board and The Board of Trade in accordance with present official shoe prices.

4. A report on the distribution plan has been submitted to SCAP and has been accepted.

5. The interests of USAFIK have been considered. This action does not affect the Korean area.

6. This is considered to be a policy matter. Surveillance is necessary.

7. Concurrences: ESS/FT (Mr. Gould); G-4 Policy (Lt Col Ebel); NRS/Ag (Mr. Hardie); PH&W (Maj. Lecker)

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
SUBJECT :Manufacture of Industrial Explosives.

1. Reference is made to memorandum for the Imperial Japanese Government, AG 471.86 (10 Feb 46) ESS/lN, subject: "Manufacture of Industrial Explosives," and letter C.L.O. No. 2816 (ECI) dated 11 June 1946, subject: "Manufacture and Use of Explosives and Accessories for Civilian Use for the Period from 1 July to 31 December 1946."

2. Manufacture of the following types and quantities of explosives, to be used for industrial purposes only, is authorized for the period 1 July 1946 to 31 December 1946:

Type of Explosives and Accessories | Quantity Authorized

Gelatine Dynamite and Hagi Dynamite | 3,618 Metric Tons

Ammonia Dynamite and Ammonium Nitrate Explosives | 2,647 Metric Tons

Carlit | 620 Metric Tons

Black Powder for blasting and fuse | 274 Metric Tons

Fuse | 30,665 Kilometers

Blasting caps for use with fuse and electric detonator | 42,250,000 Pieces

Electric detonator | 14,800,000 Pieces

3. Authorization is granted to manufacture the following explosives and accessories to be used for whaling and hunting purposes:

Smokeless powder | 22 Metric Tons

Percussion primers for whaling | 7,800 Pieces

Friction tubes for whaling | 6,000 Pieces

Sporting cap | 25,000{000 Pieces

4. Authorization is granted to reprocess into industrial explosives the following military explosives which were turned over to the Japanese Home Ministry:

Military high explosives | 304 Metric Tons

5. Authorization is granted to manufacture the following pyrotechnics to be used only as railroad and shipboard signal and warning devices:

Life name | 1,000 Pieces

Rocket (fire arrow) | 500 Pieces

Shell | 1,000 Pieces

Blue flame signal | 1,500 Pieces

Red flame signal | 8,000 Pieces

6. The Imperial Japanese Government will submit to the Commanding General, Eighth United States Army, applications for authority to operate plants necessary for the manufacture of the explosives authorized by this memorandum.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

JOHN B. COOLEY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutent General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1038)

JZR/EWS/aeb

14 June 1946

1. The Imperial Japanese Government requested authority to manufacture industrial explosives in CLO 360, dated 26 October 1945 subject, Application for Manufacture and Use of Industrial Explosives. This request was studied in detail and two SCAP memoranda concerning explosives were issued. (Memo to the IJG, AG 471.86 (12 November 1945) ESS/IN subject, manufacture of Explosives for Use in Coal Production, and Memo to the IJG AG 471.86 (10 February 1946) ESS/IN subject, Manufacture of Industrial Explosives. The authorization granted by the second memorandum will expire on 30 June 1946. Letter from IJG, CLO No.2816 (ECI) dated 11 June 1946 subject, Manufacture and Use of Explosives and Accessories for Civilian Use for the Period from 1 July to 31 December 1946, requests authorization to manufacture certain quantities of explosives and accessories for civilian use during the last half of 1946.

2. The manufacture of explosives shown by the present memorandum is authorized because of the following consideration:

a. The use for which these explosives are intended will fill important needs of the Japanese economy. These uses include coal mining, metal mining, quarrying, reconstruction due to war damage, construction of roads, bridges and tunnels, and agricultural uses such as irrigation and land reclamation. Small quantities of smokeless powder and accessories are also authorized for the manufacture of ammunition for whaling and hunting purposes. Manufacture of flares and phrotechnics are necessary for the safe operations of ships and railroads.

b. Quantities authorized are believed to be minimum needs. These quantities are about two-thirds of the quantity authorized for the period of January to June 1946.

c. Any desired checks on the manufactures can easily be carried out. The Japanese maintain a detailed system of controls and licensing at all levels and a detailed plan for manufacture and use of the explosives has been prepared. The plan includes a listing of plants to be used and the amount of production scheduled for each. The large number of plants to be used for the manufacture of these explosives is necessary because of the limited operable capacity available at each plant at present, and because difficulties of distribution of products would be increased by reducing the number of authorized plants.

3. The detailed plan for manufacturing the subject explosives is based on a Japanese proposal, but includes several changes made by this division after careful study. The plan is being forwarded by separate correspondence to the Commanding General, Eighth Army for his use in considering the applications to be submitted by the Japanese Government in compliance with paragraph 6 of the attached memorandum.

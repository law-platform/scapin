OFFICE OF THE SUPREME COMMANDER
FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 452.01 (12 Sep 45) GD

(SCAPIN - 22)

APO 500

12 September 1945

SUBJECT :Japanese Aircraft.
TO :Japanese Imperial Government.

1. It is desired to rescue number of Japanese aircraft for test and study by the clinicians of with the United States Armed Forces the requirements of this program are as follows

a. Four (4) of each aircraft and all accessories therefore employed or developed for both the Japanese Army and Navy. All aircraft employed should be in the best possible operation condition. Such aircraft as may have been in the development stage should be in that condition in which they existed as of 15 August 1945.

b. Sufficient spare parts for each aircraft the necessary to conduct exhaustive flight tests. The spare parts sets should include these items which are ordinary replaceable as a result of normal operation.

c. Aircraft, accessories and spare parts selected will be in the best possible condition and at locations most accessible from Tokyo area.

d. Component flying and maintenance personnel, familiar with each aircraft, will be furnished for the purpose of rendering such aircraft operational, where necessary, and for detailed interrogation as to flight characteristics and maintenance procedures. Adequate supplies of fuel, lubricants, etc. of the various types used in each aircraft will be made available.

e. The Japanese Imperial Government will cause the aircraft, accessories, supplies and spare parts to be furnished on the priority basis, first class priority being the latest types of each model in operational use as of 15 August 1945, second class priority being aircraft not in operational use but in development or testing stages, and third class priority being earlier types of each model whether in operational use, discarded, or outmodel as of 15 August 1945.

f. The Japanese Imperial Government will notified this Headquarters as each of the above aircraft is made ready and the locations at which each is available.

2. Detailed instructions as to flights, tests, and further maintenance will be given by United States Army Air Force personnel as required. In fulfilling the above requirements, aircraft will not be fueled by Japanese authorities nor will propellers be installed. Fuel and propeller this, however, would be immediately available.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

/s/ Harold Fair.
/t/ HAROLD FAIR,
Lt. Colonel, A.G.D.,
Asst. Adjutant General.

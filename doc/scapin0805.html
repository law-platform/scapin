GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 000.93 (8 Mar 46) CCS

(SCAPIN-805)

APO 500

8 March 1946

MEMORANDUM FOR :IMPERIAL JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
THROUGH :Central Liaison Office, Tokyo.
SUBJECT :Establishment of Hourly Weather Circuits.

It is directed that the Japanese Bureau of Communications furnish to the Occupation Forces, telegraph circuits and services as follows:

a. Two telegraph circuits described in subparagraphs (1) and (2) below, suitable for carrying American teletypewriter signals and arranged so that when any one subscriber transmits all other subscribers on the circuit will receive the transmitted signals.

(1) One circuit to be available for service on or before 15 March 1946 which will connect the Army signal installation at Atago-Yama, Tokyo; the General Central Meteorological Observatory (GCMO). Tokyo; the GCMO district office, Sendai: the Army airfield Chitose; and the GCMO district office, Sapporo.

(2) One circuit to be available for service on or before 1 April 1946, which will, connect the Army signal installation at Atago-Yama, Tokyo; the GCMO district office at Nagoya; the Army airfields and GCMO district offices at Osaka and Fukuoka, respectively.

b. Dependable and fast communications over existing telephone, telegraph or radio circuits between GCMO district offices at Fukuoka and Kagoshima.

2. The Occupation Forces will furnish and install teletypewriter machines on the circuits specified in paragraph la above, in the GCMO offices at Sapporo, Sendai, Tokyo, Nagoya, Osaka, and Fukuoka.

3. Japanese communication personnel will be responsible for the maintenance of the telegraph circuits and telegraph repeaters associated with the facilities being furnished. The Occupation Forces will maintain American teletypewriter machines involved, except that Japanese operators may be given instructions so that they can perform day to day maintenance of these equipments.

4. A carrier telegraph channel having an effective frequency width of 170 cycles or a battery operated direct current circuit which will pass approximately 0 to 70 cycles, will satisfactorily carry the teletypewriter signals to be used on circuits described in paragraph la. The teletypewriters used operate at the speed, of 368 seven impulse characters per minute and direct current telegraph repeaters used on these circuits must be capable or operating at this impulse speed.

5. Circuits to be provided as directed above, are to be used for collection and distribution of hourly weather information and will be in addition to weather service now being furnished the Occupation Forces by the GCMO. Personnel at all GCMO offices involved, will be instructed by representatives of the Occupation Forces as to information which will be entered on the circuit, the time and sequence in which it is to be filed and in other details pertaining to traffic control.

6. The GCMO district office, at Kagoshima will transmit required information at times to be determined to the GCMO district office. Fukuoka, using existing communication facilities as provided in paragraph 1th above and the Fukuoka office will enter this information on the teletypewriter circuit.

7. Engineering details in connection with the establishment of the circuits and services specified will be coordinated between Civil Communications Section, this Headquarters and the Board of Communications.

8. Details in connection with the maintenance and actual establishment of the circuits will be coordinated between the Army Signal Operations Division, Tokyo. Central Telephone Office, and the Tokyo Bureau of Communications.

9. Traffic and weather reporting instructions and traffic control information will be the responsibility of the 20th Weather Squadron, Tokyo, and will be coordinated with the GCMO, Tokyo.

10. Direct correspondence between agencies listed in paragraphs 7, 8 and 9, within the scope of this memorandum, is authorized.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

B. M. FITCH,
Brigadier General, AGD,
Adjutant General.

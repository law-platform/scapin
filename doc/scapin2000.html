GENERAL HEADQUARTERS
SUPREME COMMANDER FOR THE ALLIED POWERS

AG 311.23 (4 May 49) CCS

(SCAPIN - 2000)

APO 500

4 May 1949

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
SUBJECT :Japanese Police Radio Stations.

1. Reference is made to:

a. Memorandum for the Japanese Government, file AG 676.3 (14 Jul 47)CCS, SCAPIN 1744, dated 14 July 1947, subject: Control of Radio Communications, as amended.

b. Memorandum for the Ministry of Communications, dated 12 August 1947, subject: Emergency Disaster Plan.

c. Memorandum for the Ministry of Communications, file CCS/DR 151, dated 30 August 1948, subject: Emergency Police Radio Stations.

d. Memorandum for the Ministry of Communications, file CCS/DR 152, dated 30 August subject: Test Schedule of Emergency Police Radio Stations.

e. Memorandum for the Ministry of Communications, file CCS/DR 178, dated 20 October 1948, subject: Regulations for Inactive Police Prefectural Net Radio Stations.

f. Memorandum for the Ministry of Communications, file CCS/DR 211, dated 17 January 1949, subject: Operation of Inactive Police Stations.

2. The present technical operation of Japanese police radio stations violates provisions of the International Telecommunications Convention (Atlantic City, 1947) adhered to by the Japanese Government in January 1949. In addition, operation of police radio stations is not considered reliable to adequately protect life and property in times of emergency, due to the limited technical ability of police communication employees.

3. The Japanese Government budget for the fiscal year 1949 will not permit the addition of sufficient competent technical personnel to insure the proper installation, operation and maintenance of police radio stations now authorized.

4. The Japanese Government will initiate immediately the following program to reduce the number of radio stations operated by the Japanese police to those that conform to international regulations and can be relied upon to provide service under emergency conditions.

a. Initiate a vigorous recruitment program to add, within budget limitations, sufficient radio engineering personnel to adequately plan, install, maintain and inspect all radio stations operated by the National Rural Police.

b. Equip and staff all radio stations at Regional Headquarters in accordance with Inclosure 1 (Minimum Requirements for Regional Headquarters Police Radio Stations) on or before 1 July 1949.

c. Equip and staff all radio stations at Prefectural Headquarters in accordance with Inclosure 2 (Minimum Requirements for Prefecture Headquarters Police Radio Stations) on or before 1 July 1949.

d. Equip and staff a minimum of two (2) portable emergency radio stations for each National Rural Police Troop in accordance with Inclosure 3 (Minimum Requirements for Emergency Police Radio Stations) on or before 1 September 1949. These stations will operate in accordance with references cited in subparagraphs 1b, c, d, e, and f.

e. Withdraw immediately outstanding authorizations for operation of inactive police radio stations listed in Inclosure 4, and prepare new authorizations permitting operation until 1 September 1949 in accordance with the emergency plan reference 1b, or on specific temporary authority in accordance with reference 1a.

f. Discontinue, except for urgent police business as defined in reference 1e, all unilateral transmissions from police radio stations except those from National Headquarters to Regional and Troop Headquarters using the following facilities:

(1) 1709P 3270 JPA

(2) 1709P 7560 JPA-2

(3) 1709P 14480 JPA-3

g. In the event that urgent unilateral transmissions are necessary over other police radio facilities, Japanese and English texts of messages transmitted, together with a full explanation of the emergency, will be submitted to Civil Communications Section, and Public Safety Division, G-2, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, within ten (10) days of the dispatch of such messages.

h. Inspect before 1 July 1949 all radio stations listed in Inclosure 5 to determine which, if any, of these stations are capable of meeting international regulations. Any such stations, provided sufficient competent operators are available, may be temporarily authorized to work under the same conditions as 4e above under the emergency rules outlined in reference 1b until 1 September 1949.

i . Withdraw completely all authorizations for construction and operation of radio stations listed in Inclosure 6.

j. Study the requirements of each individual National Rural Police Troop and, considering budget limitations, personnel available, topography, transportation available and conditions prevailing, prepare and submit to Civil Communications Section, and Public Safety Division, G-2, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, before 1 August 1949, a realistic plan for a reliable radio not, meeting international regulations and requirements outlined in Inclosure 3 for each National Rural Police Troop.

5. Until the program outlined in paragraph 4 above has been completed, no additional construction or operation of police radio stations will be undertaken, except present (VHF) authorizations.

6. All inactive police radio stations after 1 September 1949 will be equipped and staffed in accordance with requirements shown in Inclosure 3 and the number will be limited to those required by the plan outlined in paragraph 4j above.

7. Necessary changes to the lists required by reference 1a will be made in accordance with paragraphs 4e, h and i above.

8. Direct communication among Civil Communications Section, and Public Safety Division, G-2, General Headquarters, Supreme Commander for the Allied Powers, the Ministry of Communications, and the National Rural Police on matters within the scope of this memorandum is authorized.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMORANDUM FOR :JAPANESE GOVERNMENT.
SUBJECT :Price and Distribution Controls over Vegetables.

1. Reference is memorandum for the Japanese Government, file AG 430 (15 Jun 48) ESS/PD, SCAPIN 1910 dated 15 June 1948, subject: Price and Distribution Controls over Fish and Vegetables.

2. That portion of the memorandum referred to in paragraph 1 which pertains to price and distribution controls over vegetables is rescinded as of 31 March 1949. Vegetables, while still essential commodities, are no longer considered to be in short supply. The Japanese Government may, therefore, as of 1 April 1949, take any steps considered advisable toward the relaxation or elimination of distribution and price controls over vegetables.

3. This memorandum will not be construed as a rescission of those portions of the memorandum referred to in paragraph 1 which pertain to the maintenance of price and distribution controls over marine products. Fish production, now and in the foreseeable future, is not large enough to justify any such action. It is expected that a cessation of efforts to control vegetables will be accompanied by an intensification of distribution and price controls over marine products.

FOR THE SUPREME COMMANDER:

R.M. LEVY,
Colonel, AGD,
Adjutant General.

MEMO FOR RECORD: (SCAPIN - 1910/1)

FLW/RFS/NAS/eb

22 March 1949

1. Since March 1946, the Japanese Government has been held responsible for the maintenance of firm price and distribution controls over fresh vegetables. The Government was charged with this responsibility by Directive No. 3, SCAPIN 47, 22 September 1945, which required economic controls on all "essential commodities in short supply" and by SCAPIN 1910, 15 Jun 1948, which specifically defined both vegetables and fish as essential commodities in short supply.

2. At this time price and distribution controls over vegetables are no longer considered necessary for the following reason:

a. The production of vegetables has improved to the extent that equitable distribution is feasible without a rationing system.

b. The prevailing effective prices of vegetables have held relatively constant during the past year and will not be appreciably affected by the removal of official ceiling prices, once the summer vegetables are brought into the markets.

c. Governmental funds presently being used to maintain vegetable controls could be more effectively expended in controlling more essential commodities in shorter supply.

3. The attached SCAPIN is therefore issued relieving the Government of the responsibility for maintaining vegetable price and rationing controls. The attached letter informs the Commanding General, Eighth Army, of this action which removes from the Military Government organization the necessity for maintaining surveillance over vegetable distribution and prices.

4. Paragraph 3 of the SCAPIN and paragraph 4 of the letter indicate the fact that marine products are still considered to be in short supply and that their price and distribution controls will therefore be continued. In order to assure to all a fair share at a fair price, the Government is instructed to intensify economic controls over fish and Military Government is instructed to continue surveillance.

5. Concurrence: NRS, Lt. Col. Schenk

Nelson A. Stitt, 57-8393

Dodge Mission, P. M. O'Leary
